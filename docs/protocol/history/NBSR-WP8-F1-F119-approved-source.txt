A. Global Operator Identifier
1. A duhet Operator ID me qenë i lëshuar nga një autoritet global, apo self-certifying nga public key?


F1 — Hybrid Operator Identity Profiles: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• NBSR mbështet private/self-managed trust për përdorim internal, enterprise, home dhe private consortium.
• NBSR mbështet public/global trust për ISP dhe Internet federation.
• Operator ID krijohet kriptografikisht; registry global e regjistron dhe e verifikon për federatën publike, por nuk e cakton arbitrarisht.
• Të dy profilet përdorin të njëjtat semantika bazë të protokollit; ndryshojnë trust roots, discovery dhe kërkesat e verifikimit.


Arsyeja:
Private NBSR duhet të funksionojë pa varësi nga një autoritet global, ndërsa federata publike kërkon verifikim organizativ dhe transparencë shtesë.


2. A duhet formati me qenë tekstual, binar, apo të dyja?


F2 — Dual Operator ID Representation: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Federation Extension objects përdorin një formë binare canonical të Operator ID për hashing, signatures dhe comparisons. Frozen Core objects ruajnë fushat ekzistuese tekstuale dhe nuk ndryshohen.
• Operator ID ka edhe një formë tekstuale canonical për konfigurim, logs, dokumentim, API dhe përdorim njerëzor.
• Të dy formatet përfaqësojnë saktësisht të njëjtën vlerë. Federation v0.1 profile MUST freeze binary length, immutable genesis-identity encoding, domain-separation label, textual prefix and alphabet, case and checksum rules, and one deterministic lossless mapping compatible with frozen Core textual IDs.


Arsyeja:
Forma binare është efikase dhe e paqartë për protokollin; forma tekstuale është më e sigurt për operim dhe troubleshooting.


3. A duhet Operator ID me mbet i njëjtë kur operatori ndërron signing keys?


F3 — Stable Operator Identity: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Operator ID mbetet stabil gjatë gjithë jetës së identitetit të operatorit.
• Operational signing keys mund dhe duhet të rotatohen pa ndryshuar Operator ID.
• Operator ID nuk lidhet me një operational key të vetëm.
• Operator ID derivon nga një stable offline identity root public key ose nga një strukturë ekuivalente e qëndrueshme e identitetit.


Arsyeja:
Key rotation është operacion normal sigurie dhe nuk duhet të ndryshojë identitetin e operatorit ose të prishë trust relationships.


4. Si parandalohet collision ose regjistrimi i dy operatorëve me të njëjtin identitet?


F4 — Algorithmic Uniqueness and Global Registration: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Operator ID gjenerohet automatikisht dhe deterministikisht nga një immutable genesis identity commitment duke përdorur një domain-separated cryptographic hash. Active identity roots mund të zëvendësohen vetëm përmes një valid RecoveryTransitionRecord dhe nuk e ndryshojnë genesis commitment-in.
• Dy root keys të ndryshme prodhojnë praktikisht Operator IDs të ndryshme.
• Private deployments mund ta përdorin Operator ID pa global registry.
• Public federation kërkon regjistrim, organization verification dhe transparency publication.
• Registry kontrollon duplicate registration, proof-of-possession dhe organizational binding, por nuk e ndryshon Operator ID kriptografik.


Shembull konceptual:
OperatorIdentityCommitment = SHA-256("nbsr-operator-identity-v1" || canonical_genesis_identity_public_key); OperatorID = canonical_operator_id(OperatorIdentityCommitment)


Arsyeja:
Gjenerimi algoritmik shmang ndarjen manuale të numrave dhe funksionon offline; registry global shton verifikim publik dhe parandalon impersonation ose pretendime të dyfishta organizative.
5. A mundet një kompani me pasë disa Operator ID për rajone ose subsidiaries?


F5 — Hierarchical Multiple Operator Identities: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Një organizatë mundet me pasë disa Operator ID për rajone, subsidiaries, environments ose administrative domains të ndara.
• Secili Operator ID është identitet i plotë, unik dhe kriptografikisht i pavarur.
• Lidhja parent–child duhet të jetë e nënshkruar, e verifikueshme dhe e publikueshme sipas profilit të trust-it.
• Child Operator ID nuk trashëgon automatikisht autoritetin e parent-it; çdo authority kërkon delegation eksplicite.
• Komprometimi ose revokimi i një child ID nuk e komprometon ose revokon automatikisht parent-in apo child identities tjera.


Arsyeja:
Ky model lejon izolim sipas rajonit, juridiksionit dhe përgjegjësisë operative, pa humbur lidhjen e verifikueshme me organizatën kryesore.


6. A duhet Operator ID me zbulu emrin real të operatorit, apo mundet me qenë pseudonymous?
F6 — Verified Real-World Identity for Public Federation: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Çdo Operator ID që merr pjesë në public/global federation duhet të jetë i lidhur me një organizatë reale dhe të verifikuar.
• Vetë Operator ID mbetet identifikues kriptografik dhe nuk përmban drejtpërdrejt emrin e kompanisë.
• Emri legal, emri tregtar, statusi, jurisdiksioni dhe informacioni i verifikimit ruhen si registry metadata të nënshkruara.
• Ndryshimi i emrit ose rebranding nuk e ndryshon Operator ID.
• Public/global federation nuk lejon operatorë plotësisht anonimë ose pseudonymous pa një legal entity të verifikuar.
• Private/internal NBSR mund të përdorë pseudonymous, local ose organization-defined identities pa global verification.


Arsyeja:
Federata publike kërkon accountability dhe mundësi për verifikim, incident response dhe governance. Ndarja e Operator ID kriptografik nga metadata reale e organizatës ruan stabilitetin e identitetit kur emri, brandi ose të dhënat organizative ndryshojnë.




7. Kush e verifikon lidhjen ndërmjet Operator ID dhe organizatës reale?


F7 — Accredited Registry Verification with Demo Federation Registry: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Në public/global NBSR, lidhja ndërmjet Operator ID dhe organizatës reale verifikohet nga një accredited registry authority.
• Verifikimi duhet të përdorë tri kategori prove: kontrollin e Internet resources kur ekziston (p.sh. RIR/ASN/IP evidence), legal-entity evidence dhe cryptographic proof-of-possession të operator root key.
• Registry authority lëshon një signed operator registry record që lidh Operator ID me organizatën, statusin dhe juridiksionin.
• NBSR specification përcakton kërkesat dhe formatin e provave; nuk kërkon që projekti ose implementuesi fillestar ta operojë vetë autoritetin global.
• Private/self-managed NBSR mund të përdorë local administrator ose private consortium si registry authority.
• Reference demo implementon një NBSR Development Federation Registry që simulon global registration me të njëjtat protocol semantics.
• Demo duhet të mbështesë të dy profilet: self-signed/private trust dhe registered public-style trust.


Demo flow:
Operator krijon root key → gjeneron Operator ID → dërgon registration request → development registry verifikon proof-of-possession dhe mock organization evidence → lëshon signed registry record → publikon statusin active.


Arsyeja:
Ky model lejon që protokolli dhe verification flow të ndërtohen e testohen sot, ndërsa autoritetet reale të akreditimit dhe governance përcaktohen gjatë standardizimit dhe adoptimit publik.


8. Çka ndodh me Operator ID nëse operatori blihet, bashkohet ose mbyllet?
F8 — Operator Identity Lifecycle and Non-Reuse: APPROVED
Vendimi:
• Operator ID është permanent dhe nuk ricaktohet kurrë te një organizatë tjetër.
• Në rast blerjeje, Operator ID mund të vazhdojë, por registry metadata përditësohet me owner-in dhe statusin e ri.
• Në rast bashkimi, identitetet ekzistuese kalojnë në transitional state dhe lidhen me entitetin pasues përmes signed continuity records.
• Në rast mbylljeje, Operator ID kalon në retired ose revoked dhe mbetet historikisht i rezervuar.
• Registry ruan historinë e ownership, merger, acquisition, retirement dhe revocation changes.
• Keys, trust, delegations dhe authority të vjetra nuk transferohen automatikisht te pronari ose entiteti i ri; kërkohet autorizim dhe verifikim i ri eksplicit.
• Një identitet retired ose revoked nuk mund të ringjallet përmes një root key, registry record ose organization claim të ri.


Arsyeja:
Non-reuse dhe signed lifecycle continuity parandalojnë identity hijacking, trust inheritance të paautorizuar, rollback dhe resurrection të një operatori të vjetër.
B. Operator keys dhe key purposes
A duhet secili operator me pasë root key offline dhe keys të ndara për operim?


F9 — Offline Root, Purpose-Bound Operational Keys, and IAM Control: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Çdo Operator ID ka një stable offline root identity key.
• Root key përdoret vetëm për autorizim ose revokim të keys, recovery dhe lifecycle actions me ndikim të lartë.
• Root key nuk përdoret për transport, service publication, checkpoints ose normal federation traffic.
• Puna ditore kryhet me operational keys të ndara dhe me purpose të kufizuar.
• Komprometimi i një operational key nuk duhet të komprometojë Operator ID-në ose keys e tjera.
• IAM kontrollon cilët njerëz dhe workloads kanë të drejtë të kërkojnë, përdorin, rotatojnë ose revokojnë keys.
• NBSR key hierarchy kontrollon autoritetin kriptografik të secilës key në protokoll; IAM nuk e zëvendëson këtë hierarchy.
• Production profile duhet të përdorë least privilege, separation of duties, audit dhe approval controls për veprimet mbi root ose operational keys.
• Private/lab profile mund ta simulojë offline storage dhe IAM controls, por semantika e autoritetit mbetet e njëjtë.


Hierarkia konceptuale:
Operator ID → Offline Root Key → Purpose-Bound Operational Keys
IAM → kontrollon qasjen njerëzore dhe workload access mbi këtë hierarchy


Arsyeja:
Ndarja e root authority nga keys operative kufizon blast radius dhe mundëson rotation e revocation pa humbur identitetin e operatorit. IAM shton kontroll operacional mbi njerëzit dhe sistemet, ndërsa hierarchy e keys siguron trust-in kriptografik të protokollit.


Cilat key purposes duhet me qenë të ndara: identity, delegation, service publication, checkpoints dhe transport?


F10 — Strict Separation of Key Purposes: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Identity/Registry Key përdoret vetëm për operator identity dhe registry-related records.
• Delegation Key përdoret vetëm për delegations dhe authority grants.
• Service Publication Key përdoret vetëm për publikim dhe përditësim të service records.
• Checkpoint/Transparency Key përdoret vetëm për checkpoints dhe transparency statements.
• Transport Key përdoret vetëm për autentikim të edges, gateways dhe transport sessions.
• Një key nuk lejohet të përdoret për purpose tjetër nga ai që i është autorizuar.
• Çdo nënshkrim duhet të përmbajë ose të lidhet me një key-purpose identifier që verifikuesi e kontrollon fail-closed.
• Komprometimi i një key duhet të kufizohet te purpose-i i saj dhe nuk duhet të japë autoritet mbi funksionet tjera.


Arsyeja:
Ndarja strikte e roleve zvogëlon blast radius, mundëson rotation dhe revocation të pavarur dhe parandalon që komprometimi i një key të rrëzojë krejt operatorin.


A lejohet një key me pasë më shumë se një purpose?


F11 — Single-Purpose Keys in All Profiles: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Production dhe demo përdorin one key per NBSR purpose.
• Demo duhet ta testojë këtë si pjesë normative të arkitekturës, jo si simplification opsionale.
• Cross-purpose use refuzohet fail-closed.
• Fallback lejohet vetëm kur një primitive kriptografike kërkon operacione të lidhura brenda të njëjtit purpose logjik, p.sh. sign/verify.
• Nuk lejohet bashkimi i autoriteteve semantikisht të ndryshme, p.sh. delegation dhe service publication, në të njëjtën key.
• Nëse zbatimi i one-key-per-purpose dështon për arsye reale teknike, implementimi duhet ta dokumentojë kufizimin dhe të ruajë të paktën one key per logical purpose.


Arsyeja:
Demo duhet ta provojë arkitekturën reale të sigurisë. Ndarja e key-ve sipas purpose ul blast radius, e bën auditimin më të qartë dhe lejon rotation ose revocation pa prekur role të tjera.


A duhet keys me qenë Ed25519, apo duhet algorithm agility prej fillimit?
F12 — Ed25519-Only Current Profile: APPROVED
Decision:
- Core v0.1, WP8 demo, tests, and current implementation use only Ed25519.
- No additional signature algorithm is implemented or negotiated now.
- Unknown or non-Ed25519 algorithms fail closed.
- The current code remains aligned with the frozen Core v0.1 COSE profile.
- Additional algorithms may be considered later only when development or interoperability creates a demonstrated need.
- Adding another algorithm requires an explicit versioned protocol decision, security review, updated registry/profile rules, downgrade protections, conformance vectors, and tests.
- No silent algorithm expansion or automatic fallback is permitted.


Rationale:
A single algorithm keeps the current security surface, implementation, demo, and cross-language verification small and deterministic. Future algorithm agility remains possible through a controlled versioned change rather than being built prematurely into the current code.


A duhet signing keys me pasë expiry të detyrueshëm?
F13 — Mandatory Rotation and Validity Windows for Operational Keys: APPROVED
Decision:
- The stable offline root identity key does not use routine automatic expiry.
- Root replacement occurs only through an explicitly authorized recovery or identity-transition procedure.
- Every operational signing key must have an explicit validity window with not-before and not-after times.
- Operational keys must be rotated before expiry; a controlled overlap may exist only to complete a safe transition.
- Expired, not-yet-valid, revoked, or purpose-mismatched keys fail closed for new signatures and new protocol actions.
- Key-compromise revocation remains permanent and must not expire automatically.
- Exact operational-key lifetimes are purpose-specific and deferred until implementation tests establish safe values.
- Historical verification may accept an old signature only when the verifier can prove that the key was authorized, within its validity window, correct for that purpose, and not revoked at the relevant signing time.


Implementation direction:
The demo and current development should implement rotation behavior rather than using permanent operational keys. Initial lifetime values may be configurable test parameters; they do not become normative protocol constants without a later explicit decision.


Rationale:
This preserves the stable Operator ID and offline root while limiting the exposure period of daily signing keys. It also avoids inventing fixed rotation periods before the purpose-specific operational behavior has been tested.


A kërkohet HSM për production profile, apo mbetet implementation-specific?
F14 — Production Key-Custody Requirements: APPROVED
Production key custody is a deployment-security requirement layered above the frozen Core v0.1 wire and COSE rules; it does not modify D1–D6 schemas, algorithms, kid bindings, signatures, or protocol bytes.


- The offline root identity key MUST use hardware-backed, isolated, or equivalently protected custody and MUST NOT be routinely exportable.
- High-authority operational keys, including identity/registry and delegation keys, MUST use non-exportable HSM, KMS, hardware token, or an equivalent controlled signing boundary in production.
- The protocol does not mandate a particular vendor, cloud, device, API, or HSM product.
- Lower-authority and short-lived keys, including transport/session keys, MAY use TPM, secure enclave, protected process memory, or another custody mechanism appropriate to their lifetime and threat model.
- IAM, least privilege, audit, approval, and separation-of-duties controls govern access to signing operations; IAM does not replace cryptographic key-purpose enforcement.
- Demo and development profiles MAY use software keys only when clearly marked non-production and MUST preserve the same key hierarchy, one-key-one-purpose rules, rotation behavior, and fail-closed verification semantics.
- A deployment claiming the production profile MUST document its custody mechanism and prove that root and high-authority private key material cannot be ordinarily exported.


Compatibility note: F14 adds no wire fields and does not alter the existing Ed25519-only Core v0.1 implementation. Future custody integrations are implementation work, not implicit protocol-version changes.


Si provohet që një key i ri autorizohet nga root key i operatorit?
F15 — Root-Signed Key Authorization Records: APPROVED
Every new operational key MUST be authorized by a valid Key Authorization Record. During normal operation, the record MUST be signed by the currently authorized identity root. During verified recovery, it MAY be authorized only by an explicitly pre-authorized threshold recovery authority acting through a valid RecoveryTransitionRecord; a compromised, revoked, retired, or superseded root MUST NOT authorize new keys. The record binds the Operator ID, identity generation, new key ID, public key, single approved purpose, validity window, monotonic generation and sequence, and optional previous key ID for rotation continuity. Verifiers MUST validate the signer’s current authority, Operator ID and identity-generation binding, purpose, validity window, continuity, freshness, and revocation state. Missing, stale, expired, revoked, cross-purpose, compromised-signer, or otherwise invalid authorization fails closed. The authorization chain MUST remain independently auditable and verifiable without changing the stable Operator ID when continuity-preserving recovery is valid.


C. Delegation authority
Kush ka të drejtë me delegu një emër ose service: global authority, operatori, tenant-i apo owner-i i emrit?
F16 — Registry-Rooted Name Ownership and Owner-Controlled Delegation: APPROVED
Vendimi:
• Për public/global NBSR, ownership root i një domain-i ose emri verifikohet përmes një global naming authority ose registry duke reuse sa më shumë logjikën ekzistuese të DNS registration dhe DNSSEC ownership.
• Global authority regjistron dhe vërteton owner-in fillestar të emrit, statusin dhe lifecycle-in e tij; nuk menaxhon services, routes ose delegime operative ditore.
• Verified owner-i është burimi fillestar i delegation authority për domain-in ose emrin.
• Owner-i mund t’ia delegojë service-publication dhe operational authority një ose më shumë NBSR operators, destination operators, tenants ose sub-operators.
• Operatorët dhe tenant-ët mund të delegojnë më tej vetëm scope-in dhe sub-delegation rights që i kanë marrë në mënyrë eksplicite.
• Një delegation nuk mund të zgjerojë emrin, service scope-in, ports, territorin, tenant-in ose authority përtej asaj që deleguesi zotëron.
• Operatorët e tjerë mund ta verifikojnë chain-in nga ownership root deri te operatori final dhe ta bëjnë service-in të aksesueshëm për users pa konfigurim per-user.
• Private/internal NBSR mund të përdorë local naming authority ose private consortium registry me të njëjtat delegation semantics.
• Çdo delegation pa ownership proof, me chain të pavlefshëm ose që tejkalon scope-in refuzohet fail-closed.


Shembull konceptual:
Global naming authority / registry → verified owner i google.com → operatorët ose services e autorizuara të Google → destination edges dhe users globalë.


Arsyeja:
Ky model ruan logjikën e provuar hierarkike të DNS-it për ownership global, por e zgjeron me delegim kriptografik të authority për service publication, operators dhe secure routing pa e kthyer global registry-n në kontrollues të operimeve ditore.


A lidhet delegation me emër, service ID, tenant, territor apo kombinim të tyre?


F17 — Explicit Composite Delegation Scope: APPROVED
Vendimi:
• Çdo delegation duhet të ketë scope canonical, eksplicit dhe të nënshkruar.
• Scope-i lidhet minimum me name_scope, service_id ose me të dyja.
• Tenant ID, destination/operator ID, region/territory, allowed actions, ports dhe protocols mund të shtohen si kufizime shtesë kur aplikohen.
• Një field që mungon nuk nënkupton automatikisht authority të pakufizuar; authority globale ose e plotë duhet të deklarohet me një vlerë eksplicite normative.
• Deleguesi nuk mund të japë scope më të gjerë, actions shtesë ose të drejtë sub-delegation përtej authority që ka marrë vetë.
• Verifikuesi kontrollon përputhjen e name, service, tenant, operator, region, actions, ports dhe protocols sipas scope-it të deklaruar.
• Çdo ambiguity, scope widening, mismatch ose authority i padeleguar refuzohet fail-closed.


Arsyeja:
Delegation e përbërë ruan hierarkinë e emrave sipas logjikës DNS, por e bën authority e NBSR precize për services, tenants, operatorë, rajone dhe kufizime teknike. Kjo parandalon që një delegation e ngushtë të interpretohet si kontroll mbi krejt domain-in ose krejt services.


A lejohet sub-delegation?
F18 — Controlled Sub-Delegation with Separate Authority Classes: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Sub-delegation lejohet vetëm kur parent delegation e autorizon shprehimisht.
• Service ownership/publication authority dhe federation/access authority janë klasa të ndara autoriteti.
• Një ISP mundet me delegu te një Sub-ISP federation access, resolution, route establishment, subscriber-serving authority dhe përdorimin e upstream gateways ose trust-it të ISP-së.
• Sub-ISP mund t’i bëjë services ekzistuese të qasshme për subscribers e vet, por nuk fiton pronësi ose të drejtë me ndryshu, publiku ose ridelegu service identity të owner-it.
• ISP nuk mundet me delegu service ownership ose publication authority që nuk e posedon vetë.
• Çdo sub-delegation vetëm mund ta ngushtojë scope-in, actions, territory, validity window dhe remaining depth; nuk mund t’i zgjerojë ato.
• Expiry e child delegation nuk mund ta kalojë expiry-n e parent-it.
• Çdo hap duhet të jetë i nënshkruar, i lidhur me parent delegation dhe i verifikueshëm në delegation chain.
• Authority-class mismatch, scope expansion ose sub-delegation pa leje eksplicite refuzohet fail-closed.


Arsyeja:
Kjo lejon hierarki reale ISP → Sub-ISP dhe u mundëson operatorëve më të vegjël t’ua ofrojnë subscribers services që ekzistojnë, pa u dhënë atyre kontroll mbi ownership ose publication authority të atyre services.


Sa është maximum delegation depth?
F19 — Authority-Depth-Bounded Delegation; Reachability Not Limited by Authority Depth: APPROVED
Decision:
- Maximum delegation depth applies only to signed authority chains that transfer ownership, service-publication, administration, or sub-delegation rights.
- Delegation depth does not limit ISP transit, federation reachability, route propagation, or the number of operators between a client and the destination operator.
- A client of a valid operator may request any valid public NBSR service unless an explicit policy denies that access.
- ISPs and Sub-ISPs may use upstream federation relationships to make services reachable without receiving ownership or service-publication authority.
- Reachability paths may traverse multiple operators, but each federation relationship and the destination operator proof must be independently validated.
- Transit or reachability propagation never creates ownership, publication, administration, or sub-delegation authority.
- Authority chains that exceed their profile-specific bound, or that mix authority classes with reachability semantics, fail closed. Federation reachability traversal remains cycle-detected and profile-bounded for hops, graph nodes, CPU work, retries, response size, and time.


Rationale:
NBSR must separate control over a service from the ability to reach that service. Authority delegation is a bounded signed chain; service reachability is a federation routing graph and must not become unavailable merely because several ISPs or upstream operators are involved.


Si parandalohen delegation loops?
F20 — Acyclic Delegation Chains: APPROVED
Every authority delegation record MUST bind the delegator, delegatee, authority class, scope, validity window, and parent delegation digest. A verifier MUST reconstruct the authority chain back to the verified owner or root authority. The same Operator ID or delegation digest MUST NOT appear more than once in the same authority chain. A child delegation MUST reference exactly one valid, older parent, MUST preserve the same authority class, and MUST only maintain or narrow scope and validity. Cycles, duplicate actors, repeated digests, broken parent references, ambiguous ancestry, or any attempt to regain broader authority through a loop MUST fail closed. This restriction applies to authority delegation chains; federation reachability may use multiple network paths and is not modeled as a delegation chain.


A duhet çdo delegation me pasë not_before, expires_at, generation dhe sequence?
F21 — Time-Bounded and Monotonic Delegation Records: APPROVED
Every authority delegation MUST include explicit not_before, expires_at, generation, and sequence values. A child delegation MUST NOT become valid before its parent and MUST NOT outlive the parent delegation. Generation values MUST never decrease and represent authority-lineage transitions such as recovery or replacement; sequence values MUST increase monotonically for normal updates within the same generation. Verifiers MUST reject future, expired, stale, replayed, rollback, non-monotonic, or otherwise inconsistent delegation records fail-closed. These fields govern signed authority delegation records only and do not limit ISP transit paths or public-service reachability.


A duhet delegations me qenë të revokueshme menjëherë?
F22 — Immediate Delegation Revocation: APPROVED
Every delegation MUST be revocable before expires_at. The delegator that issued it, or a higher authority in the same valid chain, MAY revoke it. Revocation MUST identify the exact delegation digest, generation, and affected scope. Revoking a parent delegation invalidates every dependent child delegation. The issuer MUST initiate revocation propagation immediately. Every receiver MUST enforce it immediately after verified receipt. Federation-wide convergence MUST meet a profile-frozen maximum propagation deadline and a peer that cannot prove sufficiently fresh revocation state enters restricted, quarantine, or fail-closed behavior according to risk. New sessions and new grants that depend on a revoked delegation MUST fail closed as soon as the revocation is known. Key compromise, fraud, or ownership loss create terminal revocation and old records MUST NOT resurrect the authority. Existing-session behavior is severity-specific and is decided separately.


A duhet një delegation e re me emërtu digest-in e delegation-it paraprak?
F23 — Hash-Linked Delegation Continuity with Automated Child Rollover: APPROVED
Decision:
- Every update or replacement of an existing delegation lineage MUST reference the digest of the immediately previous delegation version.
- A child delegation MUST reference its active parent delegation digest; a first delegation in a lineage has no previous delegation digest.
- Parent updates MUST use a controlled transition window rather than immediately invalidating otherwise valid dependent children.
- The parent automation system MUST discover all dependent child delegations, publish the parent transition, and request rollover from each child issuer. Each child issuer MUST independently validate the new parent and reissue or rebind only its own compatible child delegation to the new parent digest. No controller may sign or reissue authority on behalf of another principal.
- A child that no longer fits the new parent authority MUST NOT be migrated automatically; it MUST be quarantined for revocation or explicit human review.
- The old and new parent versions MAY overlap only for a bounded rollout window. The old version MUST retire after successful migration or expiry of that window. Children that are not independently acknowledged and migrated before retirement MUST fail closed after the old parent ceases to be valid.
- Critical dependent services SHOULD remain available through a bounded last-known-good transition state when ordinary rollover fails. Emergency compromise revocation overrides continuity and MAY invalidate the parent and children immediately.
- Rollover automation MUST be idempotent, resumable, auditable, observable, and safe to retry. Forks, missing predecessors, stale references, failed validation, or broken continuity fail closed for new authority.


Rationale:
Hash linking protects against rollback and equivocation, while automated child rollover prevents routine parent updates from interrupting valid services.


Cross-Cutting Automation Requirements for F1–F23: APPROVED


Principle:
NBSR implementations MUST automate routine, deterministic, and safely verifiable operational work so that strong security does not create unnecessary deployment or adoption resistance. Automation MUST preserve the approved trust, authority, audit, and fail-closed semantics; it MUST NOT silently bypass approvals or invent authority.


Automation requirements:
- Identity and registry automation (F1–F8): generate canonical Operator IDs and representations, validate proof-of-possession, detect duplicate registrations, synchronize signed registry metadata, track lifecycle transitions, and alert on verification drift. Legal ownership decisions, merger approval, recovery, retirement, and terminal revocation remain explicitly authorized actions.
- Key lifecycle automation (F9–F15): provision one key per purpose, enforce purpose bindings, schedule rotation before expiry, create authorization requests, verify root-signed Key Authorization Records, distribute active public keys, retire old keys after safe overlap, monitor custody compliance, and propagate revocations. Root use, recovery, high-authority key replacement, and break-glass operations require policy-controlled human approval and complete audit evidence.
- Delegation creation automation (F16–F21): derive canonical scopes from approved policy, prevent scope widening, calculate child validity bounds, assign generation and sequence values, validate ancestry and loop freedom, and reject ambiguous or cross-authority-class delegations before signing.
- Delegation rollout and revocation automation (F22–F23): maintain dependency graphs, perform preflight impact analysis, revalidate and roll over compatible children, quarantine incompatible children, propagate revocation state, invalidate dependent new grants, and preserve bounded service continuity when security policy permits.
- Continuous verification: operators SHOULD continuously check expiry, stale state, broken digest links, invalid signatures, authority-class mismatches, custody violations, failed propagation, unresolved forks, and services at risk of interruption.
- Safe execution: automation MUST be idempotent, transactional where possible, resumable after failure, observable, rate-bounded, and fully auditable. It MUST support dry-run and impact-preview modes for high-impact changes.
- Human control: routine low-risk changes MAY be fully automated. Ownership transfer, root-key actions, emergency recovery, terminal revocation, policy expansion, and any operation that increases authority MUST require explicit approval under separation-of-duties rules.
- Failure behavior: automation failure MUST NOT silently broaden authority. The system uses bounded last-known-good state only where an approved continuity policy allows it; otherwise new authority and new grants fail closed while operators receive actionable alerts.


Implementation direction:
The WP8 reference implementation and demo SHOULD include an automation controller that reconciles desired signed state with observed federation state, similar to a policy-controlled reconciliation loop. Manual operation remains possible for testing and recovery, but routine production operation should not depend on humans editing individual records.


Çka ndodh kur dy delegations valide pretendojnë të njëjtin emër?
F24 — Single Authoritative Ownership, Multiple Authorized Operators: APPROVED
An exact name and exact ownership scope MUST have only one active authoritative ownership lineage. Multiple operators MAY be authorized in parallel only when their delegations derive from the same verified owner or the same valid authority lineage, including regional, redundancy, migration, or load-distribution scopes. Independent claims from different ownership lineages MUST NOT be merged, ranked by operator size, or accepted by first-seen behavior. When one claim is already authoritative and the competing claim is cryptographically invalid, stale, unauthorized, or otherwise deterministically rejectable, the accepted lineage remains active and the invalid claim is rejected. When two competing lineages remain cryptographically plausible or accepted evidence conflicts, the exact affected ownership scope MUST enter QUARANTINE under F115; new authority fails closed until the conflict-resolution process publishes a signed outcome. Automation SHOULD detect overlapping scopes before publication and block unauthorized duplicate claims.


A lejohet delegation e pjesshme vetëm për disa services ose ports?
F25 — Partial Service and Technical-Scope Delegation: APPROVED
A delegation MAY authorize only a bounded subset of a name’s services or technical scope, including specific Service IDs, service classes, ports, protocols, regions, tenants, destination operators, actions, or time windows. The delegated subset MUST be expressed canonically and explicitly; omitted fields MUST NOT be interpreted as unrestricted authority. A partial delegatee may publish, operate, or sub-delegate only the exact authority granted and only when sub-delegation is explicitly permitted. Parent and owner constraints from F17 and F26 MUST be inherited and may only be maintained or narrowed. Overlapping partial delegations MAY coexist when they derive from the same authoritative ownership lineage and their scopes are compatible, such as separate services, ports, regions, redundancy roles, or migration windows. Conflicting claims, scope widening, ambiguous wildcards, cross-service authority, or use outside the authorized technical subset MUST fail closed. Automation SHOULD validate intersections before signing, detect unintended overlap, and block publication that would create unauthorized authority.


A mundet deleguesi me kufizu çka lejohet të delegohet më tutje?
F26 — Owner-Controlled Delegation Policy Envelope: APPROVED
Vendimi:
• Çdo delegation mund të përmbajë një owner policy envelope canonical dhe të nënshkruar që përcakton scope-in, services, names, ports, protocols, regions, validity, actions, sub-delegation rights, approval requirements dhe automation permissions.
• Owner-i ruan kontroll ekskluziv mbi authority expansion, ownership transfer, service-publication authority të re, rritjen e delegation depth, ndryshimin e recovery authority dhe rikthimin pas terminal revocation.
• Operatorët mund të menaxhojnë edges, gateways, routing, failover, endpoint updates, operational-key rotation dhe ISP/Sub-ISP access vetëm brenda kufijve të deklaruar nga owner-i.
• Automation mund të kryejë renewal, validation, key rotation, child rollover, dependency updates, deployment dhe rollback vetëm kur policy envelope e autorizon shprehimisht.
• Child delegations duhet t’i trashëgojnë të gjitha kufizimet e parent-it dhe vetëm mund t’i ruajnë ose ngushtojnë ato.
• Çdo authority expansion, permission shtesë ose dobësim i kufizimeve kërkon owner authorization të ri dhe, kur është high-risk, approval njerëzor sipas cross-cutting automation requirements.
• Policy mismatch, scope widening, unauthorized automation ose mungesë e required approval refuzohet fail-closed.


Arsyeja:
Owner-i duhet ta kontrollojë intent-in dhe kufijtë e authority, operatorët duhet ta menaxhojnë operimin, ndërsa automation duhet ta kryejë punën rutinë. Ky model rrit kontrollin e owner-it pa e bërë sistemin të vështirë për përdorim dhe adoptim.


D. Name dhe service ownership
Kush konsiderohet autoriteti final për një emër?
F27 — Unified Name Registration, Ownership and Automated Service Onboarding: APPROVED
Decision:
- Public names use globally unique registry semantics and one active authoritative ownership lineage.
- A user may search, reserve, pay for, register, and manage a name from one unified dashboard.
- Registration automatically creates a signed NameOwnershipRecord bound to the verified owner. Service onboarding separately creates zero, one, or more stable Service IDs under that ownership. A default service MAY be created only when an explicitly selected product profile requests it.
- The registry verifies uniqueness, ownership status, payment/renewal state, and lifecycle; it does not become the owner or operate the service.
- The owner may attach one or more NBSR delivery operators, CDNs, hosting providers, or self-hosted origins without transferring ownership.
- DNS, DNSSEC, certificates, service publication, federation discovery, delivery configuration, validation, renewal, and routine reconciliation SHOULD be automated wherever the selected providers support it.
- Self-hosted origins MAY connect through an authenticated outbound NBSR tunnel without publishing the origin IP or requiring a public inbound port.
- All federated NBSR operators can validate the signed publication and make the service reachable without per-ISP manual configuration.
- Changing CDN, delivery operator, edge provider, or origin does not change the name, owner, or stable service identity.
- Ownership transfer, recovery, terminal revocation, and authority expansion require explicit high-assurance approval; routine operational changes may be reconciled automatically within owner policy.
- Existing DNS and Web PKI remain valid compatibility and bootstrap mechanisms until a later explicit versioned decision changes their role.


Rationale:
The owner should manage the name and desired service intent, while automation handles provider configuration and federation publication. This reduces manual DNS/CDN complexity, preserves provider portability, and makes secure self-hosting practical without weakening ownership controls.


A duhet NBSR me reuse DNSSEC/Web PKI ownership, apo me pasë ownership registry të vetin?
F28 — Hybrid Existing-Internet Proof with NBSR Ownership Records: APPROVED


Vendimi:
• Për domain-et ekzistuese, NBSR reuse DNS registration, DNSSEC, registrar evidence dhe Web PKI si prova bootstrap për ownership.
• Pas verifikimit, krijohet një signed NBSR ownership record që përdoret nga federation-i për authorization, delegation dhe automation.
• NBSR ownership registry nuk e zëvendëson menjëherë ICANN/DNS; vepron si federation ownership layer mbi sistemin ekzistues.
• DNSSEC dhe Web PKI shërbejnë si prova fillestare ose revalidation evidence, jo si kontroll operativ për çdo request NBSR.
• Native NBSR names dhe private/internal names mund të regjistrohen drejtpërdrejt në NBSR registry ose në private naming authority.
• Unified dashboard duhet ta zgjedhë dhe ekzekutojë automatikisht metodën e verifikimit, të krijojë ownership record-in dhe ta publikojë në federation.
• Nëse external ownership evidence dhe NBSR ownership record nuk pajtohen, ndryshimi ndalet, services ekzistuese mbeten në safe state sipas policy-së dhe kërkohet revalidation.
• Ownership transfer, recovery ose authority expansion kërkojnë explicit approval; normal reconciliation, renewal dhe evidence refresh automatizohen.


Arsyeja:
Ky model e përdor Internetin aktual si urë për adoption dhe kompatibilitet, ndërsa NBSR krijon një layer të vetin të nënshkruar, të automatizuar dhe provider-neutral për ownership dhe federation.


Si migrohet një emër nga operatori A te operatori B?
F29 — Multi-Operator Delivery and Zero-Downtime Migration: APPROVED
Një name ose service MAY përdorë disa CDN/NBSR delivery operators njëkohësisht pa ndryshuar ownership-in ose stable service identity. Owner-i kontrollon regions, services, priorities, weights, capacity limits, health thresholds, cost/latency policy, failover dhe emergency-only roles për secilin operator. Multi-CDN MAY funksionojë active-active ose active-standby. Migrimi nga operatori A te operatori B trajtohet si ndryshim i signed delivery policy dhe allocation-it të traffic-ut, jo si ownership transfer. Automation MUST kryejë preflight validation, health checks, gradual traffic shifting, session-aware draining, dependency rollover, failover dhe rollback në last-known-good state. Operatorët e vjetër dhe të rinj MAY kenë overlap të kontrolluar gjatë transition window. Operator-i i vjetër humb authority vetëm pasi owner-i e revokon, delegation-i skadon ose migrimi përfundon sipas policy-së. Asnjë CDN ose delivery operator nuk fiton ownership, service-publication authority jashtë scope-it, ose të drejtë transferimi vetëm nga fakti që shërben traffic.


A duhet ownership transfer me kërku nënshkrim nga owner-i i vjetër dhe i ri?
F30 — Dual-Signed Ownership Transfer: APPROVED
Ownership transfer normal MUST require an explicit signed transfer authorization from the current owner and an explicit signed acceptance from the new owner. The registry MUST verify both signatures, the authoritative ownership lineage, name scope, transfer intent, generation freshness, revocation state, and the identity of both parties before activating the transition. The transfer MUST be published as a signed ownership-continuity record. Existing service identities MAY remain stable, but delegations, CDN/operator authority, recovery policy, and automation permissions MUST be revalidated under the new owner and MUST NOT be inherited automatically. Missing consent, stale records, mismatched scope, replay, ambiguity, or unilateral transfer MUST fail closed. Recovery, legal intervention, or lost-key cases use a separate high-assurance process and are not treated as normal dual-signed transfer.


Çka ndodh kur owner-i i vjetër humb keys?
F31 — Downloadable Recovery Keys with Existing Recovery Patterns: APPROVED
Owner registration MUST offer a downloadable recovery kit based on established recovery patterns rather than inventing a new ceremony. The kit MAY contain one-time recovery codes, an offline recovery key, printable backup material, hardware-security-key enrollment instructions, or threshold recovery shares for larger organizations. Recovery material MUST NOT be used for normal operations, MUST be storable offline, and SHOULD be verifiable from the dashboard before an emergency. A used recovery code or key MUST be invalidated or rotated. Successful recovery creates a new ownership generation, revokes or retires the lost keys, and preserves the stable name and service identity. If both normal keys and the recovery kit are lost, recovery escalates to a higher-assurance registry process using existing ownership and organization evidence, notifications, delay, audit, and dispute handling. Automation SHOULD guide download, verification, rotation, and recovery without weakening high-risk approval controls.


A lejohet emergency recovery authority?
F32 — Restricted Emergency Recovery Authority: APPROVED
Emergency recovery authority MAY be configured in advance as a tightly bounded break-glass mechanism. It does not receive normal ownership or operational authority and MAY activate only when normal owner keys and the recovery kit are unavailable, invalid, or confirmed compromised. Recovery MAY use one or more pre-registered trustees and SHOULD support threshold approval such as 2-of-3 for organizational profiles. Activation MUST place the name in a restricted recovery state. While in this state, the recovery authority MAY create a new ownership generation, revoke compromised keys, and restore control to the verified owner. It MUST NOT transfer ownership, expand authority, sell or reassign the name, change the stable service identity, or create unrelated delegations. Every activation MUST be time-bounded, auditable, notified to the owner through available channels, and published through the transparency system for public ownership.


Si parandalohet që recovery authority ta marrë kontrollin pa të drejtë?
F33 — Threshold Recovery Guardrails (Normative Detail of F32): APPROVED
Recovery authority is not a separate recovery flow; it is the restricted break-glass mechanism defined by F32. Public ownership recovery MUST require a pre-registered threshold set such as 2-of-3 or 3-of-5, with each trustee signing independently. Recovery activation MUST notify the owner through all registered channels and SHOULD use a controlled dispute delay unless an immediately verifiable compromise requires faster containment. During recovery, the authority MAY only create a new owner-key generation, revoke compromised keys, and restore control to the verified owner. It MUST NOT transfer ownership, sell the name, expand authority, add unrelated services, or change the stable service identity. A valid owner key or unused recovery code MAY cancel a pending recovery. All recovery actions MUST be auditable and published through the applicable transparency mechanism. After successful recovery, recovery codes, trustee authorizations, and affected keys MUST be rotated automatically.


A duhet service identity me qenë e përhershme edhe kur ndryshon operatori ose origin-i?
F34 — Stable Service Identity Independent of Operator and Origin: APPROVED
A service identity MUST represent the service itself rather than its current server, IP address, CDN, operator, region, or hosting platform. The stable Service ID and name ownership MUST remain unchanged when origins, hosting environments, delivery operators, CDN providers, regions, endpoints, or routing policies change. Operational changes MUST be published as versioned service records and reconciled automatically. Multiple operators or CDNs MAY serve the same stable Service ID under valid owner-controlled delegations. A new Service ID is required only when the owner intentionally declares a semantically new service, not for migration, scaling, failover, upgrade, or infrastructure replacement.


E. Transparency log
Cilat objekte duhet me u futur në transparency log?
F35 — Trust-Critical Objects Only in Transparency Logs: APPROVED
Transparency logs MUST contain immutable evidence for objects and lifecycle events that change public trust, ownership, identity, authority, or revocation state. This includes Operator ID registration and lifecycle changes; key authorization, rotation, and revocation records; name ownership records and transfers; delegations and sub-delegations; delegation revocations; recovery activation and completion; terminal revocations; trust-bundle versions; signed checkpoints; and conflict or equivocation evidence. Normal operational traffic, subscriber sessions, individual RouteGrants, client identities, private origin addresses, routine health checks, rapidly changing traffic weights, and sensitive tenant metadata MUST NOT be published. When verification of a service-publication or other sensitive object is required, the log SHOULD contain a digest or privacy-preserving commitment rather than exposing the full operational record. Logging and proof publication SHOULD be automated, while privacy and object-class policy MUST be enforced fail-closed.


A logohen Operator IDs, keys, delegations, revocations, ownership transfers dhe trust bundles?
F36 — Full Public Records vs Privacy-Preserving Commitments: APPROVED
Public/global transparency logs MUST publish complete records when public verification requires the object itself, including Operator ID registry records, public key authorization and revocation records, public ownership records and transfers, public delegation authority and revocations, trust-bundle versions, and signed checkpoints. Objects containing sensitive private, tenant, recovery, origin, topology, or operational-policy data MUST instead be represented by canonical digests or privacy-preserving commitments unless explicit disclosure is authorized. A commitment MUST still support later proof that the exact object existed, was ordered, and was not modified or silently replaced. Private/internal records MUST NOT become public merely because their digest is logged.


A duhet log-u me qenë global apo një log për secilin operator?
F37 — Federated Transparency Logs with Public Anchoring: APPROVED
Public/global NBSR MUST use multiple federation transparency logs for trust-critical global records rather than one central database or only operator-controlled logs. Operators and private deployments MAY maintain local logs with additional operational detail. A public-federation operator MUST periodically anchor signed checkpoints or commitments from its local log into one or more accepted public federation logs. Public logs MUST exclude ordinary traffic, subscriber activity, secrets, and sensitive operational topology. Local logs MAY retain richer evidence under their own access policy. Public anchoring MUST make deletion, rewriting, rollback, or selective history presentation detectable without requiring the federation to store every local event.


A duhet me pasë disa independent logs?
F38 — Multiple Independent Logs with Scope-Based Filtering: APPROVED
Public NBSR supports multiple independent transparency logs, but records are not replicated blindly to every global or local log. Each log declares an explicit scope such as global public trust, region, operator, consortium, enterprise/private deployment, namespace, record type, visibility class, or federation membership. Records are published only to logs whose scope, policy, privacy classification, and trust requirements match the object.


Trust-critical global records MUST be published to a profile-frozen threshold of independent global logs, while regional, operator-local, consortium, and private records remain within their relevant logs. The exact minimum and threshold MUST be frozen in the public federation implementation profile before wire freeze. Local or private logs may publish only checkpoints or privacy-preserving commitments to broader federation logs when required. Clients and operators retrieve and verify only the log partitions relevant to their trust policy and scope rather than downloading the complete global history.


Automation selects the eligible logs, enforces minimum independent-log thresholds, performs filtered publication and replication, verifies inclusion and consistency proofs, republishes after log-set changes, and prevents sensitive records from being exposed outside their authorized scope.


A duhet klienti me kërku inclusion proof për çdo objekt?
F39 — Operator-Level Inclusion Verification with Bounded Caching: APPROVED
Operatorët, federation controllers dhe independent monitors MUST verify inclusion proofs for trust-critical records before treating them as accepted federation state. A normal lightweight client MUST NOT request or verify a new inclusion proof for every connection. Verified proof state MAY be cached together with the exact signed checkpoint, object digest, generation, sequence, validity, log scope and freshness metadata.


A fresh proof or revalidation is required when the object changes, a newer generation or sequence appears, a key or delegation rotates, revocation or ownership transfer occurs, the accepted checkpoint exceeds freshness policy, the object is first observed, or conflict, rollback, equivocation or split-view evidence exists. Revocation state MUST be refreshed more aggressively than immutable historical records. High-assurance clients MAY verify proofs directly, but ordinary clients MAY rely on an operator that presents already verified authority.


WP8 SHOULD reuse the existing WP6 building blocks for canonical digests, monotonic generation and sequence, previous-digest continuity, bounded retained state, idempotence, rollback rejection, equivocation detection and fail-closed verification. WP6 snapshots remain internal continuity objects and MUST NOT silently become federation wire objects or transparency logs. InclusionProof and TransparencyCheckpoint are new versioned federation/transparency objects. Existing Core v0.1 ServiceRecord, RouteIntent and RouteGrant schemas MUST NOT be modified implicitly to add them.


Plain-language meaning: an inclusion proof is cryptographic evidence that a specific record really appears in the accepted transparency log and was not invented only for one operator or client.


A duhet checkpoint-i me qenë Merkle-tree root i nënshkruar?
F40 — Signed Merkle-Tree Transparency Checkpoints: APPROVED
A transparency checkpoint MUST be a compact signed summary of one exact log state. It MUST include at least the Log ID, declared scope or partition, tree size, Merkle root hash, checkpoint timestamp, monotonic checkpoint sequence or generation, and checkpoint signing-key ID. The checkpoint MUST be signed by the log’s authorized checkpoint/transparency key. Inclusion proofs MUST demonstrate that an exact record or commitment is contained in the tree represented by that checkpoint, while consistency proofs MUST demonstrate that a newer checkpoint extends the earlier history without deletion, rewriting, rollback, or substitution. Operators and federation controllers MAY cache one verified checkpoint and use it to verify many records. The checkpoint MUST NOT contain every underlying record and MUST NOT expose private log content beyond its authorized scope. WP8 SHOULD reuse established Merkle-tree transparency-log patterns and the repository’s existing canonical digest, monotonic-version, previous-state, rollback-detection, and fail-closed principles rather than redefine WP6 snapshots or silently modify Core v0.1 objects.


Sa shpesh publikohet checkpoint-i?
F41 — Periodic, Threshold-Based and Immediate Checkpoint Publication: APPROVED
Checkpoint publication MUST support three trigger classes: periodic publication within a declared maximum_checkpoint_delay; threshold-based publication after a bounded batch or record-count threshold; and immediate publication when technical or security policy requires the new state to become verifiable without waiting for the normal batch. Immediate publication MUST be available for key compromise, emergency revocation, ownership or delegation revocation, recovery activation, critical trust-bundle changes, split-view or equivocation evidence, incident response, and technically urgent migration, cutover, or authority activation events. An authorized owner or operator MAY request publish-now when permitted by policy. Automation MUST select the correct trigger, prevent checkpoint flooding through bounded rate controls, prioritize critical events, and fail closed if urgent trust state cannot be published within its required deadline. Exact normal intervals and thresholds remain profile-specific until WP8 testing establishes safe values.


Si provohet consistency ndërmjet checkpoint-eve?
F42 — Append-Only Consistency Proofs Between Checkpoints: APPROVED
Every non-genesis checkpoint MUST be an append-only continuation of an earlier trusted checkpoint. A genesis checkpoint has no predecessor and MUST use the profile-frozen genesis representation. The log MUST provide a Merkle consistency proof between the two tree sizes so that operators and monitors can verify that all earlier records remain unchanged and that only new records were appended. A checkpoint MUST be rejected fail-closed if the tree size decreases, earlier history changes, the proof is missing or invalid, or a fork cannot be resolved. Proof verification SHOULD be automated and cacheable, and a verifier MAY request a direct proof from its last trusted checkpoint to the newest checkpoint even if intermediate checkpoints were missed. WP8 SHOULD reuse the monotonicity, digest-chaining, rollback protection, and equivocation-detection principles already established in WP6 without changing the frozen Core v0.1 objects.


Çka ndodh kur dy klientë marrin checkpoint-e të ndryshme?
F43 — Split-View Detection, Safe Containment and Forensic Traceability: APPROVED
Checkpoints MUST be compared by log ID, scope, tree size, sequence, timestamp and Merkle root. Different roots for the same log, scope and tree size or sequence constitute equivocation evidence. An older checkpoint is acceptable only when a valid consistency proof connects it to the newer checkpoint.


When a conflict is detected, the disputed checkpoint MUST NOT authorize new records, delegations, grants or trust changes. The verifier MUST preserve the last-known-good checkpoint, quarantine conflicting evidence, notify relevant operators, witnesses and monitors, and MAY temporarily remove the affected log from the accepted log set. Existing sessions are handled according to severity and revocation policy rather than being terminated automatically by every unresolved suspicion.


The transparency system MUST also preserve a bounded forensic trace showing how the conflicting checkpoint reached each observer. Evidence SHOULD include the signed checkpoint bytes or digest, log ID, log node or endpoint identity, checkpoint signing key ID, tree size, sequence, scope, observed and received timestamps, requesting or receiving operator ID, source peer or witness when known, consistency-proof result, inclusion-proof context, transport/request correlation identifier, and the previous trusted checkpoint digest. Each forwarding operator, mirror, witness or cache that republishes a checkpoint SHOULD attach a signed receipt or provenance statement that links the received checkpoint digest to the next propagation step without exposing subscriber identity or sensitive traffic metadata.


Automation MUST correlate these signed receipts and local audit events into an append-only incident trace, identify the earliest known divergence point, distinguish an ordinary stale checkpoint from a true split view, retain both conflicting branches as evidence, and produce a privacy-safe incident report. Missing provenance does not make an invalid checkpoint valid; it reduces attribution confidence and MUST be reported explicitly. Forensic evidence MUST be tamper-evident, access-controlled, retention-bounded by policy, and must not create a global catalogue of subscribers or service traffic.


In simple terms: if two clients merely have different ages of the same history, the system synchronizes them; if they receive two incompatible histories for the same version, the log is treated as dishonest or compromised and the system preserves a signed traceback of where each version came from.


A duhet me pasë witness ose gossip mekanizëm?
F44 — Witnessed Checkpoints and Privacy-Safe Gossip: APPROVED
Operators, federation controllers, monitors, and independent witnesses SHOULD exchange signed checkpoint metadata and the minimum proofs needed to compare observed log history. Gossip MUST NOT include subscriber identities, traffic records, private origin addresses, session data, or unrelated tenant metadata. Independent witnesses MAY verify checkpoint signatures and consistency proofs and then issue a signed receipt, cosignature, or observation statement bound to the exact log ID, scope, tree size, sequence, Merkle root, timestamp, and checkpoint signing key.


Private and consortium trust policies MAY define their own witness threshold. The public/global federation profile MUST satisfy the minimum witness thresholds in F118, and a higher threshold MAY be required for high-impact checkpoints. Conflicting checkpoints for the same log state MUST be preserved as signed evidence, correlated with the provenance trace defined by F43, and distributed to the relevant monitors and operators for automated split-view detection and incident response. Gossip and witness processing MUST be bounded, authenticated, rate-limited, replay-resistant, privacy-safe, and auditable. Missing witness evidence does not silently create authority; when policy requires a witness threshold, the checkpoint fails closed for new authority until that threshold is satisfied.


Sa informata mund të publikohen pa zbulu subscriber, tenant ose service metadata sensitive?
F45 — Minimum Necessary Transparency Disclosure: APPROVED
Public transparency logs MUST disclose only the minimum information required to verify trust, ordering, freshness, revocation, inclusion, consistency, and witness evidence. Publicly visible fields MAY include log ID, declared scope, Operator ID, public key identifiers, record class, generation, sequence, validity or revocation status, canonical digest or privacy-preserving commitment, signed checkpoint metadata, and inclusion, consistency, or witness proofs.


Subscriber identity or pseudonym, client IP or device identity, session identifiers, private tenant names, private service names, origin addresses, topology, traffic volume, precise usage timing, internal policy, and other correlation-capable metadata MUST NOT be published unless an explicit policy and authorization require disclosure. Sensitive objects SHOULD be represented by domain-separated canonical commitments, using additional confidentiality protections such as keyed or salted commitments when appropriate, so an authorized party can later prove the exact object without exposing it publicly.


Publication MUST follow the scope filtering rules of F38. Global logs receive only public trust evidence; regional, operator-local, consortium, and enterprise logs retain only records authorized for their declared scope. Timestamp precision and other metadata SHOULD be reduced when exact values are unnecessary. Logging automation MUST enforce object classification and disclosure policy fail-closed and MUST NOT broaden visibility because a record is included in a transparency workflow.


In simple terms, the transparency system proves that a valid decision exists and was not rewritten without revealing who used it, where the private service runs, or how the private infrastructure is organized.


A duhet private enterprise delegations me qenë në një transparency log të veçantë?
F46 — Isolated Self-Signed Enterprise Transparency Domains: APPROVED
Enterprise deployments MAY operate as fully self-signed private NBSR trust domains. The enterprise controls its own Operator IDs, naming authorities, delegation roots, trust bundles, transparency logs, witnesses, access policies, retention rules and recovery authorities. Private delegations MUST remain inside an isolated enterprise transparency domain or an equivalently isolated partition, and only authorized identities or systems may read their records and proofs.


The private profile MUST preserve the same core transparency semantics: signed Merkle checkpoints, inclusion proofs, consistency proofs, rollback and equivocation detection, bounded retention, auditability and fail-closed verification. The enterprise MAY separate logs by tenant, environment, subsidiary, region or security classification. Public anchoring, public witnesses and global registration are NOT required for a fully internal self-signed deployment.


When an enterprise intentionally exposes a service or authority to public federation, only the minimum necessary public record, digest, checkpoint commitment or delegation proof is published. Moving from a private self-signed domain into public federation requires explicit registration, organizational verification and a new public trust decision; it MUST NOT happen automatically or silently. Public operators MUST NOT gain the right to query the enterprise’s full private log merely because a public-facing relationship exists.
F. Trust bundles
Çka përmban saktësisht një trust bundle?
F47 — Canonical Scoped Trust Bundle Contents: APPROVED
A trust bundle is the canonical signed package that tells a verifier which identities, authorities, keys, logs, witnesses, profiles, and revocation sources are trusted for a specific scope and time window. It MUST include at minimum: bundle version; monotonic generation and sequence; scope type and scope identifier; issuer ID and signing-key ID; accepted trust roots and registry or directory authorities; authorized Operator IDs or authority classes where explicitly enumerated; public keys with purpose, status, validity window, and lifecycle state such as active, next, retiring, or revoked; accepted transparency-log IDs; witness identities and required thresholds; supported NBSR profiles and protocol versions; revocation references or endpoints; creation time, expiry, and maximum staleness; previous-bundle digest for every non-genesis bundle, with that field prohibited for genesis; and policy constraints that limit where and how the bundle may be used. A trust bundle MUST NOT contain private keys, subscriber identities, session state, traffic data, private service topology, or operational secrets. Trust bundles are scoped trust material, not complete operator configuration.


Kush e nënshkruan trust bundle-in?
F48 — Scope Authority Signs the Trust Bundle: APPROVED
Global/public trust bundles MUST be authorized by the governance-controlled independent threshold defined by the global governance profile; no single global federation actor may authorize them alone. Regional bundles are signed by the authorized regional authority or its required threshold. Operator-specific bundles are signed by the operator with a dedicated trust-bundle signing key. Private enterprise or self-signed bundles are signed by the enterprise trust root or by a purpose-bound key explicitly authorized by that root. Consortium bundles are signed by the consortium authority or by a configured threshold of members.


The trust-bundle signing key MUST be single-purpose. Offline or high-assurance root keys SHOULD authorize bundle-signing keys rather than signing every routine update directly. High-impact changes MAY require threshold signatures or multi-party approval. Every verifier MUST confirm that the signer has authority for the exact bundle scope; participation in federation alone does not authorize an operator to sign a global bundle.


Si bëhet rotation-i i trust bundle-it pa e thy federation-in?
F49 — Automated Trust-Bundle Reconciliation and Activation: APPROVED
Routine trust-bundle rotation MUST be orchestrated by a policy-controlled reconciliation controller rather than by manual per-operator edits. The controller MUST construct the next bundle from approved signed desired state, publish it as next, obtain the required transparency inclusion and witness evidence, distribute it to affected operators, collect or infer readiness, and activate it at not_before only when signer authority, monotonic generation and sequence, previous-bundle digest continuity, scope policy, freshness, revocation state, and minimum rollout requirements all validate. The old and new bundles MAY overlap only within the bounded transition semantics already defined by F13, F15, F23, F35, F41, F47, and the cross-cutting automation requirements; F49 does not redefine those primitives. Routine changes such as operational-key rotation, expiry renewal, log or witness refresh, and safe retirement of old material SHOULD be fully automated, idempotent, resumable, observable, auditable, and safe to retry. Operators that remain behind MUST NOT block the federation indefinitely; policy MAY place them in warning, quarantine, restricted last-known-good, or fail-closed state without broadening authority. Authority expansion, trust-root removal or replacement, root-key actions, governance changes, emergency recovery, and other high-impact changes MUST require the applicable human, threshold, or separation-of-duties approval. Emergency compromise MAY bypass the ordinary rollout window only through explicit revocation, a newly authorized bundle, immediate transparency publication, and preservation of complete audit evidence.


A është trust bundle global, regional apo operator-specific?
F50 — Hierarchical and Composable Scoped Trust Bundles: APPROVED
NBSR supports global federation, regional, consortium, operator-specific, and enterprise/private trust bundles. Every bundle MUST declare one canonical scope_type and scope_id, and each applicable bundle MUST be verified independently. Verifiers MAY compose several bundles for one decision, but composition MUST preserve authority boundaries: a narrower bundle MAY add trust only where an authorized parent policy permits it, and MUST NOT remove higher-scope revocations, resurrect revoked keys, replace an unauthorized trust root, or expand authority beyond its scope. Regional bundles MAY add regional operators, logs, witnesses, or policies without changing global ownership. Operator bundles govern only that operator’s authorized trust material. Enterprise self-signed bundles are authoritative only inside their private trust domain unless explicit public registration and verification grants wider authority. When multiple bundles apply, restrictions are intersected rather than merged into broader authority. Conflicts, scope ambiguity, unauthorized inheritance, or hidden expansion fail closed or are isolated to the affected scope. Automation SHOULD select and verify the relevant bundle chain according to service, operator, region, federation membership, and policy.


A duhet bundle me përmbajt active, next, retiring dhe revoked keys?
F51 — Explicit Trust-Key Lifecycle States: APPROVED
Every public key represented in a trust bundle MUST have an explicit lifecycle state with deterministic verification semantics. next means the key is authorized and pre-published but MUST NOT create new signatures or authority before its not_before time and activation conditions are satisfied. active means the key MAY create new signatures and protocol actions only for its authorized purpose. retiring means the key SHOULD NOT create new authority or routine signatures but MAY remain accepted for bounded rollout continuity and verification of existing signatures. retired means the key MUST NOT be used for new operation and MAY be retained only for historical verification when the verifier proves that the signature was valid at signing time. revoked means the key MUST NOT create or validate new authority and is terminal for that key ID; compromised is recorded as a revocation reason rather than requiring a separate reusable lifecycle state.


Only active keys may normally produce new signatures. next keys MUST NOT activate early. retiring keys MUST have a bounded end condition. retired keys MUST NOT return to active. Revoked key IDs MUST NOT be reused, and the same key ID MUST NOT identify different key material. Every transition MUST be monotonic, signed, auditable, included in the applicable bundle sequence, and published through the required transparency mechanism. Routine transitions MAY be automated under approved policy, while compromise response, root-impacting changes, emergency recovery, and authority expansion require the applicable approval or threshold controls.


Sa duhet me zgjatë overlap period ndërmjet key të vjetër dhe të ri?
F52 — Bounded, Purpose-Specific and Readiness-Based Key Overlap: APPROVED
Each key purpose and deployment profile MUST define a bounded minimum_overlap and maximum_overlap rather than using one universal duration. A new key is pre-published as next and may become active only after its authorization, transparency, propagation, and readiness checks succeed. During the controlled transition, the new key is active while the old key is retiring. The old key MAY retire only after the minimum overlap has elapsed, the required operator or verifier readiness threshold has been reached, and no unresolved conflict, rollback, revocation, or propagation failure remains. The maximum overlap MUST be finite; operators that remain behind after that bound enter warning, restricted, quarantine, or bounded last-known-good behavior according to policy and MUST NOT block the entire federation indefinitely. Exact durations remain purpose-specific and configurable until WP8 testing establishes safe profile values. Emergency compromise revocation bypasses normal overlap and revokes the affected key immediately. Routine transitions SHOULD be automated, observable, auditable, resumable, and safe to retry.


A duhet bundle version me generation dhe sequence monotonic?
F53 — Monotonic Trust-Bundle Versioning: APPROVED
Every trust bundle MUST include monotonic generation and sequence values for its exact scope. sequence MUST increase for ordinary updates within the same trust-authority lineage. generation MUST increase only when the trust lineage itself is re-established or replaced, including recovery, root replacement, authority reconstruction, governance transition, or another explicitly authorized high-impact event. A verifier MUST retain the highest accepted generation, sequence, and canonical digest for each bundle scope. A lower generation or sequence is REJECT as rollback or stale state. The same generation and sequence with the same canonical digest is an idempotent ACCEPT with state_changed=false and MUST NOT create duplicate authority or side effects. The same generation and sequence with a different canonical digest is QUARANTINE as equivocation evidence. A higher version is accepted only after signer authority, continuity, scope, freshness, transparency, and revocation validation succeeds. Routine sequence allocation SHOULD be deterministic and automated. A generation transition MUST carry stronger authorization, continuity evidence, transparency publication, and the applicable human or threshold approvals. Historical verification MAY use older bundle versions only for decisions made while those versions were valid; older versions MUST NOT authorize new authority after a newer accepted version exists.


A duhet bundle i ri me referu digest-in e bundle-it paraprak?
F54 — Hash-Linked Trust-Bundle Continuity: APPROVED
Every trust bundle update within an existing scope and lineage MUST reference the canonical digest of its immediately preceding accepted bundle through previous_bundle_digest. The first bundle in a lineage has no predecessor. A verifier MUST confirm that the referenced predecessor is the last accepted bundle for the same scope, belongs to the expected generation, has valid signer authority and continuity state, and is not revoked, conflicted, or otherwise disqualified. A missing, incorrect, stale, cross-scope, or ambiguous predecessor reference MUST fail closed.


The digest MUST be computed over the complete canonical representation of the preceding bundle. A bundle MUST NOT silently skip an intermediate accepted version. A verifier that missed several versions MAY validate the complete continuity chain or an authorized proof linking its last-known-good bundle to the current one. Two different bundles that reference the same predecessor and claim incompatible continuation state constitute a fork or equivocation until resolved through the applicable transparency, witness, and governance process.


A generation transition SHOULD reference the final accepted digest of the preceding generation so that recovery, root replacement, governance transition, or authority reconstruction remains historically connected. A high-assurance recovery process MAY proceed when ordinary continuity evidence is unavailable only when the break is explicitly authorized, documented, published through the required transparency mechanism, and treated as a new generation rather than silently repaired. Automation MUST select the expected predecessor, verify observed state before publication, prevent concurrent unauthorized branches, and stop rollout when continuity does not match.


Si parandalohet rollback në një bundle të vjetër por valid?
F55 — Trust-State Rollback Protection with Independent Software Rollback: APPROVED
A verifier MUST preserve the highest accepted trust-bundle generation, sequence, digest, revocation state, and continuity evidence for each scope. A cryptographically valid but older bundle MUST NOT become authoritative again after a newer accepted trust state exists. Lower generation or sequence, a mismatched previous-bundle digest, conflicting content at the same version, or an attempt to bypass a known revocation MUST fail closed. Highest accepted state MUST survive restart, node replacement, cache loss, and failover through durable local state or recovery from accepted transparency, quorum, or trusted bootstrap evidence. A fresh installation MUST NOT trust the first bundle it sees without verifying current accepted state.


This rule governs trust state, not software release versions. Operators MUST be able to roll back a faulty NBSR software build to a previously known-good implementation, provided the rolled-back software still supports the currently authoritative protocol profile and can load, preserve, and enforce the newest accepted trust state. Software rollback MUST NOT restore old bundles, old keys, removed authorities, stale checkpoints, superseded policy, or revoked state. State migration, schema compatibility, signed release provenance, preflight checks, canary rollout, health validation, and automatic rollback SHOULD be used so a bad software release can be removed without weakening federation trust.


If an older software build cannot safely understand or enforce the current trust state or required protocol version, it MUST NOT rejoin normal authority. It MAY enter a restricted recovery mode for diagnostics, state export, or controlled upgrade, but new authority and new grants fail closed. In simple terms: code may roll back to last-known-good; security state only moves forward.


Çka ndodh kur operatorët kanë bundle versions të ndryshme?
F56 — Bounded Version Skew Between Operators: APPROVED
Operators are not required to hold the identical trust-bundle version at every instant. A bounded version skew MAY exist during controlled rollout, provided both versions belong to the same valid continuity chain, remain within the allowed freshness and skew policy, and agree on all keys, revocations, ownership, delegation authority, and restrictions relevant to the attempted operation. Each operator MUST expose the bundle generation, sequence, digest, and accepted checkpoint needed to evaluate that compatibility without disclosing unrelated private state. If the newer bundle contains a relevant revocation, compromise status, ownership change, authority reduction, policy restriction, or other security-critical change that the older operator has not yet accepted, new sessions, grants, delegations, and authority changes MUST fail closed. Existing sessions are handled according to severity and revocation policy. Automation SHOULD detect skew, request the missing bundle or proof, retry synchronization, and place an operator that exceeds the permitted window into warning, restricted, quarantine, or bounded last-known-good behavior. A lagging operator MUST NOT block federation-wide rollout indefinitely, and version skew MUST never be interpreted as permission to ignore a known revocation or expand authority.


Sa stale mund të jetë një bundle para se lidhjet të dështojnë closed?
F57 — Risk-Based Trust-Bundle Freshness and Staleness Limits: APPROVED
Every trust bundle MUST declare created_at, expires_at, maximum_staleness, and an applicable freshness class or policy profile. NBSR MUST NOT use one universal freshness duration for all bundles. Security-critical state such as revocations, compromises, ownership changes, and authority restrictions requires stricter freshness than immutable historical trust. Within the normal freshness window, ordinary authorization MAY proceed. After that window but before maximum_staleness, the verifier enters a degraded or restricted mode, MUST attempt automatic refresh through primary and alternate trusted sources, and MAY permit only operations explicitly allowed by policy. After maximum_staleness, new authority, new grants, new sessions, and trust changes MUST fail closed. Existing sessions MAY continue only according to severity, session lifetime, and revocation policy. A known revocation or compromise MUST NOT be ignored merely because the local bundle has not yet expired. Bounded last-known-good state MAY be used during directory or transparency outages, but never indefinitely and never to override known critical revocation state. Exact limits remain profile-specific for global/public, regional, operator, enterprise/private, and emergency deployments until WP8 testing establishes safe values. Automation SHOULD refresh bundles before they become stale, use retries and alternate sources, surface actionable alerts, and preserve auditable evidence of degraded operation.


Si bëhet emergency key revocation?
F58 — Immediate Revocation with Automated Emergency Key Replacement: APPROVED
Emergency key revocation MUST immediately disable the exact compromised, lost, misused, or unauthorized key without waiting for normal overlap. The revocation record MUST identify the key ID, Operator ID or authority scope, key purpose, reason, effective time, generation, sequence, and authorizing authority, and MUST be propagated through the applicable trust bundle, transparency log, revocation endpoints, and federation channels.


To preserve service continuity, the automation controller SHOULD immediately generate a new purpose-bound replacement key or activate a pre-authorized emergency replacement key already held as next in an HSM, KMS, hardware token, or equivalent signing boundary. The controller MUST create or obtain the required Key Authorization Record, publish the new bundle version, obtain the minimum required transparency and witness evidence, activate the replacement only after its authorization and scope validate, and automatically re-sign or republish dependent trust-critical objects. Transport, service-publication, and other lower-authority operational keys MAY use nearly fully automated replacement. Delegation, registry, trust-bundle-signing, root-impacting, or similarly high-authority keys require the applicable root, threshold, recovery, or separation-of-duties approval before activation, although generation, preparation, validation, and rollout MAY be automated.


The compromised key MUST NOT remain active merely for availability. New signatures, sessions, grants, or authority depending on that key fail closed as soon as revocation is known. Existing sessions are handled by severity policy through immediate termination, reauthentication, or bounded draining. A revoked key ID is terminal and MUST NOT be reused or restored; a false-positive recovery creates a newly authorized key rather than resurrecting the old one. The emergency workflow MUST be idempotent, resumable, observable, auditable, privacy-safe, and capable of completing without unnecessary service interruption.


A lejohet break-glass recovery dhe kush e autorizon?
F59 — Pre-Authorized Threshold Break-Glass Recovery: APPROVED
Break-glass recovery MAY be used only as a preconfigured, narrowly scoped and time-bounded mechanism. The applicable recovery authority, trustees, threshold, permitted actions, scope, validity, audit requirements and notification paths MUST be registered before an incident in the trust bundle or in a signed recovery policy referenced by it. Public, global and other high-authority scopes MUST require an independent threshold such as 2-of-3, 3-of-5 or a governance-controlled quorum; no single operator, administrator or person may activate high-impact recovery alone.


Break-glass recovery MAY create a new generation, authorize replacement keys, revoke compromised keys, publish an emergency trust bundle and restore the minimum trust state needed for federation continuity. It MUST NOT expand authority, add unrelated trust roots, transfer ownership, alter governance, resurrect revoked keys or serve as a shortcut for routine changes. Activation MUST be scope-bounded, time-bounded, auditable, transparent under the applicable policy and notified to relevant parties.


After recovery, emergency authority MUST be disabled, recovery keys or shares MUST be rotated, normal trust state MUST be rebuilt and the incident MUST be recorded through the applicable transparency mechanism. Private enterprise deployments MAY choose their own trustees and threshold, but MUST preserve the same separation-of-duties, non-expansion, audit and fail-closed semantics.


A duhet trust bundle me u verifiku edhe përmes transparency proof?
F60 — Transparency-Verified Trust Bundles: APPROVED
Public/global trust bundles MUST be verified not only by signature but also through the applicable transparency evidence before they authorize new trust or authority. The proof MUST bind the exact canonical bundle digest, scope, generation, sequence, log ID, and signed checkpoint. High-impact public/global bundles MUST satisfy the multi-log publication rule from F37/F38 and the witness threshold from F118; the profile MAY require a stronger independent-log threshold and MUST require consistency proof from the previously trusted checkpoint when prior trusted state exists.


A valid signature proves who issued the bundle; transparency evidence proves that the exact bundle was published into the accepted history and was not shown selectively as a hidden or conflicting version. Verifiers MUST confirm inclusion, continuity, freshness, signer authority, scope, and absence of rollback, fork, or equivocation. Missing, invalid, stale, cross-scope, or conflicting proof MUST fail closed for new authority and new trust changes. Bounded last-known-good state MAY be used only under the approved continuity and staleness policies.


Ordinary clients MUST NOT verify a fresh proof for every connection. Operators, federation controllers, monitors, and high-assurance clients MAY verify and cache proof state according to F39. Emergency bundles MAY activate through the explicit emergency policy when availability requires immediate containment, but transparency publication MUST occur immediately or within the declared emergency deadline, with complete audit evidence. Fully private enterprise deployments MAY use their own isolated transparency domain rather than a public log.
G. Federation discovery
Si e gjen operatori A autoritetin e operatorit B për një emër?
F61 — Federation Authority Discovery Inside NBSR Name Resolution: APPROVED
Federation discovery MUST be executed inside the NBSR Name/Resolution Plane rather than as a separate client-visible directory step. When an application requests a name through its ordinary resolver interface, the local NBSR Name Node MUST canonicalize the name and internally discover and validate the stable Service Identity, ownership and delegation chain, applicable policy, authorized Destination Operator ID, destination federation endpoints, trust-bundle and transparency evidence, and authorized OriginSet or connector reachability. On successful resolution, the client MUST receive only a scoped synthetic IP. The origin IP, Destination Operator ID, federation endpoints, delegation metadata, and other internal reachability details MUST NOT be exposed as the client-facing resolution result.


The synthetic mapping MUST be bound to the canonical name or Service ID, source-edge and resolution context, applicable policy, and freshness state. When the application connects normally to the synthetic IP, the Source Edge MUST recover the corresponding RouteIntent or resolution context, then create or reuse an authenticated encrypted Transport Session and independently authorize the service-bound Route Context or Service Channel. The Destination Edge MUST perform its own admission and select a validated internal Origin Endpoint or connector. Changing the origin address, CDN, delivery operator, or endpoint MUST NOT change the stable Service Identity or the universal synthetic-response contract.


DNS, DNSSEC, HTTPS directories, federation registries, caches, or direct operator configuration MAY be used internally as bootstrap or metadata sources, but they MUST NOT independently create route authority. For legacy services, conventional DNS MAY provide constrained internal reachability metadata only; its A or AAAA result MUST NOT be returned to the client as an origin fallback, and DNS TTL MUST NOT become a route lease. If service identity, authority, policy, destination-operator binding, or origin reachability cannot be validated, resolution or route establishment MUST fail closed without direct-origin fallback. Automation SHOULD perform lookup, proof retrieval, validation, synthetic allocation, caching, refresh, and reconciliation without per-service manual configuration.


A përdoret DNS për bootstrap, HTTPS directory, static trust apo NBSR federation registry?
F62 — Hybrid Bootstrap with NBSR-Native Federation Registry: APPROVED
NBSR federation discovery MUST use a hybrid model that preserves compatibility with the existing Internet while allowing native NBSR federation.


The client-facing entry point remains ordinary name resolution through the local NBSR Name Node. Every successful upgraded-network resolution returns only a scoped Synthetic IP; the client MUST NOT receive the Origin Endpoint, destination federation endpoint, or any direct-origin fallback.


For existing Internet names, ordinary DNS mechanisms MAY be used internally for compatibility, bootstrap, ownership evidence, and reachability discovery, including A, AAAA, CNAME, SVCB, HTTPS, DNSSEC, registrar evidence, and Web PKI where applicable. DNS-derived information is candidate metadata only. A DNS answer, DNSSEC validation, HTTPS response, or reachable endpoint does not by itself authorize an NBSR route.


HTTPS MAY transport signed federation records, endpoint metadata, proofs, checkpoints, and directory responses. HTTPS transport security does not replace verification of the signed NBSR objects.


The NBSR federation registry is the native source for Operator ID registration, signed federation endpoints, trust references, authority metadata, public-federation discovery, and applicable transparency evidence. NBSR-native names and private names MAY use an NBSR registry or private naming authority without depending on public DNS.


Static trust MAY be used for private enterprise, private consortium, direct operator peering, lab compatibility, isolated deployments, and explicitly configured recovery scenarios. Static trust MUST NOT be treated as the scalable default for public Internet federation.


Final authorization MUST derive from validated NBSR Service Identity, ownership, delegation, authorized Destination Operator ID, policy, trust bundle, revocation state, and required transparency evidence. Discovery sources provide candidates and metadata; signed NBSR authority determines whether a candidate is accepted.


If DNS, directory, registry, trust state, and transparency evidence disagree, the resolver MUST fail closed for new authorization and MUST NOT return an authorized Synthetic IP unless an approved bounded last-known-good policy applies. Discovery, proof retrieval, validation, caching, and refresh SHOULD be automated inside the Name/Resolution Plane.


A duhet bootstrap discovery me dhënë vetëm metadata, jo authorization?
F63 — Bootstrap Discovery Provides Candidates, Never Authority: APPROVED
Bootstrap discovery MUST provide only candidate metadata and locators; it MUST NOT create NBSR authority. DNS, HTTPS directories, static configuration, registry locators, caches, and similar bootstrap sources MAY identify where to retrieve signed operator records, federation endpoints, supported profiles, trust bundles, checkpoints, inclusion proofs, and related metadata. All bootstrap output MUST be treated as untrusted until the corresponding NBSR objects are verified.


A reachable endpoint, a valid DNSSEC chain, a valid Web PKI certificate, or a successful HTTPS response MUST NOT by itself create Service Identity, ownership, delegation, route authority, protocol or port permission, Origin Endpoint authorization, or permission to bypass revocation. Operator ID binding, endpoint authority, service ownership, delegation scope, key purpose, trust bundle, freshness, revocation state, and required transparency evidence MUST be validated independently. If bootstrap metadata conflicts with accepted signed NBSR state, the candidate MUST be rejected, the conflict MUST be recorded, and signed accepted authority remains controlling.


Static trust is a pre-provisioned trust-anchor profile, not an exception that turns reachability into authority. Even in private enterprise, consortium, direct-peering, lab, or recovery deployments, the discovered endpoint and requested action MUST remain bound to the configured Operator ID, keys, scope, policy, and validity constraints. Bootstrap metadata MAY be cached and refreshed, but changing a locator or endpoint does not change authority without an authorized signed update. The Name/Resolution Plane returns a scoped Synthetic IP only after full authorization succeeds; direct-origin fallback remains forbidden.


Si verifikohet që federation endpoint-i i gjetur i përket Operator ID-së së saktë?
F64 — Cryptographic Endpoint Binding to Operator Identity: APPROVED
A federation endpoint MUST be published in a signed Operator Record or signed Federation Endpoint Record that binds the exact Operator ID, endpoint locator, transport profile, port, endpoint role, authorized transport key or certificate binding, validity window, generation, sequence, and previous-record digest when applicable. A Source Operator MAY discover the endpoint candidate through DNS, registry, HTTPS directory, cache, or static configuration, but MUST NOT trust it until the signed record validates against the accepted trust bundle, revocation state, scope, and transparency evidence.


During the TLS or QUIC handshake, the peer MUST prove possession of the transport or federation-authentication key authorized for that Operator ID, endpoint role, and scope. Web PKI MAY validate hostname and transport security, but it does not by itself establish NBSR Operator Identity or service authority. A hostname, certificate, Operator ID, key ID, role, scope, signed record, or handshake mismatch MUST fail closed.


Multiple endpoints MAY be authorized independently for region, high availability, load distribution, or failover. Endpoint and key rotation require a new monotonic signed record and controlled rollout. Revoking one compromised endpoint or key MUST NOT automatically revoke the stable Operator ID or unrelated endpoints. Discovery, record verification, handshake binding, health validation, and failover SHOULD be automated and MUST select only currently authorized endpoints.


A duhet federation endpoints me qenë të publikuara në signed operator record?
F65 — Signed Publication of Federation-Facing Endpoints: APPROVED
Every endpoint that accepts federation traffic from other operators MUST be published in a signed Operator Record or signed Federation Endpoint Record. The record MUST bind the Operator ID, endpoint role, hostname or locator, transport profile, port, region or scope, priority or failover role, authorized transport-key or certificate binding, validity window, monotonic generation and sequence, and lifecycle state such as next, active, draining, retired, or revoked.


Only federation-facing endpoints are published. Origin Endpoints, private connectors, internal control-plane addresses, tenant endpoints, private service topology, and other non-public infrastructure MUST remain undisclosed. An operator MAY publish multiple independently authorized endpoints for regional service, active-active operation, active-standby failover, maintenance, or disaster recovery. A next endpoint MUST NOT receive production federation traffic before its activation conditions are satisfied. A draining endpoint MUST NOT accept new sessions but MAY finish or transfer existing work according to policy. A revoked endpoint fails closed immediately.


Changing or rotating an endpoint does not change the Operator ID, Service Identity, ownership, or delegated authority. DNS, load-balancer, directory, or registry results provide candidate addresses only; the candidate MUST match the signed endpoint record and the peer MUST prove possession of the bound key during the authenticated TLS or QUIC handshake. Signed endpoint records MUST be distributed through the applicable registry or directory and, where required by policy, supported by transparency evidence. Publication, readiness checks, health validation, rollout, failover, draining, retirement, and revocation SHOULD be automated and auditable.


Sa gjatë cache-ohet federation metadata?
F66 — Risk-Based Federation Metadata Caching: APPROVED
Every signed federation record MUST declare created_at, not_before, expires_at, maximum_staleness, generation and sequence. Cache lifetime MUST NOT be derived blindly from DNS TTL or HTTP cache headers. DNS and HTTPS caching may bound candidate discovery, while authoritative cache validity is controlled by signed NBSR metadata and local policy.


Stable metadata such as Operator ID, organizational registry binding, durable endpoint roles and historical transparency evidence MAY be cached longer. Dynamic or security-sensitive metadata such as active endpoints, priorities, failover state, supported profiles, endpoint keys and operator status MUST use shorter freshness windows. Revocation, compromise, endpoint removal, ownership change and authority reduction override normal cache lifetime and MUST trigger immediate invalidation or refresh.


Resolvers and federation controllers SHOULD refresh before expiry through primary and alternate trusted sources. After the normal freshness window, metadata MAY be used only in degraded or restricted mode when policy permits. After maximum_staleness, it MUST NOT authorize new sessions, routes, grants or authority changes. Existing sessions are handled separately by severity and revocation policy. Negative discovery results SHOULD use short bounded caching. Cached state MUST bind the exact canonical digest, generation, sequence, scope and accepted checkpoint, not only a hostname or IP address.


Çka ndodh kur directory dhe transparency log nuk pajtohen?
F67 — Safe Reconciliation of Directory and Transparency Conflicts: APPROVED


Directory records and transparency evidence MUST bind to the same object digest, scope, generation, sequence, Operator ID and validity state.


A directory record that is newer but lacks the required inclusion proof or checkpoint MUST be treated as pending. It MUST NOT authorize new authority or new sessions until the required transparency evidence is obtained and validated.


If the transparency log contains a newer accepted version than the directory, the directory MUST be treated as stale and refreshed from alternate trusted sources. Revocation, compromise, ownership transfer and authority reduction recorded in accepted transparency state MUST take effect immediately.


If the directory and transparency log present different digests for the same generation and sequence, the event MUST be treated as possible fork or equivocation. Both versions and their proofs MUST be retained as evidence, the affected scope MUST enter quarantine and new authority MUST fail closed.


A directory MUST NOT override a terminal state, revocation, key compromise, ownership transfer or authority reduction published through accepted transparency state.


A last-known-good record MAY be used only while it remains within its signed freshness and maximum-staleness policy, no known revocation or compromise exists, and the conflict does not affect the relevant authority. It MUST NOT be used to create or expand authority after maximum staleness.


Existing sessions MAY be handled according to incident severity and revocation policy rather than being terminated for every metadata mismatch. Immediate termination is required where the accepted state indicates compromise, revocation or loss of authority.


Automation MUST compare exact digests and versions, retrieve missing proofs, query alternate trusted directories and logs, preserve conflict evidence, alert the relevant operators and avoid arbitrary source selection.


Directory state describes the currently served claim; accepted transparency evidence proves the authorized history. When they disagree, NBSR contains the conflict safely instead of guessing.


A lejohet direct operator-to-operator configuration pa global directory?
F68 — Explicit Bilateral Federation Without a Global Directory: APPROVED
Two operators MAY establish a direct federation relationship without using a global directory. Each operator MUST explicitly configure or import the peer Operator ID, trusted root or authorized federation keys, federation-facing endpoints, endpoint roles and transport profiles, allowed federation scopes, bilateral trust bundle or policy, revocation and refresh sources, validity windows, and maximum-staleness limits.


Direct bilateral federation MAY be used for private enterprise, private consortium, ISP–Sub-ISP relationships, direct peering, isolated deployments, lab and development environments, and controlled bootstrap before public registration. It MUST NOT be interpreted as trust in whatever system responds at a configured IP address. During the authenticated TLS or QUIC handshake, the peer MUST prove possession of the key authorized for the configured Operator ID, role, and scope.


A bilateral relationship MUST NOT automatically grant ownership, service-publication authority, sub-delegation rights, unrestricted access to all services, or authority to represent third-party operators. Direct configuration MAY authorize only the exact scopes and actions declared by the bilateral policy. Signed Operator Records, endpoint records, delegation proofs, trust state, freshness, and revocation state MUST still be verified unless the bilateral trust package explicitly and cryptographically binds the equivalent state.


A global directory MAY later be used as an additional discovery or verification source, but it MUST NOT silently broaden, replace, or override the bilateral trust relationship. Conflicts between bilateral and public federation state MUST be isolated by scope and MUST NOT be resolved through first-seen or arbitrary source selection. Configuration validation, endpoint refresh, key rotation, health checks, and revocation propagation SHOULD be automated, while creation or expansion of bilateral trust requires explicit authorized approval.


A duhet federation të funksionojë edhe në private consortium pa public Internet registry?
F69 — Self-Governed Private Consortium Federation: APPROVED
A private consortium MAY operate a complete NBSR federation without depending on a public Internet registry. The consortium MUST define a private federation scope or identifier, signed governance policy, approved member Operator IDs, a scoped consortium trust bundle, private registry or directory, private transparency logs and witnesses, and explicit membership, suspension, revocation and recovery rules.


Members MAY use self-certifying Operator IDs or identities registered by the consortium authority. The consortium authority MUST NOT automatically become the owner of member names, services or operational authority. Each member retains its own identity, keys, services, ownership and delegated authority; consortium governance determines only which identities, trust roots, logs, witnesses, profiles and policies are accepted inside the private scope.


Private names, delegations, service metadata and transparency evidence MUST remain inside the authorized consortium domain. Public Internet registration is not required unless a service, operator identity or authority is intentionally exposed to public NBSR federation. Transition into public federation requires explicit registration, organization verification, public trust evaluation and publication of only the minimum necessary public evidence; it MUST NOT happen automatically or silently.


Consortium governance SHOULD support threshold control for high-impact actions such as admitting or removing members, replacing trust roots, revoking operators, activating emergency recovery and changing federation policy. No single member SHOULD control the entire consortium trust unless the deployment is explicitly a single-enterprise hierarchy with subordinate operators. Automation SHOULD reconcile membership, distribute trust bundles, validate endpoints and proofs, propagate revocations, remove suspended members and preserve auditable state without bypassing governance approvals.


H. Federation authorization
A duhet source operator me validu krejt delegation chain-in apo vetëm proof-in final?
F70 — Full Authority Validation with Verifiable Chain Proofs: APPROVED
The Source Operator MUST validate the complete authority semantics proving that the Destination Operator is authorized for the requested service. Validation MUST cover the authoritative ownership root, every relevant delegation and sub-delegation, authority class, canonical name and Service ID scope, tenant, region, ports and protocols where applicable, validity windows, monotonic generation and sequence, parent-digest continuity, revocation and terminal state, accepted trust bundles, and required transparency evidence.


A Source Operator MUST NOT trust only an unverified final statement that a Destination Operator is authorized. For efficiency, NBSR MAY use a compact verifiable chain proof instead of transferring and reprocessing every full record on every request. The compact proof MUST bind the exact service and requested scope, identify the accepted authority root, commit to the relevant chain digests, bind to the accepted trust checkpoint and freshness state, and prove that no known revocation invalidates the authority.


The verifier MUST be able to retrieve or reconstruct the full chain when the proof is first observed, the ownership or delegation changes, a trust bundle or checkpoint changes, revocation or conflict evidence appears, or audit and incident response require it. Previously verified chain state MAY be cached only when bound to the exact digests, generation, sequence, scope, checkpoint and freshness policy.


A compact proof MUST NOT hide scope widening, delegation loops, expired or revoked ancestors, broken continuity, authority-class mismatch, stale trust state or equivocation. Missing chain evidence, an unverifiable proof, ambiguity, mismatch or inability to reconstruct the required authority MUST fail closed for new routes, sessions, grants and authority changes.


A duhet destination operator me e validu të njëjtën chain në mënyrë të pavarur?
F71 — Independent Dual-Sided Federation Authorization: APPROVED
Source Operator and Destination Operator MUST validate federation authorization independently. Source validation confirms that traffic is being sent to the authorized Destination Operator and stable Service Identity. Destination validation confirms that the request comes from the declared authenticated Source Operator, that the source is permitted to participate in the applicable federation scope, that the requested name, Service ID, tenant, region, ports and protocols match the accepted authority, and that the destination still holds valid service and delivery authority.


Neither side may rely blindly on the other side’s authorization result. Both operators MAY verify the same compact chain proof, but each MUST verify it against its own accepted trust bundles, checkpoints, revocation state, freshness policy, peer Operator ID binding and local policy. Each side MAY apply additional local restrictions, including subscriber policy at the source and service, capacity, abuse, jurisdiction or compliance policy at the destination.


A route, Route Context or Service Channel becomes authorized only when both sides independently accept it. A positive decision by one operator does not compel the other operator to accept. If either side observes a relevant revocation, newer restriction, authority loss, rollback, equivocation, stale proof or policy mismatch, new route establishment MUST fail closed. Existing sessions are handled separately according to severity and revocation policy.


Authorization evidence SHOULD record the exact service and scope, Source and Destination Operator IDs, chain or proof digest, trust-bundle generation and sequence, accepted checkpoint, policy version, freshness state and decision outcome used by each side. This evidence MUST be privacy-safe, bounded and auditable. Independent dual-sided validation preserves the NBSR rule that Source Edge and Destination Edge each authenticate and authorize the route rather than delegating security to the other operator.


A kërkohet që source dhe destination operatori të kenë të njëjtin trust checkpoint?
F72 — Compatible Trust Checkpoints, Not Exact Equality: APPROVED
Source and Destination Operators do not need to hold an identical checkpoint byte-for-byte at every instant. A bounded propagation delay or version skew MAY exist only when both checkpoints belong to the same accepted log and scope, are connected by a valid append-only consistency proof, contain no conflicting Merkle roots for the same tree size or sequence, remain inside freshness policy, and agree on every trust fact relevant to the attempted route.


Relevant trust facts include the participating Operator IDs, Service Identity and ownership, delegation chain, authorized endpoint keys, revocations, authority reductions, trust-bundle restrictions, and the accepted transparency state needed for authorization. Each operator MUST expose or exchange the log ID, scope, checkpoint sequence or tree size, Merkle root or checkpoint digest, and freshness state needed to evaluate compatibility without revealing unrelated private state.


If the newer checkpoint contains a relevant revocation, compromise state, ownership change, authority reduction, policy restriction, or other security-critical fact that the older operator has not accepted, new sessions, routes, grants, and authority changes MUST fail closed until synchronization succeeds. If the difference is only append-only history unrelated to the current operation, authorization MAY continue within the bounded skew policy. Automation SHOULD retrieve the missing checkpoint and consistency proof, compare relevant trust facts, detect forks or split views, synchronize the lagging operator, and retry safely.


Exact checkpoint equality MAY be required by profile for high-risk actions such as ownership transfer, authority expansion, trust-root change, emergency recovery, or high-authority key activation. In ordinary routing, checkpoints need not be numerically identical, but they MUST be verifiably compatible parts of the same accepted history.


Çka ndodh kur njëri operator ka checkpoint më të ri?
F73 — Secure Checkpoint Synchronization Before Authorization: APPROVED
An operator with an older checkpoint MUST NOT reject a peer solely because the peer has a newer checkpoint. The newer-state operator MUST provide the signed newer checkpoint, a valid consistency proof from the older accepted checkpoint, and the inclusion, freshness, and witness evidence required for trust facts relevant to the attempted route.


The older-state operator MUST independently verify checkpoint signature, scope, append-only continuity, consistency proof, freshness, and absence of fork or split-view evidence before advancing its accepted state. A checkpoint MUST NOT be accepted merely because it is newer.


Authorization MAY proceed within bounded skew only when both checkpoints belong to the same accepted history and the newer checkpoint contains no relevant revocation, compromise, ownership change, authority reduction, key invalidation, or policy restriction. If the newer state contains a security-critical change relevant to the route, new sessions, grants, and authority MUST fail closed until synchronization succeeds.


If the newer checkpoint cannot be verified, the verifier MUST preserve its last-known-good state, quarantine the disputed checkpoint or peer evidence, and avoid arbitrary trust advancement. Existing sessions are handled according to severity: compromise or revocation MAY require immediate termination or reauthentication, while unrelated append-only changes SHOULD NOT collapse an otherwise valid transport session.


Checkpoint exchange and synchronization SHOULD be automated, bounded, rate-limited, resumable, auditable, and safe to retry. An operator that remains beyond the maximum permitted skew MUST enter restricted, quarantine, or fail-closed state and MUST NOT block federation-wide trust advancement indefinitely.


A duhet authorization me u lidh me exact Operator IDs në të dy anët?
F74 — Exact Bilateral Operator Binding with Automated Fast-Path Authorization: APPROVED
Every federation authorization MUST bind the exact Source Operator ID, exact Destination Operator ID, stable Service ID or canonical service name, source and destination endpoint roles, trust scope, delegation or authority evidence, and the exact Route Context or Service Channel. Authorization for one operator pair MUST NOT be replayed or reused for another source, destination, child operator, subsidiary, region-specific Operator ID, or unrelated endpoint authority. Parent-company ownership, consortium membership, shared CDN infrastructure, or shared hosting does not create Operator ID equivalence.


The binding and verification path MUST be automated. Operator IDs, signed endpoint bindings, delegation-chain proofs, trust bundles, checkpoints, revocation state, supported profiles, and policy inputs SHOULD be discovered, validated, refreshed, and cached by the NBSR control plane before ordinary application traffic reaches the fast path. Full chain retrieval and transparency verification MUST NOT be repeated for every connection while the accepted state remains exact, fresh, unrevoked, and policy-compatible.


For ordinary route creation, the Source Edge and Destination Edge SHOULD perform a bounded local lookup against prevalidated state, confirm the exact bilateral Operator IDs, bind them cryptographically to the authenticated Transport Session and service-specific Route Context or Service Channel, apply local policy, and open the authorized flow. The same authenticated Transport Session MAY be reused when the exact operator pair, trust scope, transport profile, and policy permit reuse; each service retains independent authorization, revocation, quota, and audit identity.


The slow path is used for first discovery, cache miss, expiry, key or endpoint rotation, delegation or ownership change, checkpoint refresh, revocation, conflict, recovery, or failover to a different Operator ID. Failover among independently authorized endpoints of the same Operator ID MAY use the existing verified authority. Failover to another Operator ID requires new delegation evidence and a new bilateral authorization decision.


Any mismatch among discovery state, handshake identity, endpoint record, Operator IDs, chain proof, grant, Route Context, or destination admission MUST fail closed. Cached state MUST be bound to exact digests, generations, sequences, scopes, checkpoints, and freshness limits. Revocation or security-critical changes override the cache immediately. The fast path MUST remain deterministic, local, observable, and auditable so normal route creation requires only cached validation and channel establishment rather than global trust verification from zero.


A duhet RouteGrant me përmbajt federation/delegation digest?
F75 — RouteGrant Binding to Exact Federation Authority State: APPROVED
The frozen Core RouteGrant MUST NOT be extended silently. Federation Extension v0.1 MUST bind each route authorization to one exact FederationAuthorizationContext carried or referenced by a versioned federation extension container alongside the immutable RouteGrant. A future RouteGrant version MAY carry this binding directly only after an explicit Core and version decision.


The FederationAuthorizationContext MUST bind, at minimum, the exact Source Operator ID, Destination Operator ID, Service ID, authority or delegation-chain digest, applicable trust-bundle digest or version, relevant checkpoint digest or identifier, policy digest, validity window, generation, sequence, and RouteIntent or route-context identifier. Its canonical digest, the immutable RouteGrant digest, and the selected Core and federation-extension versions MUST be bound into the route-establishment authorization transcript before a Route Context or Service Channel is accepted.


The RouteGrant and FederationAuthorizationContext MUST remain compact and MUST NOT carry the complete delegation chain or transparency proofs on every flow. Their digests reference exact authority state already verified and cached by both operators. Source and Destination Edges MUST validate locally that the referenced state exists, is fresh, is not revoked or superseded, matches the exact operators and service, and permits the requested scope, port, protocol, and action.


If ownership, delegation authority, trust bundle, relevant checkpoint, or policy changes in a security-relevant way, the combined RouteGrant and FederationAuthorizationContext authorization MUST NOT authorize a new route until revalidation or replacement. Non-authority changes such as ordinary endpoint health, traffic weight, or load-balancing state SHOULD NOT invalidate the authorization unnecessarily. Missing state, stale references, digest mismatch, scope mismatch, replay, or collision evidence MUST fail closed.


Heavy verification occurs on the slow path during first discovery, refresh, rotation, conflict, audit, or cache miss. Normal routing uses the immutable RouteGrant plus the compact FederationAuthorizationContext and local cached authority state so both edges can perform a bounded fast-path check without downloading or reconstructing the full evidence chain for every connection.


A duhet grant-i me u invalidu kur delegation ose trust bundle ndryshon?
F76 — Selective Grant Invalidation by Authority Impact: APPROVED
RouteGrant MUST NOT be invalidated for every federation-state update. It MUST be invalidated immediately when a change affects the authority or security basis on which the grant depends, including delegation revocation or expiry, scope reduction, ownership transfer, Source or Destination Operator authority removal, key compromise, trust-root removal, policy restriction, Service Identity authority change, or terminal revocation.


Operational changes that do not alter authority, such as endpoint health, traffic weight, load-balancing priority, origin migration, CDN endpoint rotation within the same authorized Operator ID, or unrelated metadata updates, MUST NOT invalidate the grant unnecessarily.


Each combined RouteGrant and FederationAuthorizationContext authorization MUST bind to an exact dependency set or authority-state digest. The dependency binding resides in FederationAuthorizationContext and MUST NOT add or change a field in the frozen RouteGrant. When new signed state is accepted, automation MUST perform scoped impact analysis to determine which grants remain valid, which require revalidation, and which must be revoked immediately. Invalidation MUST be selective rather than global. New routes MUST NOT use a grant after relevant authority has changed. Existing flows MAY continue, reauthenticate, drain, or terminate according to incident severity and revocation policy. Routine safe updates SHOULD refresh affected grants automatically without service interruption. Emergency revocation overrides normal overlap and cache. Fast-path validation remains local: the edge checks the referenced dependency digest and version rather than rebuilding the full authority chain for every route.


In simple terms: a grant expires when the reason it was allowed changes, not whenever anything around the route changes.


Si bëhet revocation propagation ndërmjet operatorëve?
F77 — Multi-Path Revocation Propagation: APPROVED
Revocation records MUST be signed and MUST identify the exact object type, object digest or key ID, Operator ID and scope, reason, effective time, generation, sequence, and authorizing authority.


The issuing operator MUST propagate revocation immediately through multiple independent paths: authenticated operator-to-operator push, federation directory or registry, trust-bundle or revocation feed, transparency log with an urgent checkpoint, and witness or monitor channels when policy requires them. Operators MUST also perform periodic pull, refresh, and checkpoint comparison; no deployment may rely on push alone.


Critical revocations MUST receive higher priority than normal updates and MAY bypass ordinary batching. A receiving operator MUST verify signer authority, signature, monotonic versioning, freshness, scope, and conflict state before storing the revocation durably and applying it locally. It MUST invalidate only the grants, sessions, cached state, keys, delegations, endpoints, services, or authorities that depend on the revoked object.


Critical revocations SHOULD produce signed acknowledgements or receipts so the issuer can measure propagation. Missing acknowledgement does not make a valid revocation optional; the unreachable or lagging peer enters warning, restricted, quarantine, or fail-closed behavior according to policy.


Propagation MUST be idempotent, replay-safe, resumable, rate-bounded, auditable, privacy-safe, and safe to retry. If the directory is unavailable, direct peer push and transparency or witness channels continue. If a peer is offline, it MUST retrieve the revocation through refresh or checkpoint reconciliation before resuming affected authority. A known critical revocation MUST NOT be ignored because of cache lifetime, checkpoint skew, stale metadata, or last-known-good state.


In simple terms: revocation is not a message sent once and hoped to arrive; it is published through several verifiable paths, stored durably, and enforced automatically by every operator.


A duhet source operator me ditë owner-in final të service-it apo vetëm destination operatorin?
F78 — Verified Final Ownership with Minimal Operational Disclosure: APPROVED
Source Operator-i duhet ta verifikojë kriptografikisht që Service Identity ka owner/issuer authority valide dhe që Destination Operator-i është autorizuar prej asaj authority.


Core v0.1 basis:
• Signed ServiceRecord është prova aktuale normative e publication authority.
• ServiceRecord lidhet me owner_key_id, service_id, destination_operator_id, destination edges, ports, transport, validity dhe revocation_ref.
• COSE Sign1 protected kid duhet të përputhet byte-for-byte me owner_key_id.
• RouteIntent dhe RouteGrant lidhen me service_id, exact Source/Destination Operator IDs, accepted ServiceRecord sequence, policy hash, validity dhe route context.
• Fast path mund të përdorë signed ServiceRecord, accepted record sequence dhe cached trust/revocation state pa rindërtuar ownership evidence për çdo connection.


WP8 / Core v0.2 extension direction:
• Global ownership registry, signed ownership/delegation-chain objects, compact chain proof, transparency-bound ownership digest dhe federation-wide trust distribution ende nuk ekzistojnë në Core v0.1.
• Këto duhet të futen si objekte ose extensions të versionuara të WP8/Core v0.2, jo si fields të shtuara në heshtje në frozen Core v0.1 schemas.
• Destination Operator statement vetëm nuk mjafton; authority duhet të lidhet deri te një owner/issuer root i pranuar.


Minimal disclosure:
• Për normal route establishment, Source Operator-it i mjaftojnë Service ID, Destination Operator ID, signed ServiceRecord/accepted sequence dhe cached verified authority state.
• Emri legal, kontaktet, internal tenant identity, private organization metadata, origin dhe topology details nuk bartën në fast path.
• Legal owner identity mund të jetë e disponueshme vetëm për registry governance, dispute resolution, abuse handling, compliance dhe authorized audit.
• Private consortium/enterprise profiles mund të përdorin private ose pseudonymous owner records brenda trust domain-it të tyre.
• Mungesa, expiry, revocation, transfer ose conflict i owner/issuer authority dështon closed për route të re.


Përmbledhje: Source Operator-i duhet ta dijë dhe verifikojë kriptografikisht se ekziston authority valide mbi Service Identity dhe se Destination Operator-i është autorizuar prej saj; fast path përdor signed ServiceRecord dhe cached proof state, jo identitetin legal ose krejt dosjen e owner-it.


I. Privacy
A duhet global directory me zbulu cilat services i hoston secili operator?
F79 — Privacy-Preserving Federation Directory Without Global Service Enumeration: APPROVED
Global directory MUST NOT expose a complete catalogue of the services hosted, delivered, or represented by each operator. It MUST NOT permit unrestricted bulk listing of public or private services, tenants, internal names, origins, connectors, customer relationships, or topology.


Federation discovery SHOULD use exact-name, exact-Service-ID, or otherwise narrowly scoped queries rather than operator-wide enumeration. A successful response MAY disclose only the minimum authority and routing metadata required for authorization, including the exact matching Service ID or privacy-preserving commitment, authorized Destination Operator ID, signed federation-endpoint references, delegation or trust-proof references, validity, freshness, and revocation state.


A service MAY be globally discoverable only when the owner has explicitly classified and published it as public. Restricted, private, enterprise, and consortium services MUST require an authenticated requester, an authorized scope, an exact or capability-bound query, and applicable policy approval. Prefix search, wildcard enumeration, reverse listing by Operator ID, bulk export, and scraping-friendly interfaces MUST be prohibited or tightly policy-controlled, rate-bounded, and audited.


Negative responses for private or restricted services SHOULD avoid revealing whether the service exists and MAY use an indistinguishable result such as not-authorized-or-not-found. Directory responses SHOULD use authenticated encrypted transport, scoped partitions, privacy-preserving commitments, and role-specific access controls. Advanced profiles MAY add blinded or private exact-match lookup mechanisms after an explicit versioned design decision.


Transparency evidence MAY prove that a valid authority record or commitment exists without exposing the complete service metadata. Operators, monitors, and governance roles MAY receive additional information only when explicitly authorized and MUST NOT obtain public enumeration rights merely because they participate in federation.


Automation MUST classify each service according to owner policy, publish only the permitted minimum, enforce query authorization and rate limits, detect enumeration attempts, and preserve auditable evidence without recording subscriber-sensitive lookup history beyond approved retention. In simple terms: the directory may explain how to reach and verify one requested service, but it must not reveal everything an operator hosts.


A duhet transparency proofs me qenë name-specific apo privacy-preserving?


F80 — Scope-Aware Transparency Proofs with Privacy-Preserving Commitments: APPROVED
Public names and public services MAY use name-specific transparency proofs when their owner has explicitly classified the record as public and public verification requires the canonical name or Service ID.


Private, enterprise, consortium, restricted, or hidden services MUST NOT publish the clear-text name or Service ID in a public transparency log. Their trust-critical state MUST instead use a canonical, domain-separated privacy-preserving commitment bound to the object class, authorized scope or partition, generation, sequence, validity or revocation state, and the applicable signed checkpoint.


The commitment MUST allow an authorized party to prove later that the exact signed object existed in the accepted log state, was ordered correctly, and was not silently modified, deleted, or substituted. Because predictable names are vulnerable to offline guessing, private commitments SHOULD use an appropriate salt, keyed commitment, tenant-specific domain separation, or another profile-approved anti-enumeration mechanism. Opening secrets MUST remain outside the public log and be disclosed only to authorized verifiers.


Proof responses MUST reveal only the information the requester is authorized to receive. Public and private records MUST remain separated by scope and visibility policy. Revocation MAY be published against the same commitment without revealing the protected name. Operators and federation controllers verify and cache the inclusion and checkpoint evidence; ordinary clients MUST NOT retrieve a fresh transparency proof for every connection.


Automation MUST classify records as public, restricted, private, or hidden; select clear name-specific proof only for authorized public records; select privacy-preserving commitments for protected records; enforce scope filtering; and fail closed when classification or disclosure authority is missing or ambiguous.


In simple terms: a public service may be proven by name, while a private service must be proven without publishing its name to the world.
A mundet një operator me query një delegation pa zbulu subscriber-in?
F81 — Subscriber-Blind Delegation Discovery: APPROVED
Source Operator MUST query delegation and authority state using its authenticated Operator Identity, not the subscriber’s real identity. The normal federation query MAY contain only the minimum authorization context: canonical name or privacy-preserving lookup token, Service ID when known, Source Operator ID, requested federation scope, and region, port, protocol, service class, nonce, or freshness data only where required for exact scope validation and replay resistance.


The query MUST NOT carry the subscriber’s name, email, account or customer identifier, device identity, application credentials, cookies, browsing history, or home or public IP unless a separately approved protocol requirement proves that one exact field is indispensable. The directory, Destination Operator, transparency service, and witnesses may learn which authenticated operator requests authority for which service scope, but MUST NOT automatically learn which person or device behind that operator initiated the request.


The Source Operator MAY retain a local subscriber-to-route correlation for subscriber policy, billing, abuse response, and troubleshooting. That local correlation MUST remain inside the Source Operator’s controlled domain and MUST NOT be transmitted into federation as part of routine discovery, proof retrieval, route authorization, or audit exchange.


Public services MAY use exact-name or exact-Service-ID lookups. Private, consortium, enterprise, or restricted services MUST use an authenticated scoped request, opaque service handle, keyed or salted commitment, pre-issued capability, or another privacy-preserving exact-match mechanism. When disclosure policy requires hiding service existence, not-found and not-authorized responses SHOULD be indistinguishable. Query handles and correlation identifiers SHOULD be short-lived, minimally linkable, rate-bounded, replay-resistant, and auditable.


Rate limiting and abuse controls SHOULD bind primarily to the authenticated Source Operator ID and privacy-safe query context rather than exposing subscriber identity. Subscriber identity MAY be disclosed only through a separate authorized and auditable incident, abuse, legal, or compliance process; it MUST NOT be a prerequisite for ordinary route establishment.


In simple terms: federation needs to know which operator requests which service authority, not which person behind the router clicked the service.


A duhet subscriber identity me mbet pseudonymous për destination operatorin?
F82 — Scoped Pseudonymous Subscriber Identity: APPROVED
Destination Operator-i MUST NOT receive the subscriber’s real identity by default. Source Operator-i MAY present a pseudonymous subscriber handle only when subscriber-level context is required by an explicit service, quota, entitlement, abuse, enterprise-access, or continuity policy.


The pseudonym MUST be scoped to the exact Source and Destination Operator IDs and SHOULD also be service-specific or privacy-scope-specific. It MUST be short-lived or rotatable, cryptographically authenticated, bound to the applicable Service ID, validity window, Route Context or Service Channel, policy and revocation state, and MUST NOT be replayable at another operator, service, or scope.


NBSR MUST NOT use one global stable subscriber identifier across federation. For ordinary public-service routing, the authenticated Source Operator ID, authorized service context, quota posture and privacy-safe abuse posture SHOULD be sufficient without any subscriber identifier. Destination Operator-i MUST NOT be able to derive the subscriber’s name, email, address, customer ID, device identity, or activity at other operators from the pseudonym.


Source Operator-i retains any real-identity mapping locally under its own policy, access controls and retention rules. That mapping MUST NOT be shared during normal route establishment. Identity disclosure requires a separate authorized and auditable process with a valid legal or policy basis, minimum-necessary disclosure, scoped recipients and bounded retention.


Pseudonym rotation SHOULD preserve bounded valid session continuity when policy permits, without making separate sessions or services globally linkable. Audit systems MUST avoid converting scoped pseudonyms into a federation-wide tracking identifier. Privacy-safe abuse evidence MAY be exchanged before real identity is disclosed.


In simple terms: Destination Operator-i may know that the same scoped pseudonymous subscriber is acting inside one authorized context, but not who that person is in real life or what they do elsewhere.


Cilat audit fields mund të ndahen ndërmjet operatorëve?
F83 — Minimum Necessary Inter-Operator Audit Evidence: APPROVED
Operators MAY share only the minimum audit evidence required to prove an authorization decision, incident, revocation, propagation event, or policy outcome. Shareable fields MAY include the exact Source and Destination Operator IDs; a public Service ID or privacy-safe service commitment; Route Context, RouteGrant, event, or evidence digest; decision outcome and bounded reason code; policy version or digest; delegation or authority-proof digest; trust-bundle scope, generation, sequence, and digest; transparency-log ID and checkpoint digest; revocation reference; federation endpoint role or identifier without Origin Endpoint disclosure; protocol or port class where required; reduced-precision event time; incident-scoped pseudonymous handle; signed receipt or acknowledgement; propagation status; provenance; and verifier result.


Routine inter-operator audit exchange MUST NOT disclose the real subscriber identity, client IP or device identity, cookies, credentials, reusable tokens, packet payload, application content, private Origin Endpoint, internal topology, unnecessary tenant metadata, complete session or browsing history, private keys, or operational secrets. Every disclosed field MUST have a declared purpose and MUST be limited to the exact incident, route, service, trust scope, and retention window that require it.


Audit packages MUST be authenticated, integrity-protected, tamper-evident, replay-resistant, access-controlled, retention-bounded, and auditable. Timestamps, identifiers, and correlation data MUST be minimized or coarsened when exact values are unnecessary. Abuse investigations SHOULD use incident-specific pseudonyms and evidence digests rather than globally stable subscriber identifiers. Routine privacy filtering, field selection, signing, receipt handling, and retention enforcement SHOULD be automated before evidence leaves an operator boundary.


More detailed evidence MAY be disclosed only through a separate authorized process with explicit purpose, minimum-necessary selection, approval and accountability appropriate to the risk. Audit evidence MUST NOT independently create ownership, delegation, service authority, or route authorization. Missing nonessential private fields MUST NOT invalidate an otherwise verifiable authorization or incident record.


In simple terms: operators share proof of what was authorized or what failed and why, not the person’s identity, traffic content, or private infrastructure map.


A duhet abuse evidence me qenë unlinkable mes sessions?
F84 — Session-Unlinkable Abuse Evidence with Controlled Escalation: APPROVED
Normal abuse evidence MUST NOT use a globally stable or permanent subscriber identifier. Each session or incident SHOULD use a session-scoped handle, incident-scoped pseudonym, short-lived evidence identifier, or privacy-safe commitment so Destination Operators cannot automatically correlate the same subscriber across services, operators, or long periods.


The Source Operator MAY retain local correlation needed for rate limiting, fraud detection, repeated-abuse handling, contractual enforcement, and legal obligations. A Destination Operator requesting escalation MUST provide signed, bounded evidence such as incident ID, service and scope, reduced-precision timestamps, event digests, reason codes, severity, and proof that the evidence is valid rather than fabricated.


The Source Operator performs correlation locally and MAY answer with privacy-preserving results such as same source confirmed, different sources, threshold exceeded, or subscriber action taken without revealing real identity. Repeated incidents MAY be linked through a temporary abuse-case pseudonym limited to that exact case and automatically expired when the case closes.


Cross-session linking is permitted only for a declared purpose, sufficient evidence threshold, bounded scope and retention, and auditable authorization. Global subscriber reputation identifiers are forbidden by default. Abuse evidence MUST NOT be repurposed for tracking, advertising, profiling, or service enumeration. Real-identity disclosure requires a separate legal or policy basis, explicit authorization, minimum-necessary disclosure, and bounded retention.


Automation SHOULD generate unlinkable handles, validate evidence, perform local correlation, enforce thresholds, expire temporary case identifiers, and delete mappings according to retention policy.


In simple terms: sessions remain unlinkable to the destination by default, while the Source Operator can still identify repeated abuse without exporting the subscriber’s identity or full history.


Si parandalohet federation directory me u shndërru në katalog global të targets?
F85 — Anti-Enumeration Federation Directory: APPROVED
The federation directory MUST operate as an exact-match authorization and discovery service, not as a public search engine or global catalogue of targets.


The directory MUST NOT provide listing by Operator ID, prefix search, wildcard search, bulk export, reverse lookup of hosted services, tenant browsing, Service ID enumeration, or unrestricted registry download. Public lookup MUST accept only an exact canonical name or exact privacy-safe service handle. Restricted and private services require an authenticated requester and an authorized scope before any metadata is returned.


When privacy policy requires existence hiding, responses for a nonexistent service, a private service, and an unauthorized requester MUST be indistinguishable. The directory MUST enforce rate limits by Operator ID and trust level, bounded query budgets, anomaly detection, short negative caching, bounded response sizes, and privacy-safe query logging. Proof-of-work or additional challenges MAY be activated during abuse but MUST NOT create authority or expose private records.


Transparency logs MUST publish only trust-critical records or privacy-preserving commitments, not a browsable operational catalogue. Public services MAY be discoverable through exact-name resolution with minimum necessary metadata. Private, enterprise, and consortium records MUST remain in access-controlled partitions without global indexes. Trusted operators MAY receive higher operational quotas but MUST NOT receive unrestricted catalogue access.


Systematic probing, scraping, distributed enumeration, reverse mapping, and service-list extraction MUST be detected and contained at federation level. Automation SHOULD classify queries, apply disclosure policy, enforce quotas and anomaly response, preserve auditable evidence, and avoid manual intervention during normal operation.


In simple terms: the directory answers whether an exact requested service exists and is authorized for that requester; it does not reveal a list of Internet targets.


J. Revocation dhe failure behavior
Cilat objekte mund të revokohen: operator, key, delegation, service, bundle apo checkpoint?
F86 — Typed Revocation Across All Trust-Critical Object Classes: APPROVED
Operator ID revocation removes the operator from the applicable federation scope and blocks new sessions, grants, endpoints, keys, and delegated authority that depend on that identity. Key revocation targets the exact key ID and purpose; it does not automatically revoke the stable Operator ID or unrelated keys, and compromise is terminal for that key ID. Delegation revocation targets the exact delegation digest and invalidates dependent child delegations while preserving independent scopes. Service Identity or ServiceRecord-authority revocation blocks publication and route authorization only for the affected service or scope.


A trust bundle is normally superseded by a newer signed bundle, but an exact bundle digest or version MAY also be marked revoked or disqualified for its declared scope. A transparency checkpoint is not deleted from history; it MAY be marked invalid, compromised, equivocated, or no-longer-trusted, while the signed checkpoint and conflict evidence remain available for audit and fork detection. Federation endpoints, Origin Connectors, RouteGrants, routes, leases, Route Contexts, and Service Channels MUST also be independently revocable without unnecessarily revoking the parent Operator ID or Service Identity.


Every revocation MUST identify the target type, exact target digest or ID, issuer authority, scope, reason, effective time, generation, sequence, and enforcement mode such as deny-new-use or terminate-active-use. Revocation MUST apply through the actual dependency graph: parent-authority revocation invalidates dependent child authority, while child revocation does not invalidate the parent. Revoked objects remain in historical evidence and MUST NOT be resurrected; recovery or replacement creates a new object, key, version, bundle, or generation.


Automation MUST calculate scoped impact, invalidate only affected grants, sessions, caches, delegations, endpoints, and services, propagate the revocation through the approved federation channels, and preserve auditable evidence. Emergency critical revocation overrides ordinary overlap, caching, and last-known-good behavior. Ambiguous target type, digest, scope, issuer authority, or dependency state MUST fail closed.


In simple terms: every component that creates trust or authority must be stoppable on its own; NBSR cuts the compromised branch rather than unnecessarily cutting down the whole tree.


A është revocation terminal?
F87 — Reason-Dependent Terminal Revocation: APPROVED
Revocation ndahet në terminal dhe non-terminal sipas reason-it, target class-it dhe security impact-it.


Terminal revocation është permanent për exact target ID ose digest dhe përdoret për key compromise, Operator ID retirement ose permanent removal, fraud ose identity impersonation, ownership loss ose unauthorized ownership claim, terminal delegation abuse, Service Identity retirement, endpoint ose connector compromise kur identifier-i nuk duhet ripërdorur, dhe exact checkpoint ose record version të provuar si fork/equivocation ose të pabesueshëm.


Për terminal revocation, expires_at MUST të mungojë, target ID nuk mund të ripërdoret, old generation nuk mund të rikthehet dhe recovery krijon key, object, generation ose identity të re. Tombstone state ruhet për rollback dhe resurrection prevention.


Non-terminal ose time-bounded revocation përdoret për temporary suspension, maintenance, administrative restriction, policy violation që mund të rishqyrtohet, route ose lease cancellation, session termination, temporary federation restriction dhe delegation termination pa compromise permanent. Kjo revocation MAY të ketë expires_at, por expiry nuk e ringjall automatikisht objektin e vjetër; kërkohet revalidation ose objekt/version i ri dhe local policy mund ta mbajë target-in restricted më gjatë.


Revocation record MUST ta deklarojë reason-in dhe terminality semantics në mënyrë deterministic. Key compromise është gjithmonë terminal. Një authority më e ulët nuk mund ta ulë terminal revocation në temporary. Një false positive nuk e ringjall target-in terminal; lëshohet replacement me identifier ose generation të re. Existing sessions trajtohen sipas deny-new-use ose terminate-active-use. Automation MAY të skadojë revocations time-bounded, por MUST NOT të fshijë terminal tombstones. Ambiguity rreth terminality dështon closed për authority të re dhe historical evidence ruhet edhe pasi një temporary revocation skadon.


Në simple terms: temporary problem mund të kalojë; compromised identity nuk kthehet nga varri — zëvendësohet me identitet ose version të ri.


Sa gjatë ruhet terminal revocation state?
F88 — Permanent Terminal Tombstones with Tiered Retention: APPROVED
Terminal revocation state MUST NOT expire automatically. For every exact target ID or canonical digest, the system MUST retain a permanent terminal tombstone sufficient to prevent reuse, rollback, or resurrection.


The tombstone MUST retain at minimum the target type, exact target ID or digest, terminal status and reason, effective time, authorizing authority, applicable scope, highest accepted generation and sequence, revocation-record digest, and required transparency or checkpoint reference.


Verifiers MUST consult terminal state before accepting an old key, reused identifier, lower generation, resurrected delegation, retired Operator ID, revoked service authority, or restored state after rollback. Cache eviction, restart, failover, database compaction, disaster recovery, or node replacement MUST NOT erase the highest accepted terminal state.


Full historical objects, proof chains, and incident evidence MAY move to archival, transparency-history, or cold forensic storage according to governance and legal retention policy. The compact non-resurrection tombstone MUST remain in active verification state or always be recoverable from accepted durable trust evidence.


Fresh installations and recovered nodes MUST synchronize terminal state from trusted sources such as current trust bundles, revocation feeds, transparency logs, replicated quorum state, or signed bootstrap snapshots before authorizing affected lineages.


Storage MAY use tiers: hot storage for active or frequently checked revocations, warm storage for compact historical tombstones, and cold storage for complete records and forensic evidence. Compaction is permitted only when exact target binding, terminality, monotonic watermark, non-reuse guarantee, and audit trace remain intact.


Privacy-sensitive terminal state SHOULD use scoped digests or commitments when full identity is unnecessary for verification. No garbage-collection rule may make a terminally revoked identifier valid again. If terminal state cannot be reconstructed after recovery, new authority for the affected lineage MUST fail closed.


In simple terms: full records may be archived, but the proof that an identity is permanently dead must be retained forever.


Çka ndodh nëse transparency log ose federation directory nuk është i qasshëm?
F89 — Bounded Degraded Operation During Directory or Transparency Outage: APPROVED
Directory dhe transparency log duhet të kenë disa replicas ose providers, alternate trusted endpoints, cached checkpoints dhe proofs, direct operator-to-operator synchronization, si dhe witness ose mirror channels. Kur një source nuk është i qasshëm, sistemi duhet automatikisht të provojë burimet alternative para se të kalojë në degraded mode.


Gjatë outage-it mund të përdoret vetëm state i verifikuar më herët që është ende brenda validity dhe maximum-staleness, lidhet me exact digest, generation, sequence dhe checkpoint, nuk ka revocation, compromise ose conflict të njohur dhe nuk kërkon authority expansion. Kjo mund të lejojë vazhdimin e sessions ekzistuese, session reuse me të njëjtin exact operator pair dhe authority state, përdorimin e cached Operator Records dhe endpoint records, si dhe route të reja vetëm për services tashmë të njohura kur profile policy e lejon.


Gjatë outage-it duhet të dështojnë closed ownership transfer, authority expansion, delegation ose sub-delegation e re, trust-root change, key activation që kërkon transparency evidence, operator registration i ri, recovery generation, acceptance e checkpoint-it të panjohur, first-time service discovery pa proof të mjaftueshëm dhe çdo përdorim i state-it pas maximum-staleness.


Known revocation ose compromise mbetet aktiv edhe kur directory ose log është down. Last-known-good nuk mund ta tejkalojë një revocation të njohur dhe outage nuk e kthen bootstrap metadata në authority. Nëse directory mungon por transparency evidence është valide dhe endpoint-i gjendet nga një burim tjetër i autorizuar, route mund të vazhdojë sipas policy-së. Nëse transparency log mungon por operatori ka proof dhe checkpoint të freskët të verifikuar më herët, mund të përdoret bounded cached state. Nëse directory dhe transparency state kundërshtohen ose freshness nuk provohet, new authorization dështon closed.


Existing sessions nuk ndërpriten automatikisht vetëm për outage; ndërpriten kur expiry, revocation, compromise ose risk policy e kërkon. Degraded mode duhet të jetë time-bounded, scope-bounded, observable, auditable dhe pa authority expansion. Pas rikthimit, automation duhet të sinkronizojë versionet, të verifikojë consistency, të marrë revocations e humbura, të ri-autorizojë state-in e përdorur gjatë outage-it dhe të mbyllë ose reautentikojë routes që nuk janë më valide.


Në përmbledhje: kur directory ose log bie, NBSR mund të ecë përkohësisht me state të freskët të verifikuar më herët, por nuk guxon të krijojë trust ose authority të re në errësirë.


A lejohet përdorimi i last-known-good state?
F90 — Bounded Last-Known-Good State: APPROVED
Last-known-good state MAY be used only as a bounded continuity mechanism for state that was previously fully verified, remains bound to the exact digest, generation, sequence, scope and accepted checkpoint, is still within maximum_staleness, and has no known revocation, compromise, fork, conflict, authority reduction or scope change.


It MAY temporarily support existing sessions, session reuse for the same exact operator pair and trust scope, previously authorized routes, endpoint failover within the same Operator ID and authority, and local policy, quota and abuse enforcement. It MUST NOT authorize ownership transfer, authority expansion, new delegation, new key activation, trust-root change, operator registration, recovery generation, first-time service discovery, or any state beyond maximum_staleness.


Last-known-good state MUST NOT override a known revocation, terminal tombstone, newer restriction, key compromise, authority reduction or conflicting checkpoint. Every use MUST be time-bounded, scope-bounded, risk-classified, observable and auditable, including the exact state used, reason fresh sources were unavailable, duration, operations allowed and operations denied.


Automation MUST continue refresh attempts through alternate trusted sources, stop using the state when its bound expires, revalidate immediately when sources recover, and close, reauthenticate or replace routes that are no longer valid. A fresh installation or node without durable monotonic state MUST NOT treat the first state it observes as last-known-good. High-risk profiles MAY prohibit last-known-good use for selected operations.


In simple terms: last-known-good may keep alive trust that was already proven; it may not invent new trust while the system cannot verify what changed.


Për sa kohë?
F91 — Risk-Based Maximum Duration for Last-Known-Good State: APPROVED
Last-known-good duration MUST NOT use one universal value. Every trust bundle, federation record, deployment profile, or continuity policy MUST declare the normal freshness window, degraded-mode window, absolute maximum_staleness, and the operations permitted during each phase.


The effective last-known-good limit is the strictest applicable bound among the record’s maximum_staleness, key or delegation validity, grant or service-authority expiry, session lifetime, deployment outage policy, and the most restrictive policy of the participating operators. High-risk state such as revocation, compromise, operator status, endpoint keys, delegation authority, and trust-root changes MUST use shorter limits than stable identity bindings, immutable historical proofs, ownership history, or terminal tombstones.


New operations MUST use stricter limits than continuation of existing sessions. Ownership transfer, authority expansion, recovery, high-authority key activation, trust-root change, and equivalent high-risk actions have zero last-known-good allowance. Existing sessions MAY continue only while their own validity, authority, and severity policy permit it and no known revocation or compromise applies.


As the outage grows older, the system MUST progressively restrict operation: normal cached operation, restricted degraded mode, existing sessions only, then fail closed. Operators and administrators MUST NOT extend last-known-good indefinitely. Any emergency extension, if a profile permits it, MUST be explicit, bounded, high-assurance approved, auditable, and incapable of expanding authority.


Exact durations remain profile-specific and MUST be established through WP8 testing and conformance evidence rather than becoming invented universal constants. Automation MUST expose the remaining time, warn before expiry, attempt refresh through alternate trusted sources, and stop use automatically when the hard bound is reached.


In simple terms: last-known-good lasts only while the state remains provably safe under a predeclared limit; it never lasts forever and never creates new authority.


Cilat operacione lejohen gjatë outage dhe cilat dështojnë closed?
F92 — Explicit Outage Operation Matrix: APPROVED
Gjatë outage-it, operacionet MUST klasifikohen në mënyrë deterministic sipas riskut dhe freshness state-it.


Kur cached state mbetet brenda normal freshness window, sistemi MAY vazhdojë sessions ekzistuese, krijimin e Route Contexts ose Service Channels për services të verifikuara më herët, session reuse për të njëjtin exact Source/Destination Operator pair, endpoint failover brenda të njëjtit Operator ID dhe authority, local policy, quota dhe abuse enforcement, bounded refresh/reconciliation, si dhe përdorimin e grants që ende lidhen me authority state valide.


Kur normal freshness ka kaluar por state-i mbetet brenda maximum_staleness, sistemi MAY hyjë vetëm në restricted degraded mode. Në këtë fazë MAY lejohen sessions ekzistuese, reauthentication ose channel renewal mbi authority ekzistuese, routes të reja vetëm për services të njohura më herët kur profile policy e lejon, endpoint failover vetëm te endpoint-e të autorizuara më herët, session reuse pa ndryshim të operatorit, service scope-it ose authority, dhe emergency containment që nuk zgjeron authority. Këto operacione MUST kenë limits më të rrepta, countdown deri te hard expiry dhe audit të plotë.


Gjatë outage-it MAY lejohen vetëm për containment ose recovery preparation: revokimi i state-it të komprometuar që tashmë mund të verifikohet, quarantine ose mbyllja e routes, ndalimi i sessions të reja, export i evidence, health checks, përgatitja e replacement key ose trust bundle, retrieval nga alternate trusted sources, incident notification dhe audit. Përgatitja MAY automatizohet, por activation-i i authority së re pret proof-in dhe approval-in e kërkuar.


Gjatë outage-it MUST të dështojnë closed: first-time Operator ID trust; first-time service discovery pa proof; ownership registration ose transfer; authority expansion; delegation ose sub-delegation e re; trust-root addition, removal ose replacement; high-authority key activation; recovery-generation activation; operator admission në federation; federation-policy expansion; checkpoint i panjohur ose unverifiable; RouteGrant që lidhet me authority të re ose të ndryshuar; failover te një Operator ID tjetër pa authorization të re; përdorimi pas maximum_staleness; dhe çdo override i revocation, compromise ose terminal tombstone.


Outage MUST NOT ta kthejë availability në authority. Operacioni me riskun më të lartë përdor policy-n më të rreptë. Një operation që nuk klasifikohet qartë MUST fail closed. Known revocation dhe compromise kanë përparësi ndaj çdo continuity rule. Automation MUST ta zbatojë matricën në mënyrë deterministic, të regjistrojë operation class, policy, state digest dhe remaining validity, dhe të revalidojë çdo degraded operation pasi burimet të rikthehen.


Në simple terms: gjatë outage-it NBSR mund të vazhdojë vetëm atë që e ka provuar tashmë dhe mund ta kufizojë ose ndalojë rrezikun; nuk mund të krijojë trust, ownership ose authority të re.


A ndërpriten sessions ekzistuese apo vetëm ndalohen sessions të reja?
F93 — Severity-Based Existing-Session Enforcement: APPROVED
Sessions ekzistuese MUST be handled according to the verified cause, severity, affected dependency, and explicit revocation enforcement mode rather than by one universal rule.


Continue is permitted when the problem is only directory or transparency-log outage, cached authority remains within approved freshness or maximum-staleness, no relevant revocation, compromise, conflict, authority loss, or policy change is known, and the session is already authenticated and authorized. In this state, existing traffic MAY continue while creation of new sessions, Route Contexts, Service Channels, or Application Streams is restricted according to policy.


Restricted continuation applies to stale state, unresolved metadata mismatch, propagation delay, or bounded checkpoint skew without evidence of compromise. The session MAY continue temporarily, but MUST NOT expand scope, add services, ports, or protocols, open unauthorized new channels or flows, or fail over to another Operator ID. Stricter quota, a short reauthentication deadline, and enhanced audit MAY apply.


Drain and reauthenticate applies to relevant but non-emergency key or endpoint rotation, delegation or trust-bundle transition, policy-driven reauthentication, or migration to newly accepted authority state. The affected session or channel MUST stop accepting new flows and close within a bounded drain timeout. Existing TCP streams MAY drain according to policy. UDP flow state MUST stop or be rebuilt according to its service profile because it does not have identical connection-draining semantics.


Immediate termination is required when the active transport or session key is compromised; the relevant Source or Destination Operator ID is revoked; Service Identity, delegation, RouteGrant, endpoint, or policy authority is revoked with terminate-active-use; ownership or authority is lost; a forged, replayed, or invalid authorization is proven; or split-view or equivocation evidence directly invalidates the session authority.


Every revocation or emergency action SHOULD declare one deterministic enforcement mode: deny-new-use, reauthenticate, drain, or terminate-active-use. Outage without compromise defaults to preserving already valid sessions. Known key compromise or terminal authority loss defaults to terminating affected active use.


Transport Session and service-specific state MUST be evaluated separately. Compromise of the transport key MAY require termination of the whole Transport Session. Revocation of one service, delegation, Route Context, TCP stream set, or UDP flow set MUST terminate only the dependent channels or flows when unrelated services in the same authenticated tunnel remain independently valid. A shared Transport Session MAY carry both TCP and UDP, while each service-bound stream or flow retains independent authorization, revocation, quota, and audit state.


Automation MUST perform dependency-impact analysis and select the narrowest action that removes the risk, while preserving signed, privacy-safe audit evidence. After fresh state becomes available, any surviving session MUST be revalidated before opening new authority, channels, streams, or flows.


In simple terms: an ordinary outage blocks risky new work; a compromise closes the affected active work, and NBSR should terminate only the compromised dependency rather than the whole tunnel when safe isolation is possible.


Si trajtohet një operator i komprometuar komplet?
F94 — Full Operator Compromise Containment and Recovery: APPROVED
A full operator compromise exists when evidence indicates that an attacker may control a broad or unknown portion of the operator’s operational signing keys, federation endpoints, trust-bundle signing, directory publication, transparency checkpoint publication, deployment control plane, IAM or administrative authority, revocation channels, or replicated trust state.


The affected Operator ID MUST enter one canonical top-level lifecycle state from F114. A suspected compromise is represented as QUARANTINED with reason SUSPECTED_COMPROMISE while evidence is preserved and classified. Recovery uses top-level RECOVERY with the exact recovery_stage RECOVERY_PENDING, RECOVERY_VERIFIED, or REENTRY_RESTRICTED. RESTORED is a transition result, not a persistent wire state; it leads to REENTRY_RESTRICTED or ACTIVE only after the required checks. Terminal revocation uses TERMINALLY_REVOKED. In quarantine, new sessions, grants, delegations, endpoint authority, trust advancement, and new federation actions from that operator MUST fail closed. Affected operational keys, endpoints, cached grants, routes, Route Contexts, and Service Channels MUST be invalidated according to their dependency and severity. Existing sessions MUST follow F93 rather than being handled by one undifferentiated global shutdown rule.


A full compromise of a delivery operator MUST NOT automatically revoke customer ownership, stable Service IDs, parent or sibling Operator IDs, unrelated services, or valid authority held independently by other operators. Multi-operator, multi-CDN, standby, or owner-controlled emergency delivery SHOULD remove the compromised operator from traffic allocation and fail over only to operators that were already authorized. Failover MUST preserve the same name and Service ID and MUST NOT create new authority during the incident.


Recovery MUST use the strongest still-trusted authority available, such as an uncompromised offline root, pre-registered threshold trustees, break-glass recovery under F59, registry or governance recovery, and required transparency and witness evidence. Recovery MUST create a new generation and new purpose-bound operational keys, endpoint records, trust bundle, checkpoint authority where needed, and reissued service or delegation records only after full revalidation. Old compromised keys, bundles, endpoints, grants, and authority state MUST be revoked and MUST NOT be resurrected.


If the offline root or stable identity authority is compromised, ordinary key rotation is insufficient. The operator MUST enter high-assurance identity recovery. The old root and dependent authority MUST be revoked. Recovery creates a new identity generation when continuity can be proven; otherwise a new Operator ID is required. If legitimate control cannot be re-established, the existing Operator ID MUST be terminally revoked.


Return to federation MUST require validated recovery authority, complete revocation of compromised material, monotonic new generation, publication of new trust state, required transparency inclusion and witness thresholds, peer propagation, documented incident scope, and successful conformance and readiness checks. Restoration SHOULD be staged through recovery verification, limited federation, canary traffic, restricted production, and normal status.


Quarantine MAY be scope-limited when compromise is provably bounded; unknown scope MUST fail closed. Last-known-good state MUST NOT keep a known-compromised operator active. Containment MAY be automated immediately, while restoration requires the applicable root, threshold, registry, governance, and separation-of-duties approvals.


In simple terms: NBSR isolates the compromised operator, preserves independently authorized services through safe alternatives, and permits return only with newly proven trust state rather than by reviving the compromised system.


K. Conformance dhe interoperability
Cila është sipërfaqja minimale që një implementim duhet me mbështet për WP8?
F95 — Minimum Mandatory WP8 Control-Plane Conformance Surface: APPROVED
A WP8 control-plane-conformant implementation MUST support more than object parsing. WP8 object-conformant implementations MAY claim only the canonical object, cryptographic, and deterministic validation surface they actually implement. The mandatory surface is a complete end-to-end federation control plane covering operator identity, delegation, trust bundles, transparency, discovery, bilateral authorization, revocation, privacy, deterministic behavior, and failure recovery.


Operator identity and keys: canonical binary and textual Operator ID; stable identity with purpose-bound operational keys; the current Ed25519 and COSE profile; Key Authorization Records; validity, rotation, generation, sequence, key-purpose enforcement, revocation, and terminal non-reuse.


Name ownership and delegation: signed ownership records; explicit composite scopes; parent-digest continuity; bounded depth and loop detection; controlled sub-delegation; scope narrowing; validity, generation, sequence, and revocation; and either full-chain or compact verifiable-chain validation.


Trust bundles: canonical signed scoped bundles containing issuer, keys, logs, witnesses, profiles, and revocation sources; generation, sequence, previous-bundle digest, key lifecycle states, freshness and maximum staleness, rollback and fork rejection, and composition without authority widening.


Transparency: signed Merkle checkpoints, inclusion and consistency proofs, freshness, split-view and equivocation detection, witness or gossip evidence when required, privacy-preserving commitments, and scope-based log filtering.


Federation discovery: discovery inside the NBSR Name/Resolution Plane; bootstrap metadata treated only as candidates; signed Operator and Federation Endpoint Records; exact Operator ID and transport-key binding; exact-name or privacy-safe lookup; anti-enumeration controls; and signed-freshness caching rather than blind DNS-TTL authority.


Federation authorization: independent Source and Destination Operator validation; exact bilateral Operator ID binding; stable Service ID and authority-chain binding; checkpoint compatibility and synchronization; compact RouteGrant or equivalent authority references; local fast-path validation over prevalidated state; and no direct-origin fallback.


Revocation and failure behavior: typed revocation for keys, operators, delegations, services, endpoints, bundles, checkpoints, and route state; dependency-aware selective invalidation; terminal tombstones; multi-path propagation; bounded last-known-good behavior; the outage operation matrix; severity-based existing-session treatment; and complete-operator quarantine and recovery.


Privacy: subscriber-blind discovery; scoped pseudonymous subscriber identity; minimum audit disclosure; session-unlinkable abuse evidence with controlled escalation; exact-match private lookup; no global service enumeration; and no disclosure of Origin Endpoints, private topology, or subscriber data.


Determinism and safety: canonical encoding, deterministic hashing and signatures, deterministic validation outcomes, bounded parsing and resource limits, stable error classes, and fail-closed handling of unknown critical fields, unsupported versions, ambiguity, stale state, rollback, or conflicting evidence.


The minimum end-to-end conformance flows are: operator registration and key authorization; ownership creation; delegation creation and verification; service publication; trust-bundle distribution; transparency inclusion and consistency verification; federation discovery; bilateral route authorization; Synthetic-IP resolution; authenticated Transport Session establishment; service-specific Route Context or Service Channel creation; successful routing; key, delegation, and service revocation; selective invalidation; bounded outage behavior; split-view detection; and operator-compromise quarantine and recovery.


The first minimum does not require a real public global registry, vendor-specific production HSM integration, advanced private-information-retrieval lookup, multi-algorithm agility, generic IP tunneling, a full production UDP profile before that profile is frozen, a real global governance institution, or Internet-scale performance. Such features MUST NOT be claimed as implemented when they are only stubs or simulations.


Conformance claims are tiered: WP8-object-conformant means canonical encoding, decoding, and validation of required objects; WP8-control-plane-conformant means complete trust, delegation, discovery, transparency, authorization, and revocation behavior; WP8-end-to-end-conformant requires all mandatory flows from name resolution through secure route establishment and failure recovery.


In simple terms: the WP8 minimum is not a parser; it is a functioning federation control plane that can create, verify, use, revoke, and recover trust end to end.


Cila gjuhë përdoret për implementimin e dytë: Go, Node/TypeScript apo tjetër?
F96 — Go as the Independent Second WP8 Runtime Implementation: APPROVED
Node.js 24 remains the existing independent second-language conformance verifier for the checked-in Core v0.2 deterministic vector package. It independently verifies canonical CBOR, COSE Sign1, Ed25519, manifests, artifacts and expected outcomes without importing Python or NBSR runtime code. It is not an NBSR runtime implementation and must not be counted as end-to-end federation interoperability evidence.


Python remains the reference and control-plane implementation for protocol semantics, vector generation, validation, automation, conformance orchestration and federation state-machine modeling. Rust remains the primary transport/runtime implementation for QUIC Transport Sessions, Service Channels, TCP streams, UDP datagrams, admission, resumption and draining.


Go is selected as the independent second WP8 federation object and control-plane implementation. It becomes an independent end-to-end runtime only if it also implements the required transport/session/channel boundary independently rather than relying on the same Rust runtime used by the reference stack. It MUST be built from the approved specification, schemas and conformance vectors rather than by importing or mechanically translating Python, Rust or Node internal code. It MUST provide its own canonical parser and encoder, cryptographic verification, object validation, state machines, error mapping and federation behavior.


The Go implementation proceeds in stages. The first stage covers canonical CBOR, Ed25519 and COSE Sign1, Operator ID and Key Authorization Records, ownership and delegation objects, trust bundles, transparency checkpoints and proofs, revocation, tombstones and WP8 deterministic vectors. The second stage covers federation discovery, exact bilateral authorization, trust and checkpoint synchronization, RouteGrant authority-state validation, outage handling, bounded last-known-good behavior and operator quarantine. The third stage integrates the independent Go control plane with the Rust transport runtime and proves live Source/Destination communication, secure route establishment, revocation propagation, and recovery. This proves independent control-plane interoperability over a shared transport implementation, not independent end-to-end runtime interoperability. A stronger end-to-end-runtime claim requires an independently implemented transport/session/channel stack or an explicitly narrower conformance label.


Independence requires that Go use only public specification material, wire schemas, vectors and explicitly documented interoperability contracts. Ambiguities discovered during implementation MUST be raised as specification issues or corrections rather than resolved through hidden knowledge of the reference implementation.


Conformance evidence is layered: Python plus Node proves cross-language object and vector agreement; Go proves independent federation object and control-plane behavior; Go integrated with Rust proves live control-plane interoperability over the documented Rust transport boundary. Independent end-to-end-runtime evidence requires two independently implemented complete stacks, including the transport/session/channel boundary.


In simple terms: Node already proves the bytes independently; Go will prove that a second real federation runtime can communicate and enforce the protocol independently.


A duhet implementimi i dytë me u ndërtu vetëm nga specification dhe vectors?
F97 — Clean-Room Independent Implementation from Specification and Vectors: APPROVED
The independent implementation MUST be built from the normative specification, wire schemas and registries, protocol profiles, deterministic conformance vectors, symbolic expected outcomes and error classes, public errata or clarification records, interoperability instructions, and the external standards explicitly referenced by NBSR.


It MUST NOT use Python, Rust, Node, or another reference implementation source as the behavioral model; import internal runtime libraries; mechanically translate code; call the reference implementation as an oracle; rely on private design notes, undocumented runtime traces, or hidden behavior; or use tests that generate answers by executing the reference runtime.


Repository material permitted as implementation input includes normative documents, checked-in vector packages and manifests, public test keys, expected pass/fail outcomes, error identifiers, versioned errata, and public interoperability instructions. The implementation MUST independently perform parsing, canonical encoding, hashing, signature verification, state transitions, authorization, and error mapping.


When the specification is ambiguous, the implementer MUST NOT inspect reference source and silently copy its behavior. The ambiguity MUST become a specification issue, followed by a normative clarification or versioned erratum and corresponding valid and invalid vectors. Every implementation is then updated against the same public material.


The implementation MUST document the specification version, vector manifest, referenced external standards, dependencies, reported ambiguities, and isolation from reference runtime code. Conformance review SHOULD inspect imports, dependency graphs, generated-code provenance, shared internal packages, test isolation, and any network or process call that could delegate decisions to a reference implementation.


Normative constants such as numeric keys, message codes, and error codes MAY be identical because they come from the specification. Live interoperability remains mandatory after vector conformance: each peer MUST encode, sign, verify, authorize, and enforce independently over standard NBSR wire messages without a private translation adapter.


An implementation qualifies as independent only when a separate team can build it from the published specification and vectors, it produces the same canonical bytes and normative outcomes without reference-source knowledge, and every missing rule discovered during implementation is corrected publicly in the specification.


In simple terms: the second implementation must prove the specification, not imitate the first implementation. If source-code knowledge is required to implement the protocol correctly, the specification is not complete yet.


Cilat objekte dhe flows duhet me pasë deterministic vectors?
F98 — Complete Deterministic Vector Coverage for WP8 Trust and Federation: APPROVED
Every normative WP8 object that is signed, hashed, published, cached, referenced by digest, changes authority, or determines an interoperability accept/reject outcome MUST have deterministic vectors.


Required object coverage includes Operator ID derivation and representation; Operator Registry Records; Key Authorization Records; key lifecycle, rotation, purpose binding, recovery transition, revocation, and terminal tombstones; Name Ownership Records; ownership continuity and transfer; Delegation Records and sub-delegations; composite scopes; parent-digest continuity; generation, sequence, expiry, revocation, recovery generation, and compact chain proofs; trust bundles, lifecycle states, scope composition, previous-bundle continuity, freshness, rollback, fork, and equivocation; transparency leaves, privacy-preserving commitments, signed Merkle checkpoints, inclusion proofs, consistency proofs, witness evidence, and split-view evidence; Operator and Federation Endpoint Records; exact-match discovery requests and privacy-safe responses; endpoint-key binding; federation authority proofs; exact bilateral Operator binding; checkpoint compatibility; RouteGrant authority-state references; Source and Destination admission; selective invalidation; typed revocation; outage state; quarantine; and recovery.

Transparency vectors MUST also expose ordered leaf inputs, expected leaf hashes, intermediate Merkle hashes, expected root, exact checkpoint bytes, and the proof path so implementations can identify the exact point of disagreement.


Ownership and delegation vectors MUST cover single-level and multi-level chains, sibling and partial-scope delegations, permitted and forbidden sub-delegation, maximum depth, loops, broken parent references, scope expansion, stale generation, expired authority, revocation, and conflicting ownership lineages. Trust-bundle vectors MUST cover initial state, ordinary sequence updates, generation transitions, previous_bundle_digest, active/next/retiring/retired/revoked key states, global/regional/operator/private scopes, restriction intersection, disqualification, rollback, fork, freshness, and maximum staleness.


Static vectors are insufficient by themselves. WP8 MUST also provide deterministic stateful scenario manifests with ordered events, fixed evaluation times, exact expected state after every step, and expected emitted objects or errors. Minimum flows include operator registration and key authorization; ownership, delegation, and service publication; bundle publication and transparency inclusion; discovery and independent bilateral authorization; synthetic resolution, RouteGrant, and authorized channel establishment; key and delegation rotation; trust-bundle rotation; checkpoint synchronization; relevant versus irrelevant updates; selective invalidation; revocation propagation; directory or log outage; bounded last-known-good operation; maximum-staleness failure; split-view detection; operator quarantine and recovery; multi-operator failover; and ownership transfer with revalidation.

The package MUST use a closed manifest that declares vector ID, object or flow class, protocol and profile version, artifact paths, exact byte lengths and SHA-256 values, expected accept or reject outcome, expected symbolic error, fixed evaluation time where needed, resource bounds, and artifact dependencies.


Vectors MUST NOT depend on wall-clock time, live DNS, live directories, network access, runtime randomness, implementation-specific JSON, platform paths, or iteration order. Test keys, nonces, salts, timestamps, sequences, checkpoints, and fixture state MUST be fixed, reproducible, and clearly marked test-only.


A WP8 object or flow is not frozen until it has valid and invalid vectors, the Python reference can generate or verify them, Node verifies the byte and object layer where applicable, Rust verifies the runtime portions it consumes, Go implements them independently, and all implementations produce the same canonical bytes, digests, outcomes, and symbolic errors.


In simple terms: every byte that creates trust and every transition that changes authority must be reproducible and identical across independent implementations.

Cilat rezultate janë pass/fail normative?
F100 — Deterministic Normative Pass/Fail Outcomes: APPROVED
Every vector and stateful flow MUST produce a deterministic, machine-verifiable normative outcome. Security-critical behavior MUST NOT depend on implementation-specific interpretation, warning-only acceptance, or partial success.


The normative decision outcomes are ACCEPT, REJECT, PENDING, RESTRICTED, and QUARANTINE. Enforcement is a separate field with the exact modes NONE, DENY_NEW_USE, REAUTHENTICATE, DRAIN, and TERMINATE_ACTIVE_USE. Every result MUST contain exactly one decision outcome and exactly one enforcement mode, even when the mode is NONE.


ACCEPT applies only when encoding is canonical; schema and wire types are valid; signatures, key purpose, identity, authority chain, scope, generation, sequence, continuity, validity, freshness, transparency evidence, revocation state, and applicable profile rules all validate; and the expected state transition completes exactly. The result MUST identify the accepted object or flow class, resulting state digest, emitted messages or records, selected enforcement mode, dependency set, and authorized Route Context or Service Channel where applicable.


REJECT applies when an input is malformed, non-canonical, cryptographically invalid, unauthorized, scope-widening, expired, revoked, terminal, replayed, rolled back, conflicted, unsupported in a critical way, resource-abusive, ambiguous, or otherwise fail-closed. REJECT MUST NOT modify accepted authority state and MUST return one deterministic primary symbolic error plus the validation stage and a bounded diagnostic classification.


PENDING is permitted only when the input is not yet proven invalid but mandatory evidence that may arrive later is missing, such as inclusion proof, checkpoint verification, witness threshold, authority-chain retrieval, or rollout readiness. PENDING MUST NOT create authority, activate a key, issue a new grant, or authorize a new route. It MUST have a declared timeout or expiry and transition deterministically to ACCEPT, RESTRICTED, or REJECT according to the applicable profile.


RESTRICTED applies only to previously accepted state that may continue with reduced scope, including bounded last-known-good operation, degraded-mode continuation, compatible checkpoint skew, existing-sessions-only mode, no-new-channel mode, or containment of an unaffected scope. The result MUST declare exactly which operations remain allowed, which are denied, the remaining freshness or staleness bound, the enforcement mode, and the condition for returning to ACCEPT or moving to REJECT.


QUARANTINE applies when conflict or compromise evidence must be preserved and investigated, including equivocation, split view, conflicting same-version digests, unknown compromise scope, invalid recovery lineage, full operator compromise, or a directory/transparency conflict affecting authority. New authority fails closed, disputed evidence is retained, and the conflict MUST NOT be resolved through first-seen, latest timestamp, or arbitrary operator preference.


TERMINATE_ACTIVE_USE is an enforcement mode, not a decision outcome. It applies only when a valid revocation or compromise requires active Transport Sessions, Route Contexts, Service Channels, TCP stream sets, UDP flow sets, grants, or leases to be closed or invalidated. The accompanying decision outcome is REJECT, RESTRICTED, or QUARANTINE as specified by the affected flow and policy. Conformance MUST verify that only the affected dependency is terminated when safe isolation is possible.


Every non-ACCEPT outcome MUST have one primary symbolic reason. The WP8 error registry MUST include stable classes for malformed input, non-canonical encoding, bad signature, wrong key purpose, scope exceeded, expiry, revocation, terminal state, stale sequence, rollback, equivocation, split view, missing proof, incompatible checkpoint, maximum staleness, resource limit, unsupported critical behavior, and policy denial. Exact numeric or symbolic allocation is frozen only through the WP8 wire/profile decision and MUST NOT drift silently.


When several failures exist, implementations MUST apply deterministic precedence: parsing and resource safety; canonical encoding; cryptographic structure and signature; identity and key-purpose binding; schema and version; authority and scope; generation, sequence and continuity; revocation and terminal state; transparency and checkpoint; freshness and outage policy; then local authorization policy.


A peer-facing wire error MAY be more general for privacy, anti-enumeration, or security reasons, but the internal conformance result MUST retain the exact primary normative outcome and reason.


For stateful flows, the manifest MUST declare after every step the expected outcome, symbolic error, accepted-state digest, quarantine or revocation state, emitted messages, active sessions and channels, durable monotonic watermark, and confirmation that rejected input caused no unauthorized state mutation.


The same input over the same initial state MUST produce the same outcome, symbolic error, state digest, and permitted side effects on every retry. It MUST NOT create duplicate authority, unauthorized sequence advancement, or additional mutation.


A vector passes only when the implementation produces the exact normative outcome, exact primary symbolic error where required, expected canonical artifacts, expected resulting state, and no forbidden side effect.


In simple terms: WP8 pass/fail means the exact decision, the exact reason, and the exact state after that decision—not merely whether the input could be parsed.


A duhet conformance suite me testu edhe timing dhe resource bounds?
F101 — Mandatory Timing and Resource-Bound Conformance: APPROVED
Conformance suite MUST test both hard safety bounds and protocol timing bounds. Hard safety bounds are normative and include maximum object size, CBOR nesting depth, collection lengths, delegation depth, trust-bundle entries, Merkle-proof length, manifest and checkpoint size, pending validations, replay state, memory allocation, CPU work, recursion, traversal, and bounded diagnostic or audit output. Exceeding a hard bound MUST produce deterministic REJECT with ERR_RESOURCE_LIMIT, without crash, hang, uncontrolled allocation, or partial state mutation.


Protocol timing bounds that affect correctness or security are also normative. Tests MUST cover not_before, expires_at, freshness, maximum_staleness, minimum and maximum overlap, checkpoint publication delay, pending-evidence timeout, recovery delay, quarantine reevaluation, drain timeout, session and grant lifetime, retry and backoff limits, revocation deadlines, checkpoint skew, and allowed clock skew. These tests MUST use fixed or virtual time rather than nondeterministic wall-clock timing.


Performance targets such as fast-path latency, discovery latency, proof-verification throughput, synchronization time, route establishment, memory per service or session, CPU cost, and sustainable query rate are profile-specific readiness evidence. They MUST be measured in a documented environment but MUST NOT change wire validity merely because one test machine is slow.


Resource vectors MUST include exact-boundary and over-boundary cases: an object exactly at the limit and one byte above it; a chain at maximum depth and one level beyond; maximum proof length and an excessive proof; replay storms, invalid-signature floods, enumeration attempts, recursive references, oversized manifests, and large but bounded dependency graphs. Expected outcome, symbolic error, allowed state mutation, and bounded output MUST be declared.


A timeout MUST NOT create partial authority. A timed-out operation enters PENDING, RESTRICTED, or REJECT according to profile, leaves no half-activated key, delegation, bundle, route, or checkpoint, and remains resumable and idempotent. Retry MUST NOT create duplicates or roll back accepted monotonic state.


Clock tests MUST include clocks slightly ahead or behind, clocks outside permitted skew, rollback after restart, monotonic-timer reset, expiry during validation, and state valid at evaluation but expired before activation. Wall clock MUST NOT replace generation, sequence, or transparency continuity.


The suite MUST also prove resource isolation, including per-operator, per-service, per-query, and per-proof limits, bounded queues, fairness, backpressure, overload rejection, and isolation between federation scopes.


Conformance tiers are cumulative: object conformance covers parsing, memory, and validation bounds; control-plane conformance covers state, proofs, retries, outages, and propagation bounds; end-to-end conformance covers route, session, channel, drain, and recovery timing; production-profile conformance additionally covers declared performance and scale targets.


In simple terms: conformance must prove that NBSR reaches the correct decision inside safe time and resource limits, without crashing, consuming unbounded resources, or leaving partial security state.


A duhet dy implementimet me komuniku live, apo mjafton byte-level agreement fillimisht?
F102 — Layered Interoperability: Bytes First, Live Communication Mandatory: APPROVED
Byte-level agreement is the first mandatory interoperability gate, but it is not sufficient for complete WP8 conformance. Live communication between independently implemented peers is a second mandatory gate before any implementation may claim end-to-end interoperability.


Gate A — deterministic byte-level agreement: implementations MUST produce and validate identical canonical CBOR, COSE Sign1 structures, hashes and digests, Operator ID derivation, key-purpose bindings, ownership and delegation objects, trust bundles, transparency leaves and Merkle roots, inclusion and consistency proofs, RouteGrant authority-state bindings, symbolic outcomes, error precedence, and resulting state digests. This gate MUST pass before live networking work is treated as normative interoperability evidence.


Gate B — stateful cross-implementation object exchange: one implementation MUST consume and verify the original bytes produced by another implementation. The harness MUST NOT recreate or translate objects into the receiver’s preferred internal format. Operator records, delegation chains, trust bundles, checkpoints, proofs, revocations, and state transitions created by one side MUST produce the same normative result on the other side.


Gate C — live control-plane interoperability: independent Source and Destination Operator implementations MUST communicate through documented NBSR wire messages and profiles for authenticated peer discovery, transport identity verification, protocol and version handling, checkpoint and trust-state comparison, authority-chain or compact-proof exchange, independent Source and Destination admission, RouteGrant or equivalent authorization exchange, revocation, refresh, and synchronization.


Gate D — full end-to-end live routing: WP8 end-to-end conformance requires two independently implemented operator stacks to prove ordinary name resolution, scoped Synthetic IP response, connection interception, authenticated encrypted Transport Session establishment, exact bilateral Operator ID binding, service-specific Route Context or Service Channel authorization, TCP routing, UDP routing when the applicable profile is frozen, session reuse, selective revocation, endpoint failover, outage behavior, operator quarantine, and recovery.


Private translation adapters are forbidden as interoperability evidence. Tests MUST NOT rely on a private JSON conversion layer, shared internal object model, Python bridge inside Go, implementation-specific proxy, shared database, or direct internal API that bypasses NBSR wire behavior. A test harness MAY launch processes, inject fixed fixtures, control time, and collect results, but peer communication MUST use only documented NBSR protocol messages and profiles.


The interoperability matrix MUST reverse roles: each implementation acts as Source and Destination, issuer and verifier, newer-checkpoint and older-checkpoint peer, revocation sender and receiver, and failure initiator and recovery participant. Negative live cases MUST include malformed frames, unsupported versions, invalid signatures, wrong Operator ID or transport-key binding, stale or incompatible checkpoints, revoked delegation, replayed grant, scope mismatch, timeout, duplicate or reordered updates, partial disconnect, split-view evidence, and incompatible resource limits.


Conformance claims are layered: byte and vector agreement permits WP8-object-interoperable; stateful object exchange permits WP8-control-object-interoperable; live federation exchange permits WP8-control-plane-interoperable; and a complete name-to-secure-route flow between two independent stacks permits WP8-end-to-end-interoperable. Passing the same vector package alone MUST NOT justify an end-to-end claim.


The implementation sequence SHOULD be: freeze schemas and vectors; establish Python, Node and Go byte agreement; prove Rust and Go consumption of normative objects; implement live control-plane exchange; bind the control plane to the Rust Transport Session; prove the complete route; then test revocation, failure, recovery, soak, and scale behavior.


In simple terms: byte agreement proves that implementations speak the same language; live interoperability proves that they can conduct the complete secure conversation independently.

For a solo or early-stage project, a separate development team is desirable but not required; technical isolation and reproducibility are the mandatory proof.

Normative constants, registries and schemas MAY be shared because they originate from the public specification.

The implementation MUST create canonical and signed artifacts that other implementations consume directly, including the applicable operator, key, ownership, delegation, trust, checkpoint, proof, revocation and federation-authority objects. A harness MUST NOT recreate or translate the producer’s original bytes for the receiver.


Conformance evidence MUST record the implementation commit, environment, commands, vector-manifest digest, exact pass/fail and symbolic-error results, generated artifact digests, resulting state digests, retry and idempotence outcomes, resource-bound results, timing fixtures and unsupported cases. Screenshots or unbound summaries are insufficient.

Future Go and Rust/Python-side tests MUST prove role reversal, authenticated standard NBSR wire exchange, independent admission on both sides, secure route establishment, negative scenarios, checkpoint synchronization, revocation propagation, outage handling, quarantine and recovery without private adapters, shared authority databases or reference-runtime decisions.

In simple terms: the repository already has real independent byte-verification evidence through Node; future Go or another runtime earns a stronger claim only when its source isolation, reproducible results and live behavior are proven without borrowing decisions from the reference implementation.

A futet federation në Core v0.2 apo në një extension/profile të veçantë?
F104 — Versioned Federation Extension Layered on the Approved Core v0.2 Candidate Baseline: APPROVED
WP8 federation MUST be defined as a separately versioned extension or profile layered on the approved Core v0.2 candidate baseline. Before Federation v0.1 wire freeze, the exact Core v0.2 baseline commit, vector-manifest digest, registries, schemas, and transport/session profile MUST be ratified and frozen. Federation work MUST NOT silently redefine the checked-in Core v0.2 vectors, artifacts, verifier behavior, transport assumptions, registries, or candidate Core object semantics.


The ratified Core v0.2 baseline remains the common base for canonical wire encoding, envelope and framing rules, the current cryptographic profile, common error and version-selection behavior, and base Transport Session, Route Context, and Service Channel primitives. The federation layer is identified independently, for example as NBSR Federation Extension v0.1 with an explicit extension identifier, extension version, and required Core version.


The federation extension covers Operator identity and registration, Key Authorization Records, name ownership and delegation, trust bundles, transparency checkpoints and proofs, federation discovery, bilateral authorization, typed revocation, recovery, privacy, and federation conformance. Supporting Core v0.2 alone does not imply federation support; supporting the federation extension requires successful Core conformance plus the extension’s object, stateful-flow, and interoperability gates.


The Source Edge MUST select one exact Core version before the Transport Session according to the Core v0.2 D8 decision; no in-band supported-version list or automatic fallback is added. Federation Extension support and the exact extension version MUST then be agreed as a separate authenticated capability bound to that selected Core session before federation-specific messages are sent. A peer that does not support the federation extension MUST NOT interpret federation messages as ordinary Core messages. Federation is mandatory for a WP8-federation-conformant claim, but Core-only, private static-trust, and consortium deployments MAY exist under explicitly declared profiles.


Frozen Core objects such as ServiceRecord, RouteIntent, RouteGrant, the Core envelope, and existing registries MUST NOT receive federation fields or changed semantics silently. Federation bindings MUST use new versioned objects, an explicit extension container, or a new object version when the base semantics change. In particular, delegation, trust-bundle, and checkpoint bindings for route authorization require either a separate FederationAuthorizationContext or a newly versioned RouteGrant; they MUST NOT be injected into a frozen RouteGrant schema without an explicit version decision.


Conformance artifacts MUST remain separated, for example vectors/core-v0.2 and vectors/federation-v0.1. The federation package MAY reference immutable Core artifacts but MUST NOT rewrite them. Clarifications and errata that do not alter wire semantics may remain within the same extension version; changes to required fields, signature inputs, authority semantics, dispatch, canonical bytes, critical validation, or downgrade behavior require a new extension version.


An implementation claiming Core v0.2 plus Federation Extension v0.1 MUST pass Core v0.2 conformance, federation object vectors, federation stateful flows, and the live interoperability level appropriate to its claim.


In simple terms: Core v0.2 remains the frozen foundation; WP8 federation is a new versioned layer above it, not a silent rebuild of the foundation after implementations already depend on it.


A nevojiten message types të reja?
F105 — Dedicated Federation Extension Message Namespace: APPROVED
Federation Extension v0.1 requires new message types because existing Core messages do not cover operator discovery, authority and delegation retrieval, trust-bundle synchronization, transparency proofs, bilateral federation authorization, revocation propagation, conflict evidence, quarantine, or recovery. These messages MUST be allocated inside a versioned federation-extension namespace and MUST NOT silently modify the frozen Core message registry.


Every federation message definition MUST bind the extension ID and version, message type, request/response/event semantics, permitted sender and receiver roles, required object schema, normative state transition, error behavior, and resource bounds. Core messages continue to govern base handshake, Transport Session, route primitives, streams, datagrams, and generic close behavior; federation messages govern trust, authority, discovery, synchronization, and lifecycle state.


The minimum semantic message families are:


1. Federation capability agreement: FED_CAPABILITIES and FED_CAPABILITIES_ACK declare federation-extension version, supported federation object versions, federation profiles, bounds, optional capabilities, and required critical federation features after Core peer identity is established. They MUST NOT advertise or negotiate a list of Core versions; D8 selects one exact Core version before the Transport Session.


2. Operator and endpoint discovery: FED_OPERATOR_QUERY/RESPONSE and FED_ENDPOINT_QUERY/RESPONSE, carrying signed Operator Records, Federation Endpoint Records, or verifiable references and proofs. Discovery metadata never creates authority by itself.


3. Authority and delegation retrieval: FED_AUTHORITY_QUERY/RESPONSE and FED_CHAIN_PROOF_REQUEST/RESPONSE for ownership evidence, delegation chains, compact verifiable proofs, authority-state digests, and exact service/scope binding.


4. Trust-bundle synchronization: FED_BUNDLE_QUERY/RESPONSE, FED_BUNDLE_UPDATE, and FED_BUNDLE_ACK for pull, push, recovery, rollout, and readiness evidence. A bundle acknowledgement MUST bind the exact bundle digest, generation, sequence, validation status, bounded reason code, and accepted checkpoint when applicable. Transport delivery acknowledgement does not equal normative acceptance.


5. Transparency synchronization: FED_CHECKPOINT_QUERY/RESPONSE, FED_INCLUSION_PROOF_REQUEST/RESPONSE, FED_CONSISTENCY_PROOF_REQUEST/RESPONSE, and FED_WITNESS_STATEMENT. Responses MUST disclose only the minimum evidence permitted by scope and privacy policy, and newer state MUST be independently verified rather than trusted merely because it is newer.


6. Bilateral authorization: FED_AUTHORIZE_REQUEST/RESPONSE, binding exact Source and Destination Operator IDs, Service ID or canonical-name digest, requested scope, authority-chain or compact-proof digest, trust-bundle state, checkpoint state, policy digest, nonce, and validity context. Responses use deterministic accept, pending, restricted, reject, or quarantine semantics. A positive destination response never replaces independent Source validation.


7. Revocation: FED_REVOCATION_PUSH/ACK and FED_REVOCATION_QUERY/RESPONSE. Revocation messages MUST be signed, typed, monotonic, replay-safe, idempotent, priority-capable, and bound to the exact target and scope. Both push and pull are mandatory because propagation cannot rely on one path.


8. Conflict and split-view evidence: FED_CONFLICT_REPORT/ACK for same-version conflicting digests, checkpoint split views, broken continuity, directory/transparency disagreement, and provenance receipts. These messages carry evidence; they do not grant the reporter unilateral authority to convict or revoke a peer.


9. Operator lifecycle and recovery: FED_STATUS_QUERY/RESPONSE, FED_QUARANTINE_NOTICE, FED_RECOVERY_NOTICE, and FED_REENTRY_EVIDENCE for SUSPECTED_COMPROMISE evidence, QUARANTINED state, RECOVERY with the required recovery_stage, TERMINALLY_REVOKED state, and staged re-entry. Recovery notices do not create authority; peers independently validate root, threshold, registry, transparency, and witness evidence.


Every exchange MUST include bounded correlation and replay context, including extension version, message type, request ID, sender Operator ID, intended receiver Operator ID when bilateral, nonce or equivalent replay context, creation and expiry, and optional response-to identifier. Correlation identifiers MUST NOT become stable subscriber-tracking identifiers.


The protocol MUST distinguish queries, updates or notices, acknowledgements, and evidence reports. A generic FED_OBJECT_REQUEST/RESPONSE MAY exist for registered object retrieval and future extensibility, but it MUST NOT replace semantically important authorization, revocation, quarantine, recovery, or conflict messages because those operations create explicit state transitions.


Unknown critical federation messages, unsupported extension versions, invalid role usage, or messages received in an invalid protocol state MUST fail closed with a bounded generic error and no state mutation. Unknown non-critical advisory messages MAY be ignored only when the extension rules explicitly permit it.


Numeric codes are not assigned by this decision. The implementation process is to freeze the semantic list, combine only messages whose semantics remain unambiguous, freeze schemas, define the extension namespace, allocate numeric codes, create valid and invalid vectors, and verify behavior across Python, Node, Rust, and Go.


Federation messages may be interpreted only after Core peer authentication succeeds, the federation extension version is negotiated, the message code is known for that version, and the sender is authorized for the requested role and operation.


In simple terms: federation needs its own clear protocol vocabulary, but that vocabulary is added as a versioned extension rather than hidden inside the already frozen Core language.


A nevojiten object schemas të reja?
F106 — Minimal Versioned Federation Object Model: APPROVED
Federation Extension v0.1 MUST define new, versioned object schemas because the frozen Core objects do not represent global ownership, delegation chains, trust bundles, transparency proofs, operator discovery, typed revocation, quarantine, or recovery. The object surface MUST remain minimal: a new object exists only when it has independent identity, lifecycle, signature or digest semantics, authority impact, revocation behavior, publication or caching requirements, or deterministic interoperability consequences.


The minimum federation object set is:


1. OperatorRegistryRecord — binds stable Operator ID, identity-root reference, organization or private-domain binding, federation scope, status, registry issuer, generation, sequence, validity, previous-record digest, and revocation reference. It MUST NOT expose operational endpoints or private infrastructure.


2. KeyAuthorizationRecord — binds Operator ID, key ID, public key, one exact key purpose, lifecycle state, not_before, expires_at, generation, sequence, optional predecessor, authorizing root or recovery authority, and revocation state. It is the normative basis for one-key-per-purpose and cross-purpose rejection.


3. NameOwnershipRecord — binds canonical name scope, stable owner identity or privacy-safe owner reference, ownership generation and sequence, source or bootstrap evidence reference when applicable, stable Service ID creation or binding authority, transfer or recovery state, previous ownership digest, validity, revocation, and naming-authority or registry issuer. It MUST NOT contain Origin Endpoints, CDN endpoints, or runtime route state.


4. DelegationRecord — binds delegator and delegatee, authority class, canonical composite scope, allowed actions, sub-delegation permission, remaining depth, parent delegation digest, previous-version digest, generation, sequence, not_before, expires_at, policy-envelope reference, and revocation reference. Missing scope fields MUST NOT imply unlimited authority.


5. FederationTrustBundle — binds bundle scope, issuer, generation, sequence, previous-bundle digest, accepted roots and authorities, purpose-bound public keys and lifecycle states, accepted logs and witnesses, thresholds, supported profiles and versions, revocation sources, validity, freshness, maximum staleness, and restrictive policy constraints. It MUST NOT contain private keys, subscriber identities, Origin Endpoints, or session state.


6. TransparencyCheckpoint — binds log ID, exact scope or partition, tree size, Merkle root, checkpoint sequence or generation, timestamp, signing-key ID, and optional previous trusted checkpoint or witness-policy reference. It is a compact signed summary and MUST NOT contain the underlying records.


7. InclusionProof — binds log ID and scope, exact checkpoint reference, leaf index, tree size, leaf digest, ordered proof path, hash profile, and optional object-class binding.


8. ConsistencyProof — binds log ID and scope, old and new checkpoint or tree sizes, old and new roots or checkpoint digests, ordered consistency path, and hash profile.


9. WitnessStatement — binds witness ID, log ID and scope, exact checkpoint digest, tree size, Merkle root, observation time, verification result, optional previous checkpoint, key ID, signature, validity, and replay context.


10. OperatorEndpointRecord — publishes only federation-facing endpoints and binds Operator ID, endpoint ID or locator, role, region or scope, transport profile, port, priority or failover role, transport-key or certificate binding, lifecycle state, generation, sequence, validity, previous-record digest, and revocation reference. Origin Endpoints and private connectors MUST remain undisclosed.


11. FederationAuthorityProof — compactly binds the ownership-root digest, relevant delegation-chain digests, exact Service ID or name scope, Source and Destination Operator IDs when bilateral, trust-bundle digest, relevant checkpoint digest, freshness, revocation-state commitment, proof generation, and validity. It MUST NOT hide broken ancestry, scope widening, or revoked ancestors.


12. FederationAuthorizationContext — binds exact Source and Destination Operator IDs, Service ID or canonical-name digest, authority-proof digest, trust-bundle digest or version, checkpoint digest, policy digest, allowed protocols, ports and actions, validity, generation, sequence, dependency-set digest, and RouteIntent or Route Context reference. Existing RouteGrant or route-establishment messages MAY reference its digest. This avoids silently adding federation fields to the frozen RouteGrant.


13. TypedRevocationRecord — one common typed revocation schema binding target object class, exact target ID, key ID or digest, issuer authority, scope, reason, terminal or non-terminal semantics, enforcement mode, effective time, optional expiry only where permitted, generation, sequence, replacement reference when applicable, and transparency or checkpoint reference.


14. ConflictEvidence — binds conflict class, exact scope, object class, conflicting canonical digests, claimed generation, sequence or tree size, relevant checkpoints and proofs, provenance receipts, observer or witness statements, bounded first-observed times, and quarantine recommendation. Evidence does not grant the reporter unilateral authority.


15. OperatorLifecycleRecord — binds Operator ID, one exact top-level lifecycle_state from APPLIED, VERIFICATION_PENDING, VERIFIED, PROVISIONAL, ACTIVE, SUSPENDED, QUARANTINED, RECOVERY, RETIRED, TERMINALLY_REVOKED, or REJECTED, plus affected scope, reason, generation, sequence, effective time, authorizing authority, required re-entry evidence, previous lifecycle digest, and transparency reference. When lifecycle_state is RECOVERY, recovery_stage is required and is exactly RECOVERY_PENDING, RECOVERY_VERIFIED, or REENTRY_RESTRICTED; recovery_stage is prohibited for every other lifecycle_state. Non-canonical aliases MUST NOT appear on the wire.


16. RecoveryTransitionRecord — binds the affected identity or authority lineage, old and new generations, revoked keys and objects, replacement keys and records, recovery authority, threshold approvals, continuity or explicit-break evidence, transparency and witness evidence, activation restrictions, and staged re-entry status. It MUST NOT be used for ordinary rotation.


17. ConflictResolutionRecord — binds conflict ID, evidence-set digest, affected scope, authoritative outcome, invalidated or preserved objects, required generation and sequence, revocations, recovery or migration requirements, effective time, appeal window, approving authorities, previous resolution digest where applicable, and transparency reference. It is the machine-consumable authority for final conflict resolution and MUST NOT delete or conceal the losing evidence branch.


18. AppealDecisionRecord — binds appeal ID, original decision digest, evidence-set digest, appellant standing class, affected scope, grounds category, outcome, exact state transitions, invalidated or preserved objects, effective time, required follow-up, approving authorities, and transparency reference. It reviews the correctness of a prior decision and MUST NOT reactivate revoked or quarantined authority merely because an appeal was filed.


A separate normative object is not required for every query, response, delivery acknowledgement, retry state, cache row, health check, traffic weight, Origin Endpoint, or implementation-specific database record. Those remain message bodies or local operational state unless they independently create trust or authority.


Federation objects SHOULD share common lifecycle conventions for object type, object version, issuer, generation, sequence, validity, optional previous digest, extensions, and COSE signature wrapping, but fields MUST NOT be copied blindly into every schema. Each schema retains only the fields required by its own semantics.


Schemas are closed for the first extension version. Required and optional keys, wire types, bounds, COSE signer and kid bindings, and canonical CBOR encodings MUST be frozen. Unknown critical fields fail closed. Non-critical extensions are allowed only in a declared extension namespace. Duplicate, ambiguous, cross-schema, cyclic, wrong-class, or unbounded references MUST be rejected.


Objects SHOULD reference exact dependencies by canonical digest where that enables continuity, caching, selective invalidation, and auditability. Reference depth, graph size, and traversal MUST remain bounded, cycles MUST fail closed, and every reference MUST declare the expected object class and compatible scope.


Frozen Core ServiceRecord, RouteIntent, RouteGrant, envelopes, and registries remain unchanged. Federation Extension MAY reference and validate them or introduce explicitly versioned replacements later; it MUST NOT add fields or change their semantics silently.


Before numeric-key allocation, WP8 MUST freeze the minimal object list, define authority and lifecycle for each object, eliminate unnecessary overlap, define exact fields and resource bounds, allocate deterministic CBOR keys, define COSE signer and kid bindings, generate valid and invalid vectors, and prove cross-language agreement.


In simple terms: federation needs new schemas because it creates new forms of trust and authority, but every object must justify its existence through distinct security and lifecycle semantics.


Cilat fields janë critical dhe cilat optional?
F107 — Explicit Required, Optional-Critical and Optional-Non-Critical Fields: APPROVED
Federation Extension v0.1 MUST classify every field as required critical, optional critical, or optional non-critical. Requiredness determines whether the field must be present; criticality determines whether a verifier may safely ignore semantics it does not understand.


Required critical fields are those needed to identify, authenticate, authorize, version, or safely enforce an object. Missing, malformed, unsupported, or invalid required critical fields MUST cause deterministic rejection. Depending on the object, these include object type and version, issuer or authorizing authority, signer key binding, exact scope or target, generation, sequence, validity or lifecycle state, authority-defining fields, signature wrapper and kid, mandatory continuity digests, revocation target, checkpoint tree size and root, exact Source and Destination Operator IDs for bilateral authorization, and required enforcement mode.


Optional critical fields MAY be absent only in explicitly defined contexts with one safe normative meaning. When present they MUST be recognized, validated, covered by the signature, and enforced. Examples include previous_record_digest for non-genesis updates, parent_delegation_digest for child delegations, expires_at for time-bounded but not terminal revocation, witness or recovery thresholds when the applicable profile enables them, regional, tenant, port or protocol restrictions, replacement references, and privacy-commitment metadata. Optional never means ignorable when the field changes trust, scope, validity, revocation, privacy, or enforcement.


Optional non-critical extension fields MAY be ignored for the normative decision only when they cannot change authority, scope, signer selection, key purpose, trust roots, revocation, lifecycle, routing, admission, recovery, witness thresholds, policy enforcement, downgrade behavior, or grant validity. Examples MAY include bounded human-readable labels, localized descriptions, non-authoritative contact or documentation hints, observability correlation metadata, UI presentation preferences, and advisory deployment metadata. They MUST remain canonical, privacy-safe, size-bounded, and inside a declared extension namespace.


Security semantics MUST NOT be hidden in non-critical fields. If a previously advisory field becomes relevant to authority or enforcement, it requires a new critical extension capability or a versioned schema change; it MUST NOT silently acquire security meaning.


Each base object schema uses a closed key space. Unknown keys in the frozen base range, duplicate keys, wrong wire types, or keys whose semantics belong to another object or version MUST be rejected. Non-critical extensions use the reserved extension namespace already established by the protocol direction, conceptually keys 1000 and above, subject to the final frozen registry allocation. Each extension declares its ID, version, criticality, value type, maximum size, multiplicity, and privacy classification.


A new critical extension MAY be used only after its capability and version have been negotiated. A peer that does not support that critical extension MUST reject the object fail-closed. A critical extension MUST NOT be mislabeled as non-critical to preserve compatibility.


Defaults are permitted only when they are unique, normative, safe, and identical across implementations. Missing fields MUST NOT imply unlimited scope, all ports, all protocols, all tenants, unlimited delegation depth, no expiry, absence of revocation, global visibility, unrestricted sub-delegation, or broad federation access. Ambiguous absence MUST cause rejection.


Requiredness MAY be context-dependent: previous_digest is required for an update and forbidden for genesis; parent_digest is required for a child delegation and forbidden for an ownership root; expires_at is required for temporary revocation and forbidden for terminal revocation; witness statements are required when threshold is greater than zero; recovery evidence is required for RECOVERY_VERIFIED and REENTRY_RESTRICTED; destination_operator_id is required for bilateral authorization; and Origin Endpoint details are forbidden in public federation objects. These conditions MUST be part of schema validation and conformance vectors.


Every field contained in a signed object, including optional non-critical extensions, is covered by the exact canonical signed bytes. Non-critical does not mean mutable after signing.


Unknown-field behavior is deterministic: an unknown base-schema field, unknown critical extension, unsupported critical-extension version, malformed or oversized extension, extension in the wrong namespace, extension that widens authority, or ambiguous criticality causes rejection. An unknown non-critical extension in the valid extension namespace MAY be ignored for the decision, but the implementation MUST preserve the exact signed bytes and canonical object digest. A forwarding implementation MUST NOT remove or rewrite an extension it does not understand.


Conformance vectors MUST cover required fields, missing required fields, absent optional fields, valid and invalid optional-critical fields, unknown critical and non-critical extensions, malformed extensions, wrong namespaces, ambiguous defaults, forbidden state-specific fields, context-dependent requiredness, attempted authority expansion through extensions, and byte-preserving forwarding.


In simple terms: required tells whether a field must be present; critical tells whether the verifier may fail to understand it. Anything that can change trust, authority, validity, privacy, revocation, or enforcement must be fully understood or the object is rejected.


Si sillet një implementim që nuk e njeh federation extension?
F108 — Safe Behavior for Implementations Without Federation Extension Support: APPROVED
An implementation that supports Core v0.2 but does not support Federation Extension v0.1 MUST continue only with explicitly negotiated Core capabilities and MUST reject every operation that depends on federation authority. It MUST NOT guess federation semantics, silently downgrade, reinterpret federation messages as Core messages, convert unsupported federation objects into static trust, or use DNS, HTTPS reachability, Web PKI, or direct-origin fallback as replacement authority.


The exact Core version is selected before the Transport Session according to D8 and is only confirmed and bound to the authenticated session. During federation capability agreement, each peer MUST advertise only the federation-extension versions, federation profiles, federation object versions, critical features, and limits it actually supports for that already selected Core version. If federation is mandatory for the requested profile and no compatible federation version exists, negotiation for that profile fails with a bounded unsupported-extension or unsupported-profile result before federation state, grants, or channels are created. If federation is optional, peers MAY continue only with capabilities that both sides explicitly negotiated; federation-specific messages, objects, authorization claims, and channels remain unavailable.


A Core-only implementation MAY support Core framing, Transport Session primitives, other negotiated transport or application profiles, local or lab operation, and an explicit preconfigured static-trust profile. It MUST NOT claim WP8 federation, global discovery, ownership or delegation validation, federation trust bundles, transparency verification, bilateral federation authorization, or public federation reachability.


A federation message received before negotiation, under an unsupported version, or by a peer that does not support the extension MUST be rejected without state mutation. It MUST NOT be parsed as another Core message or forwarded as opaque authority. The implementation MAY reject only the extension exchange or MAY close the session when Core framing, authenticated state, or downgrade safety is affected, according to Core error rules.


An unknown federation object MUST NOT create trust, accepted authority, routing state, or a static-trust relationship. It MUST NOT be interpreted partially. A truly non-critical opaque attachment MAY be preserved or forwarded only when the enclosing decision does not depend on understanding it and the signed bytes remain intact.


Static trust is not an automatic fallback. It MAY be used only when the static profile was configured in advance, negotiated explicitly, binds exact Operator IDs, keys, endpoints, scopes, validity and policy cryptographically, and does not claim public-federation semantics. Federation failure or absence alone MUST NOT activate static mode.


When a requested name requires federation validation and the local implementation lacks the extension, resolution MUST fail deterministically for that profile. It MUST NOT return an authorized Synthetic IP, expose an Origin Endpoint, return an A or AAAA origin as fallback, or derive route authority from DNS TTL, DNSSEC, a valid HTTPS response, or a Web PKI certificate. An explicitly configured Core-only or static-profile service may proceed only under that separate negotiated profile.


An existing Core session MAY remain active for unrelated capabilities already negotiated. A later federation-dependent request is rejected separately and MUST NOT open a federation Route Context, Service Channel, grant, or authority state. If federation was mandatory for the complete session, negotiation should have failed before authorization.


Capability negotiation and profile selection MUST be bound to the authenticated handshake transcript or equivalent session context. Capability stripping, forged unsupported responses, forced static mode, and direct-origin downgrade attempts MUST fail closed or be surfaced as downgrade evidence.


A non-federation implementation MAY claim Core-v0.2 conformance or another explicit supported profile. It MUST NOT claim Federation-v0.1, WP8 control-plane, or WP8 end-to-end conformance without federation support and the corresponding object, stateful, and live interoperability tests.


Conformance vectors and live scenarios MUST cover federation extension absence, unsupported version, mandatory and optional mismatch, federation messages before negotiation, unknown critical federation messages, explicit static-profile success, silent downgrade attempts, DNS or direct-origin fallback attempts, continued unrelated Core operation, and authenticated capability stripping.


In simple terms: if an implementation does not understand federation, it may speak only the Core language it knows; it must not invent, downgrade, or bypass the missing security semantics.


A duhet WP8 me ruajt compatibility me WP7 static trust?
F109 — Explicit Compatibility with WP7 Static Trust: APPROVED
WP8 MUST preserve compatibility with WP7 static trust as a separate, explicit deployment profile for private enterprise, small consortiums, direct operator peering, labs, isolated deployments, controlled bootstrap, and recovery environments. Compatibility is profile-level and MUST NOT merge static trust with federation semantics.


The WP7-compatible profile MAY reuse exact Operator IDs, purpose-bound peer keys, configured federation-facing endpoints, exact service or namespace scopes, tenant and resource isolation, bilateral policy, quotas, audit, revocation, source-before-destination admission, and independent destination validation. It does not automatically provide global operator registration, public ownership registry, delegation chains, trust-bundle distribution, transparency logs, witness thresholds, public discovery, multi-operator governance, or global conflict resolution.


The profile MUST be declared explicitly, for example as nbsr-static-trust-v1, and MUST bind trusted Operator IDs, keys and purposes, endpoint records, allowed scopes, services, ports, protocols, validity, revocation sources, maximum staleness, local policy, and sub-delegation behavior. Static trust MUST NOT activate automatically because federation is unsupported or unavailable.


Static and federation trust MAY coexist only with separate scopes, roots, versions, and policies. Neither profile may silently import authority from the other. When both apply, restrictions are intersected, conflicts are isolated, and first-seen, broadest-wins, or arbitrary precedence is forbidden.


WP8 SHOULD support controlled migration from static trust to federation through operator registration, signed endpoint publication, trust-bundle creation, transparency evidence, ownership or delegation validation, peer readiness, transitional overlap, federation activation, and retirement or recovery-only retention of the static profile. Migration MUST be explicit, auditable, bounded, and incapable of expanding authority without authorization.


Static trust state SHOULD be signed or integrity-protected, versioned, and bound to generation, sequence, canonical digest, validity, signer or administrator authority, revocation state, and previous-state continuity. Rollback to stale static configuration MUST fail closed. A known compromise or terminal revocation MUST override static last-known-good behavior.


Configured endpoints remain candidates rather than authority by IP alone. The peer MUST prove possession of the key bound to the exact Operator ID and role during the authenticated handshake. Static discovery MAY use configured endpoints, private directories, local mappings, or bilateral records, but it MUST preserve exact identity and scope validation.


Conformance claims MUST distinguish WP7-static-trust-compatible, WP8-static-profile-conformant, WP8-federation-conformant, and WP8-end-to-end-federation-conformant. Static compatibility MUST NOT justify a broader federation claim.


Required tests include valid bilateral static trust, wrong Operator ID or endpoint key, stale configuration, revocation, rollback, static/federation conflict, migration to federation, static retirement, recovery-profile activation, and attempted silent downgrade.


In simple terms: WP7 static trust remains a valid bridge for controlled deployments; WP8 federation is the complete distributed trust system, and neither may impersonate the other.


A lejohet fallback nga federation në static trust?
F110 — No Automatic Downgrade from Federation to Static Trust: APPROVED
When a service, operator pair, or deployment policy requires the Federation Extension, failure of federation discovery, proof retrieval, trust synchronization, or authorization MUST NOT automatically activate static trust. The implementation MUST retry through alternate trusted sources, use bounded previously verified federation state when policy permits, enter restricted mode, or fail closed.


Static trust MUST NOT replace federation when failure is caused by missing or invalid delegation evidence, ownership conflict, revocation, state beyond maximum staleness, invalid transparency proof, split view, equivocation, unsupported critical semantics, trust-bundle conflict, checkpoint incompatibility, suspected compromise, policy denial, or downgrade evidence. Such a fallback would bypass the exact control that failed.


Transition to static trust is permitted only through a pre-authorized alternate trust profile established before the incident. The signed policy MUST bind the exact Source and Destination Operator IDs, exact service or namespace scope, static profile and version, approved keys and endpoints, ports and protocols, activation conditions, maximum duration, permitted and forbidden operations, required approval, revocation sources, audit requirements, and notification paths. This is a new authorization decision, not reinterpretation of a failed federation request.


The selected profile MUST be bound to negotiation and authorization before route creation. A request for federation-v0.1 MUST NOT be silently reinterpreted as static-trust-v1 after federation failure. Profile negotiation and capability evidence MUST be bound to the authenticated handshake transcript or Transport Session context so capability stripping, false unsupported responses, forced timeout, proof suppression, or unauthorized static-profile activation are detectable.


A pre-authorized static recovery profile MAY support private disaster recovery, isolated peering, federation-infrastructure restoration, emergency access to critical services, or a controlled management plane. It MUST be scope-bounded, time-bounded, unable to expand authority, separated from unrestricted subscriber-wide public traffic, approved according to risk, and fully auditable. It MUST NOT become a universal alternate path for public federation traffic.


Known federation security state always takes precedence. Static policy MUST NOT override terminal revocation, known key compromise, ownership loss, authority reduction, service retirement, operator quarantine, or a known conflicting checkpoint. If static configuration allows an object that accepted federation state has revoked or disqualified, the result is deny.


Even under the static profile, the exact Operator IDs, authorized handshake key, endpoint binding, service scope, ports, protocols, validity, and revocation state MUST validate. Static trust does not mean trusting an IP address, and direct-origin fallback remains forbidden.


Existing federation sessions MAY continue only under the bounded last-known-good and outage rules already approved. They MUST NOT be converted silently into static sessions mid-lifecycle. Renewal or reauthorization uses the same profile or creates a separate explicitly authorized static relationship with distinct audit identity.


When federation services recover, automation MUST retrieve missing bundles, checkpoints, proofs, and revocations; verify consistency; compare operations performed under the alternate profile with current federation state; reauthorize valid routes; disable the static recovery profile; drain or terminate invalid routes; and preserve complete incident evidence. Alternate-profile operation MUST NOT continue indefinitely.


Conformance MUST cover federation success without static use; outage with bounded federation state; federation failure without alternate policy; valid pre-authorized static recovery; expiry of the recovery window; wrong Operator ID or key; known federation revocation conflicting with static allow; capability stripping; silent profile switching; re-entry and reconciliation; and attempted authority expansion through recovery mode.


In simple terms: federation failure is not permission to lower security. Static trust is usable only when it was selected and authorized beforehand for the exact affected scope.


Nëse lejohet, kur dhe me çfarë kufizimesh?
F111 — Strict Activation Conditions for Pre-Authorized Static Trust: APPROVED
Static trust MAY be activated as an alternate profile only when every applicable condition below is satisfied. It is not a generic fallback and MUST NOT be created or broadened after federation failure.


The alternate policy MUST exist before the incident. It MUST be signed or otherwise integrity-protected, versioned, bound to generation and sequence, stored in durable state, validated during normal operation, and unavailable for ad hoc creation after federation failure.


The policy MUST bind the exact Source Operator ID, Destination Operator ID, endpoint roles, authorized endpoint IDs, bound transport keys, and trust scope. Wildcard Operator IDs, subnet-based trust, hostname-only trust, or IP-only trust are forbidden.


The policy MUST bind the exact Service ID, canonical name or bounded namespace, tenant where applicable, region, ports, protocols, allowed actions, service class, and traffic direction. Missing scope fields MUST NOT mean unrestricted access.


Only predeclared activation triggers MAY activate the alternate profile. Permitted examples include directory unavailability, transparency-provider outage, isolated private deployment, disaster-recovery exercise, management-plane restoration, planned migration, or loss of public connectivity while a private bilateral path remains valid. Invalid proof, revocation, ownership conflict, split view, compromise, policy denial, or unsupported critical semantics MUST NOT trigger activation. An outage is missing evidence; invalid evidence is evidence against trust.


Before activation, the implementation MUST prove that the static policy is unexpired, all bound keys and endpoints remain valid, no known revocation or terminal tombstone exists, relevant Operator IDs are not quarantined, no known ownership or authority loss exists, no unresolved relevant conflict exists, and durable monotonic state has not rolled back. Failure to prove any required precondition results in fail-closed behavior.


Approval requirements MUST be risk-based. A low-risk isolated lab MAY activate automatically when the signed policy allows it. Production recovery SHOULD require explicit operator approval, separation of duties, or threshold approval. Public subscriber traffic, critical infrastructure, and administrative control planes require stronger approval than private testing.


Every activation MUST contain activated_at, a normal operating window, a warning threshold, a hard expires_at, a maximum cumulative duration, and automatic shutdown. Indefinite activation is forbidden. Extension beyond the hard limit requires a new high-assurance, bounded, auditable approval; resetting the timer automatically is forbidden.


Authority and scope MUST NOT expand during activation. The alternate profile MUST NOT add a service, peer, port, protocol, ownership transfer, delegation, sub-delegation, trust root, key purpose, different Operator ID, or public exposure. It MAY use only previously approved authority or narrower authority.


The policy MAY allow continuation of existing sessions, new routes for already approved services, authenticated management access, federation-restoration traffic, health checks, evidence retrieval, revocation propagation, recovery coordination, and endpoint failover inside the same approved Operator ID and endpoint set.


The following operations MUST fail closed even while static mode is active: first-time trust of an Operator ID, first-time service authority, ownership or delegation changes, authority expansion, activation of an unknown endpoint, root or recovery-key generation, global publication, federation-membership admission, third-party cross-operator transit, direct-origin fallback, and use after hard expiry.


The alternate profile MUST enforce stricter resource limits, including bounded connection rate, request rate, bandwidth, concurrent sessions, service classes, query budgets, enumeration resistance, and transit restrictions. Enhanced anomaly detection and audit are required.


Every route or session created under static mode MUST record the active profile, policy digest, activation reason, approving authority, exact scope, activation time, hard expiry, bound keys and endpoints, and whether the operation is ordinary, restricted, or recovery traffic. Static authorization MUST NOT appear in audit as federation authorization.


Static activation MUST NOT stop federation recovery attempts. Implementations MUST continue bounded attempts to refresh federation state, query alternate directories, retrieve checkpoints, validate consistency, synchronize revocations, and notify operators.


Federation becomes authoritative again only after bundles and checkpoints synchronize, consistency is proven, missed revocations are applied, trust state is fresh, relevant peers agree on authority state, static-mode operations are revalidated, and no unresolved conflict or compromise evidence remains. Valid routes are reauthorized; invalid routes are drained or terminated; the static activation is revoked; and emergency credentials are rotated where required.


The conformance suite MUST test a valid pre-existing policy, a policy created after the incident, allowed and forbidden triggers, wrong peer, wrong service scope, expired policy, known revocation, automatic low-risk activation, threshold-required production activation, hard timeout, attempted scope expansion, federation recovery and re-entry, and reconciliation that discovers an invalid route.


The normative activation condition is:
pre-authorized AND exact identity AND exact scope AND allowed trigger AND no known revocation or conflict AND bounded time AND bounded operations AND auditable AND federation recovery continues.


In simple terms: static trust is permitted only as a controlled emergency corridor built and authorized in advance, never as a spare door opened whenever federation does not return the desired answer.

Kush ka të drejtë me ndryshu federation profile-in?
F112 — Multi-Stakeholder Governance for Federation Profile Changes: APPROVED
No single operator, sponsor, maintainer, vendor, registry operator, or implementation team may unilaterally change the normative global federation profile. Changes MUST follow a documented, open, multi-stakeholder governance process with separated roles, review evidence, threshold approval, versioning, delayed activation, and auditable release artifacts.


Changes are classified into three normative categories.


Editorial changes include spelling corrections, examples, references, and clarifications that do not change wire bytes, object semantics, authority, required fields, error precedence, conformance outcomes, or security behavior. Maintainers MAY approve and merge these only when anti-drift checks demonstrate that all normative vectors and registries remain unchanged.


Backward-compatible profile changes include new optional capabilities, non-critical extensions, additional operational profiles, new registry allocations, or additional conformance tests that preserve all existing semantics. These require a public proposal, security and interoperability review, privacy review where applicable, implementation evidence, at least two independently verifiable implementations or verifiers where the change is executable, and threshold approval by the governance body.


Security-critical or breaking changes include changes to trust roots, Operator ID semantics, ownership, delegation, signature input, key purpose, revocation, transparency, downgrade behavior, critical fields, message dispatch, error precedence, mandatory algorithms, canonical bytes, version negotiation, or authority state. Such changes MUST create a new version and require a formal proposal, threat-model update, independent security review, updated conformance vectors, migration and rollback plan, interoperability evidence, public review period, threshold or supermajority approval, and a delayed activation window.


Any operator, implementer, researcher, security reviewer, service owner, standards contributor, consortium member, or public-interest stakeholder MAY submit a proposal. Proposal authority does not imply approval authority.


The approving governance body MUST include separated stakeholder classes, including implementers, large and small operators, independent security experts, privacy experts, registry or transparency operators, and public-interest or user representatives. No single stakeholder class may hold an unchecked majority.


Security-critical approval MUST require both a numerical threshold and cross-class participation. A valid approval requires a minimum quorum, supermajority, participation from multiple stakeholder classes, implementation evidence, and no unresolved critical security or interoperability objection. The exact threshold is frozen in the governance profile and MUST NOT be bypassed by repository maintainers.


Maintainers are release and process custodians. They MAY manage repositories, verify process checks, merge an already approved change, publish signed artifacts, and perform release engineering. They MUST NOT unilaterally create normative semantics.


An emergency process MAY temporarily disable an unsafe algorithm, revoke a compromised governance or release key, publish a mitigation, or suspend a capability under active exploitation. Emergency action MUST be scope-limited, time-bounded, transparently logged, followed by post-incident review, and either ratified through normal governance or automatically withdrawn. Emergency authority MUST NOT silently add a trust root, expand authority, transfer ownership, or create permanent governance rules.

Si regjistrohen dhe revokohen operatorët?
F114 — Verifiable Operator Registration, Lifecycle and Revocation: APPROVED
Operator registration MUST be proof-based, staged, transparent and auditable. Operator revocation MUST be typed, proportional, scoped where possible, fail-closed where necessary, and resistant to rollback or resurrection.


A global-registration applicant MUST submit a signed application package containing accountable entity identity, organization identifiers, jurisdiction, authorized representatives, requested Operator ID class, identity-root public keys, recovery public keys, purpose-separated keys, federation and security contacts, intended scope, disclosures, and policy acceptance. Private keys MUST never be submitted to the registry.


The applicant MUST prove control of the identity-root key, recovery keys, declared organization identity channel, security contact, operational federation endpoints when published, and administrative authority for the accountable entity. Proofs MUST use fresh nonces, bounded validity and exact application-digest binding.


The registry operator MUST verify entity existence, applicant authority, non-deceptive identifiers, global uniqueness, absence of relevant duplicate or competing applications, resistance to impersonation, and consistency between the requested Operator ID class and supplied evidence. Public records MUST expose only the minimum accountable identity required; complete identity-verification documents remain protected.


A globally registered Operator ID MUST be globally unique within its class, stable through the operator lifecycle, bound to a registry record and identity lineage, and never recycled after retirement or terminal revocation.


The top-level normative lifecycle states are APPLIED, VERIFICATION_PENDING, VERIFIED, PROVISIONAL, ACTIVE, SUSPENDED, QUARANTINED, RECOVERY, RETIRED, TERMINALLY_REVOKED, and REJECTED. APPLIED, VERIFICATION_PENDING, VERIFIED, and REJECTED create no federation authority. PROVISIONAL creates only narrowly bounded pilot authority. ACTIVE creates authority only within registered scope. SUSPENDED blocks new authority temporarily. QUARANTINED isolates compromise or conflict. RECOVERY permits only recovery-related operations and requires exactly one recovery_stage from F116: RECOVERY_PENDING, RECOVERY_VERIFIED, or REENTRY_RESTRICTED. recovery_stage is prohibited outside RECOVERY. RETIRED is controlled withdrawal. TERMINALLY_REVOKED permanently ends the lineage. SUSPECTED_COMPROMISE is a reason code that normally drives QUARANTINED, not a top-level state. RESTORED and RECOVERY_IN_PROGRESS are non-normative aliases and MUST NOT appear in canonical objects or vectors.


Before full activation, a provisional operator MUST demonstrate key possession, signed-record publication, endpoint identity binding, trust-bundle processing, transparency submission, revocation reception, bilateral interoperability, incident-contact readiness, and resource/rate compliance. Provisional scope MUST NOT exceed what has been tested.


ACTIVE status requires completed identity verification, a valid OperatorRegistryRecord, registered purpose-bound keys, valid recovery mechanisms, verified federation endpoints, required transparency inclusion, no unresolved collision or critical objection, minimum conformance evidence, registry approval, and a signed auditable activation checkpoint.


Operator updates, including organization identity, legal structure, contacts, endpoints, operational keys, recovery keys, registry operator or federation scope, MUST use generation, sequence and previous-digest continuity. Ordinary updates do not automatically require a new Operator ID, but identity transfer or entity discontinuity MAY require a new lineage.


Temporary suspension MAY be used for overdue audit, unreachable security contact, temporary non-compliance, repeated proven abuse, temporary legal restriction, or incomplete recovery evidence. Suspension MUST be reason-coded, scoped, review- or time-bounded, appealable, auditable, and history-preserving. New authority MAY be blocked while revocation and recovery traffic remain available when safe.


Security quarantine MUST be used for root compromise, federation-signing-key compromise, registry conflict, equivocation, split view, unauthorized authority changes, systematic abuse, or loss of identity control. New authority MUST fail closed. Existing use is enforced according to severity. Quarantine is not automatically terminal when secure recovery remains possible.


Revocation MUST be typed and target the narrowest independently isolatable dependency: operational key, recovery key, endpoint, service authority, delegation, trust bundle, registry record, or the complete Operator ID lineage. Operator-wide revocation MUST NOT be used for an isolated endpoint or key unless dependency analysis proves that the compromise cannot be contained.


Operator-wide lifecycle actions include temporary suspension, security quarantine, administrative retirement and terminal revocation. Temporary suspension is reversible and bounded. Security quarantine is evidence-driven and recoverable only through high-assurance recovery. Administrative retirement drains authority and permanently reserves the ID. Terminal revocation permanently ends the lineage and creates an immutable tombstone.


Revocation MAY be initiated by the operator, a registered recovery authority, an accredited registry operator, an emergency governance body, a transparency/conflict process, or a binding dispute decision. Automated security systems MAY initiate temporary quarantine but MUST NOT independently issue terminal revocation.


During active compromise, an emergency authority MAY publish a narrowly scoped temporary quarantine, affected-key or endpoint revocation, bounded emergency notice and enforcement instruction. Emergency action MUST include exact scope, evidence digest, issuer authority, expiry or review deadline, post-incident review, appeal path, and automatic rollback or escalation. It MUST NOT silently expand authority or add trust roots.


Security revocation requires verifiable evidence such as signed conflicting records, transparency proofs, proven key compromise or loss, verified identity transfer, applicable legal action under the registry charter, repeated proven protocol abuse, or failure of mandatory recovery obligations. Commercial disagreement or administrative dislike alone is insufficient. Public notices MAY be privacy-preserving, but the internal decision package MUST preserve complete evidence and provenance.


Operator lifecycle and revocation records MUST propagate through the registry record, transparency log, signed revocation feeds, push to known peers, pull/query endpoints, signed snapshots, mirrors and witnesses. Propagation MUST be monotonic, replay-safe, idempotent, priority-aware, measurable and testable.


When an Operator ID is suspended, quarantined or revoked, new federation authorizations MUST stop as required by severity; dependent cached authority MUST be invalidated; affected Route Contexts and Service Channels MUST be rejected or terminated; existing sessions MUST follow severity-based enforcement; unrelated operators and scopes MUST remain isolated; and no silent direct-origin or static-trust fallback is permitted.


Graceful retirement MUST support stopping new authority, publishing retirement intent, setting a bounded drain window, revoking delegations, transferring ownership only through a valid transfer process, closing federation endpoints, publishing final registry state and preserving a permanent tombstone. Retirement MUST NOT erase incident or lifecycle history.


Recovery from suspension or quarantine requires a new generation, new purpose-bound keys where affected, proof that compromised keys and objects are revoked, threshold recovery approvals, registry verification, transparency evidence, peer synchronization, staged re-entry, restricted initial scope and an observation period. A higher sequence signed by a compromised key MUST NOT restore authority.


Operators MUST have an appeal path for rejection, suspension, scope restriction, quarantine, retirement classification and terminal revocation. Emergency enforcement MAY remain active during review. An appeal reviews the decision but does not automatically restore authority.


After terminal revocation, the Operator ID MUST NOT be recycled; old roots, recovery keys and delegations remain invalid; stale mirrors and restored backups MUST preserve the tombstone; and a successor entity requires a new Operator ID and lineage.


The public registry SHOULD expose only Operator ID, accountable public identity, status, public keys and purposes, registered scope, lifecycle generation and sequence, registry issuer, transparency references and required public federation contacts. It MUST NOT expose personal identity documents, private addresses, personal phone numbers, internal infrastructure, origin endpoints, private recovery procedures or subscriber data.


Fees MAY fund registration operations but MUST NOT create pay-to-trust. Fee schedules MUST be transparent, support proportionate waivers or reductions, provide no privileged security semantics or secret expedited lane, and confer no permanent registration right merely through payment.


Conformance MUST cover valid registration, duplicate IDs, impersonation, failed proof of control, provisional activation, failed interoperability, key rotation, registry-key compromise, temporary suspension, scoped endpoint revocation, full quarantine, emergency action, graceful retirement, new-generation recovery, terminal revocation, attempted resurrection, stale mirrors, appeal reversal and registry-operator compromise.


In simple terms: an operator enters federation through proven accountable identity, separated keys, transparency and conformance; it is isolated or removed through signed, auditable, proportional revocation with no hidden path to resurrection.


Si zgjidhen konfliktet ndërmjet operatorëve?
F115 — Evidence-First Conflict Resolution with Scoped Quarantine: APPROVED
Conflicts between operators MUST be handled through two distinct layers: an immediate technical safety process that isolates the exact ambiguous scope, and a governance dispute process that determines durable authority from preserved evidence. No operator wins because it is larger, responded first, has more traffic, or controls the nearest endpoint.


Conflict classes MUST distinguish technical state conflicts, authority conflicts, operational conflicts, and administrative or legal conflicts. Technical validation applies the protocol; it MUST NOT decide legal ownership questions, while legal or commercial claims MUST NOT override invalid cryptographic evidence.


When a relevant conflict is detected, all conflicting branches and provenance MUST be preserved; the affected scope MUST enter QUARANTINE; new authority for that scope MUST fail closed; unrelated scopes SHOULD continue; and existing sessions MUST be handled according to severity. Implementations MUST NOT choose the newest-looking record, the closest source, first-seen state, or static fallback merely to escape ambiguity.


Quarantine MUST be dependency-scoped. An endpoint conflict isolates the endpoint; a service delegation conflict isolates that service and descendants; a partition conflict isolates that partition; an operator-root conflict isolates the operator lineage; and a global registry checkpoint conflict blocks new authority decisions that depend on that checkpoint.


Each dispute MUST have a signed evidence package containing a conflict identifier and class, affected scope, participating Operator IDs, object classes, canonical bytes or digests, generation and sequence, continuity chains, checkpoints and proofs, witness statements, registry receipts, observation times, reporter identity, provenance, requested remedy, and privacy classification. Unsupported statements do not create authority.


Deterministic protocol rules resolve cases such as invalid signatures, wrong key purpose, unauthorized issuers, scope violations, proven stale sequence, expired or revoked objects, invalid ancestry, or proofs that do not bind to the checkpoint. Human or governance review is required only where multiple branches remain cryptographically plausible, ownership or transfer is disputed, registry state conflicts, legal identity changes, recovery lineages compete, or the specification gives no precedence.


A neutral dispute panel MUST be independent of the parties, any registry operator implicated in the conflict, interested sponsors, and conflicted maintainers. Appropriate technical, security, registry, privacy, independent-operator, and legal expertise MUST be represented. Material conflicts of interest require recusal and, if necessary, replacement panel members.


Normative evidence precedence is: canonical and resource validity; signature and key-purpose validity; issuer authority; scope validity; generation, sequence and continuity; revocation and tombstones; transparency inclusion and consistency; registry status; valid transfer or recovery evidence; then a charter-authorized governance or legal decision. Market share, sponsorship and popularity have no precedence.


First registration MAY be relevant evidence, but MUST NOT be absolute where impersonation, registrar error, prior valid ownership, documented transfer, compromised registration, fraud, or conflicting jurisdictional evidence is proven. Overturning an existing registration requires strong evidence and a complete audit trail.


Temporary orders MAY maintain quarantine, allow only bounded existing sessions or recovery traffic, freeze transfers or delegations, preserve logs, or require additional evidence. Such orders MUST be scoped, signed, reason-coded, time-bounded, non-permanent, and appealable.


Final outcomes MAY select one branch, reject both, split authority by exact scope, restore prior state, require a new generation or recovery process, correct registry state, suspend or revoke a party, continue quarantine for insufficient evidence, or complete a verified transfer. The result MUST specify the exact post-state.


The final result MUST be published as a signed, machine-consumable ConflictResolutionRecord binding the conflict ID, evidence-set digest, affected scope, authoritative outcome, invalidated or preserved objects, required generation and sequence, revocations, recovery or migration requirements, effective time, appeal window, approving authorities, and transparency reference. An email or PDF alone is not wire authority.


Reconciliation MUST update registries, logs, mirrors, durable watermarks, caches, route authorizations and affected sessions while preserving the losing branch as historical evidence with revocation or tombstones. History MUST NOT be deleted. An appeal MUST NOT automatically restore authority, remove quarantine, cancel emergency enforcement, or permit parallel conflicting state.


The process MUST define acknowledgement, evidence, emergency-review, initial-decision and appeal deadlines, but missing a deadline MUST NOT create a default winner. If evidence remains insufficient, the affected scope remains quarantined or restricted.


Public records SHOULD expose the conflict class, affected public scope, status, decision, evidence commitments and reason category while protecting private contracts, personal documents, subscribers, sensitive infrastructure and secrets. Bilateral operational disputes such as quotas, route quality and maintenance SHOULD remain bilateral unless they affect public authority, global registry state, cross-operator security, protocol conformance or systemic abuse.


Abuse of the dispute process MUST be limited through evidence thresholds, signed reporters, rate limits, duplicate suppression and penalties for provably malicious claims, without discouraging good-faith security reporting.


Conformance MUST cover conflicting same-version digests, ownership overlap, valid transfer versus stale owner, compromised registry, split-view checkpoints, local versus operator-root conflict, unrelated-scope isolation, temporary orders, signed resolution, reconciliation, losing-branch resurrection, appeal, malicious dispute spam and incomplete evidence.


In simple terms: preserve evidence, quarantine the exact scope, apply deterministic protocol rules, use independent review only when necessary, publish a signed resolution, and reconcile state without erasing history.


A duhet me pasë appeals ose recovery process?
F116 — Separate Appeals and Cryptographic Recovery Processes: APPROVED
Appeals and recovery are both mandatory, but they are separate processes with different purposes, evidence and outcomes. An appeal reviews whether an administrative or governance decision was correct and procedurally fair. Recovery determines whether a cryptographic identity or authority lineage can be restored safely after compromise, key loss, corruption, rollback or quarantine. Appeal MUST NOT substitute for recovery, and recovery MUST NOT bypass a valid decision.


An appeal asks whether evidence was interpreted correctly, required process was followed, conflicts of interest existed, enforcement was proportional, or a registry/governance decision was erroneous. Recovery asks whether identity continuity remains provable, which keys and objects are compromised, which roots remain trustworthy, how a new generation is created, and how resurrection of old state is prevented.


Appeals MAY be initiated by the affected operator, affected service or ownership holder, affected registry operator, authorized representative, successor entity with valid evidence, directly affected peer, or a public-interest body where systemic trust is implicated. Anonymous reporting MAY trigger security investigation but MUST NOT create authority through an appeal without accountable evidence.


Registration rejection, scope restriction, suspension, quarantine, revocation classification, registry correction, conflict-resolution outcome, refusal of recovery, operator transfer decisions, accreditation removal and governance enforcement actions MUST be appealable. Emergency enforcement MAY remain active during appeal when continuing risk is credible.


An appeal MUST NOT automatically reactivate an operator, restore revoked keys, remove quarantine, cancel tombstones, permit new routes, activate static fallback, or select a losing conflicting branch. It reopens review, not trust.


The appeals body MUST be independent from the original decision maker, contested registry operator, initial conflict panel, materially interested sponsors/operators and maintainers with direct involvement. It MUST use conflict-of-interest declarations, recusals, quorum, threshold approval, written reasoning, evidence preservation and transparency commitments.


Valid grounds include new evidence, procedural error, incorrect protocol interpretation, incorrect identity mapping, conflict of interest, disproportionate enforcement, registry mistake, fraud or impersonation, newly verifiable evidence, or inconsistency with the charter or prior binding precedent. Mere disagreement is insufficient.


Appeal outcomes MAY uphold, narrow or remand the decision; alter a non-terminal classification; request further evidence; correct a registry error; permit or refuse recovery; or order a new review. Every result MUST define exact post-state, scope and effective time.


The appeal decision MUST be published as a signed, machine-consumable AppealDecisionRecord binding the appeal ID, original decision digest, evidence-set digest, appellant standing class, affected scope, grounds category, outcome, exact state transitions, preserved or invalidated objects, effective time, follow-up requirements, approving authorities, and transparency reference.


Recovery triggers include lost operational or recovery keys, root compromise, unauthorized publication, operator control-plane compromise, registry or transparency inconsistency, failed rotation, durable-state corruption or rollback, verified identity transfer, organizational restructuring and recovery after quarantine. Ordinary rotation SHOULD NOT use the high-assurance recovery flow without need.


Recovery authority MUST NOT depend on one key. It MUST combine applicable offline recovery keys, threshold approvals, registry verification, accountable identity proof, transparency evidence, witnesses and governance approval for operator-wide compromise. Keys inside the compromise set MUST NOT authorize their own recovery.


A recovery package MUST identify the Operator ID, incident class, compromised keys and objects, last trusted generation, requested new generation, new identity-root and recovery keys, purpose map, affected delegations/bundles/endpoints/services, required revocations, continuity or explicit lineage-break evidence, threshold approvals, registry and transparency evidence, re-entry restrictions and migration plan.


Continuity-preserving recovery is allowed only when the accountable entity remains the same, valid independent recovery authority survives and compromise scope can be bounded. In that case the Operator ID MAY be retained with a strictly higher generation. If identity control, roots and recovery paths cannot be proven safe, recovery is lineage-breaking: the prior lineage receives a terminal tombstone and a new Operator ID is required.


Successful recovery MUST establish a strictly higher generation, valid new sequence, previous trusted state or explicit break marker, new purpose-bound keys, new lifecycle and bundle state, updated transparency inclusion, durable watermark and revocation of the compromised lineage. An old generation MUST NEVER become active again.


Recovery MUST be staged through QUARANTINED, RECOVERY_PENDING, RECOVERY_VERIFIED, REENTRY_RESTRICTED and ACTIVE states. Re-entry MUST begin with narrow scope, reduced limits, no new broad delegation, active monitoring, peer synchronization, fresh checkpoints and staged restoration.


Registry acceptance alone does not complete recovery. Peers MUST verify the new registry record, key revocations, trust bundle, checkpoints, consistency or break evidence, endpoint bindings and authority dependencies. Routes based on stale operator generations MUST NOT continue unchanged.


Recovery refusal, lineage-break classification, excessive scope restriction, prolonged re-entry, registry failure and witness disagreement MAY be appealed, but an appeal MUST NOT reactivate compromised keys.


Recovery and appeals MUST have bounded acknowledgement, evidence, verification, review and escalation windows. If recovery cannot be completed, the operator remains quarantined, retired or terminally revoked according to evidence. Missing a deadline MUST NOT create default authority.


Public records SHOULD expose operator status, affected public scope, recovery stage, generations, revocations, decision digests and effective times while protecting private recovery shares, incident secrets, personal documents, subscriber data and unmitigated security details.


Static trust MUST NOT revive an Operator ID, bypass quarantine, accept old keys, replace registry recovery or create permanent authority. A pre-authorized static recovery profile MAY carry narrowly scoped restoration traffic only.


Conformance MUST test valid and invalid appeals, reviewer conflicts, emergency enforcement during appeal, overturned decisions, operational-key loss, root and recovery-key compromise, continuity-preserving and lineage-breaking recovery, staged re-entry, stale peers, old-key resurrection, timeouts, refusal plus appeal and static-bypass attempts.


In simple terms: an appeal decides whether the decision was fair and correct; recovery decides whether the identity can be saved without reviving the compromise.


A mundet një operator i vetëm me kontrollu root trust?
F117 — No Unilateral Control of Global Root Trust: APPROVED
No single operator, registry operator, foundation, sponsor, cloud vendor, ISP, maintainer or other actor may unilaterally add, remove, activate, replace or revoke global trust roots, change global trust thresholds, or authorize a global trust-bundle transition. A single actor may propose a change, but global authority requires independent threshold approval.


Root trust MUST be purpose-separated. Federation governance release roots, Operator ID registry roots, registrar roots, transparency-log roots, witness roots, emergency-response roots, private-consortium roots and individual operator identity roots MUST NOT collapse into one universal master key. Every root is bound to exact object classes, purposes, scope, validity and lifecycle.


The global trust state MUST be represented by a versioned FederationTrustBundle containing generation, sequence, previous-bundle digest, authorized roots, key purposes, lifecycle states, activation and retirement times, accepted registries, logs, witnesses, thresholds, emergency rules, transparency references and migration constraints.


Security-critical global trust transitions require M-of-N threshold authorization plus organizational diversity. Multiple keys controlled by the same organization do not count as independent authorities. The threshold profile MUST require approvals from distinct stakeholder or authority classes as defined by the governance profile.


Proposal, review, approval, signing, publication, transparency logging, witnessing and revocation duties MUST be separated. A compromise of a release pipeline or one organization MUST NOT be sufficient to create global trust authority.


Each operator controls its own identity-root lineage and may authorize its own purpose-bound operational keys, endpoints, delegations, rotations and recovery actions within its registered scope. An operator root does not become a global trust anchor and cannot self-register, alter Operator ID semantics or impose trust on other operators.


Registry operators may sign only records permitted by their accreditation. They cannot alter their own accreditation, modify the global root set, hide allocation history or create a private root that claims global authority.


Emergency authorities MAY temporarily disable or quarantine a compromised key, algorithm or capability and publish bounded deny updates. They MUST NOT independently add a permanent root, expand authority, transfer ownership or alter thresholds. Emergency actions require exact scope, expiry, audit and later ratification or automatic rollback.


Private enterprises and consortia MAY use a single-root private profile only when the trust domain, root controller, namespace and centralization risk are explicit and preconfigured. Such profiles MUST NOT claim global NBSR federation authority or collide with global Operator ID semantics.


Root rotation requires old-root or surviving-threshold authorization, new-root proof of possession, threshold approval, overlap and activation windows, transparency inclusion, witness confirmation, rollback protection and durable generation watermarks. A new root does not become authoritative merely because it appears in configuration or at an endpoint.


On compromise, the affected root is quarantined, dependent new authority is stopped, surviving threshold members are verified, emergency revocation is published, a replacement bundle is created, transparency and witnesses confirm the transition, peers synchronize, and the old root receives a terminal tombstone when required. Loss of sufficient threshold integrity is a global trust compromise and requires out-of-band recovery rather than ordinary rotation.


Implementations MUST apply trust minimization: a root is trusted only for its exact purpose, object class, registry/log/witness identity, scope, validity and lifecycle. Cross-purpose use is rejected. Local operators MAY impose stricter trust policy but cannot relabel local roots as global roots.


Global root authorities SHOULD have independent organizations, public membership, conflict disclosures, term limits, audited key custody, geographic and jurisdictional diversity, transparent succession and removal processes, and no permanent sponsor-controlled seat.


Conformance MUST test single-signature global bundles, valid thresholds, fake diversity from one organization, wrong-purpose roots, expired roots, root rotation, early activation, partial approval, compromised signers, emergency disable, unauthorized permanent root addition, private single-root profiles, rollback to old bundles and loss of threshold quorum.


In simple terms: an operator may control its own house, but no single actor may hold the master key for the entire federation.


Sa independent authorities ose witnesses kërkohen?
F118 — Risk-Based Authority and Witness Thresholds: APPROVED
Independent authorities and witnesses MUST use a risk-based threshold matrix rather than one universal number. Independence is organizational and operational, not merely cryptographic: authorities controlled by the same legal entity, parent company, beneficial owner, operational team, cloud account, HSM cluster, administrator group, or materially common command path count as one authority for diversity purposes.


For the Global Federation Trust Bundle, the initial normative minimum is five independent authorities with 3-of-5 approval. A valid 3-of-5 approval MUST span at least three distinct stakeholder classes, such as neutral foundation/governance, independent technical or security authority, registry ecosystem, operator community, and public-interest or privacy representation. Three signatures from one stakeholder class or one controlling organization MUST NOT satisfy the requirement.


High-risk changes, including global root-set replacement, threshold reduction, Operator ID semantic changes, global recovery after threshold compromise, removal of most roots, or permanent conversion of emergency action, require 4-of-5 approval, public review, transparency inclusion, witness confirmation, activation delay, migration evidence, and independent implementation evidence.


Ordinary global operations, including routine root rotation, registrar accreditation updates, witness-set updates, optional profiles, and bounded operational changes without authority expansion, may use 3-of-5 when versioning, review, and conformance requirements are satisfied.


Temporary emergency deny actions may use 2-of-5 emergency authorities only for bounded disable, quarantine, algorithm blocking, key suspension, or other deny-only mitigation. Emergency authority MUST NOT add trust, expand authority, create a permanent root, alter ownership, or reduce thresholds. Emergency actions MUST expire promptly or be ratified by the normal threshold.


Each global or high-impact transparency partition requires at least three independent witnesses with 2-of-3 matching statements. Global registry checkpoints, root-bundle transitions, and operator-wide recovery SHOULD use five witnesses with 3-of-5 confirmation. A checkpoint without the required witness threshold may remain PENDING but MUST NOT create new authority.


Witnesses confirm observed checkpoint state, consistency, and absence of conflicting views. Witnesses do not register operators, grant ownership, approve delegations, change trust bundles, or replace authority signatures.


Ordinary operator registration may be issued by one accredited registry operator only when accompanied by registry signature, transparency inclusion, at least 2-of-3 witness confirmation, audit trail, and an appeal path. Operator ID transfer, terminal revocation, recovery after root compromise, reversal of conflict resolution, and registrar compromise require multi-party approval involving registry authority, independent governance authority, and transparency or witness evidence.


Operator-level recovery uses the operator’s own threshold plus registry verification and transparency inclusion. Ordinary operational-key recovery may use the operator-defined threshold. Root compromise requires operator recovery threshold plus registry verification. Full lineage recovery requires at least three independent control paths. A default model is 2-of-3 operator recovery approvals plus registry verification and transparency inclusion, excluding compromised keys.


Private deployments MAY use 1-of-1 for low-risk labs, 2-of-3 for private production federation, or 3-of-5 for high-assurance consortiums, provided the profile explicitly states the threshold, independence assumptions, witness requirements, compromise model, and accepted risk. A private 1-of-1 profile MUST NOT claim global federation assurance.


Threshold reduction is itself a high-risk trust change and MUST require the current high-assurance threshold, public notice, reason, replacement plan, activation delay, conformance evidence, and witness confirmation. Emergency mode MUST NOT become permanent through timer resets or silent member removal.


When quorum is lost, no new global authority may be created. Existing verified state may continue only within approved last-known-good bounds. Revocations and temporary deny actions may use the bounded emergency path. Root additions, ownership changes, and recovery completion fail closed until governance quorum is restored.


Authorities and witnesses SHOULD be diversified across organizations, cloud providers, regions, DNS and control planes, HSM vendors, CI/CD systems, jurisdictions, and sponsors. These dependencies MUST be disclosed and auditable.


Adding or removing an authority or witness requires approval by the current set, proof of possession by the new member, independence verification, an overlap period, transparency inclusion, an updated bundle, rollback protection, retirement or revocation of the old member, and conformance vectors. Multiple members SHOULD NOT be replaced simultaneously without a staged transition.


Initial threshold matrix:
- Ordinary global trust update: 3-of-5 authorities and 2-of-3 witnesses.
- Global root or threshold high-risk change: 4-of-5 authorities and 3-of-5 witnesses.
- Temporary emergency deny: 2-of-5 emergency authorities with post-publication confirmation.
- Ordinary registry issuance: one accredited registrar with 2-of-3 witnesses.
- Operator-wide recovery: operator threshold plus registry/governance approval and at least 2-of-3 witnesses.
- Private production federation: 2-of-3 recommended, witness profile-defined.
- Private lab: 1-of-1 allowed, witnesses optional.


Conformance MUST test valid and invalid thresholds, organizational diversity, same-company signatures, high-risk operations with insufficient approval, emergency authority expansion attempts, conflicting witness statements, quorum loss, threshold reduction, member rotation, and common-mode infrastructure failure.


In simple terms: global federation starts with five authorities and 3-of-5 approval plus at least three witnesses and 2-of-3 confirmation, while changes to the foundations of trust require stronger thresholds and real organizational diversity.


Si parandalohet capture i governance nga sponsorët më të mëdhenj?
F119 — Anti-Capture Governance for Sponsors and Large Operators: APPROVED
Sponsorët mund ta financojnë NBSR-në, por nuk duhet të blejnë authority. Governance duhet të përdorë caps mbi voting power, disclosure të plotë, stakeholder diversity, term limits dhe separation të prerë mes funding-ut, technical decisions, registry control, transparency, witnesses dhe conformance.


1. One organization, one bounded influence
Voting power nuk rritet proporcionalisht me financimin, market share, numrin e customers, infrastructure të dhuruar, numrin e employees, affiliates ose keys. Një sponsor i madh mund të ketë vetëm influence të kufizuar sipas klasës së vet, jo vota të shumëfishuara nga madhësia ekonomike.


2. Stakeholder classes dhe class-based approval
Governance ndahet në klasa të pavarura: small/medium operators, large operators, independent implementers, security researchers, privacy/public-interest, registry/transparency ecosystem, service-owner/community dhe standards/academic contributors. Asnjë klasë nuk kontrollon shumicën vetëm. Security-critical vendimet kërkojnë supermajority totale dhe approval nga disa stakeholder classes.


3. Funding nuk krijon vote
Rregulli normativ është: financial contribution ≠ governance authority. Sponsor agreement nuk mund të japë permanent seat, veto, privileged root key, registry override, private roadmap control, secret review lane, reserved namespace, conformance exemption ose competitor blocking rights.


4. Contribution dhe affiliate caps
Duhet funding diversity, reserve fund, funding concentration report dhe plan për zëvendësim kur sponsor largohet. Subsidiaries, sister companies, funds dhe fronts me të njëjtin beneficial owner trajtohen si një grup për vote caps, seat caps, conflict-of-interest, funding disclosure dhe threshold diversity.


5. Term limits dhe independent seats
Seats kanë term të kufizuar, cooldown, rotation, no automatic renewal, attendance requirements dhe removal rules. Duhet vende të mbrojtura për security, privacy, public-interest, small operators, independent implementation dhe user/community representation.


6. Transparent finances dhe restricted funding
Foundation publikon sponsorët, contribution bands, restricted/unrestricted funding, contracts, infrastructure donations, staff secondments, travel sponsorship, grants, conflicts dhe concentration metrics. Sponsorët mund të financojnë implementations, audits, interoperability labs, documentation, grants dhe infrastructure, por technical acceptance varet vetëm nga specification, tests, security review, interoperability dhe governance approval.


7. No sponsor-controlled maintainer majority
Maintainer team nuk mund të ketë shumicë nga një kompani ose sponsor group. Kërkohen organization caps, independent maintainers, cross-organization review, merge restrictions për high-risk files, signed releases dhe transparent decision logs.


8. Registry, transparency dhe certification separation
Asnjë sponsor group nuk duhet të kontrollojë njëkohësisht foundation board, technical governance, registry operations, transparency log, witness majority dhe conformance certification. Beneficial-control overlap llogaritet edhe kur rolet duken të ndara në letër.


9. Procurement neutrality dhe portability
Contracts përdorin transparent criteria, competitive process, conflict declarations, review, term limits, portability requirements dhe exit clauses. Një cloud sponsor nuk duhet ta bëjë federation-in teknikisht të varur përgjithmonë nga cloud-i i vet. Open specification, open vectors, multiple implementations, reproducible builds, portable registry snapshots, independent mirrors dhe independent witnesses janë detyrim.


10. Conflict-of-interest dhe anti-retaliation
Members recuse kur vendimi prek kompaninë, customer/vendor, competitor direct, funded project, acquisition, registry contract, incident ku janë palë ose standard change që favorizon produktin e tyre. Security researchers, small operators dhe independent reviewers mbrohen nga retaliation për negative review, vulnerability disclosure, vote kundër sponsorit, conflict report ose governance complaint.


11. Public review dhe minority reports
Major changes kërkojnë public proposal, review window, recorded objections, response to objections, minority report, voting record dhe implementation evidence. Kundërshtimet serioze nuk fshihen nga history edhe kur shumica fiton.


12. Sponsor-exit resilience
Foundation duhet të mbijetojë kur sponsor kryesor largohet, ndalon infrastructure, tërheq staff, ndalon grants, kërkon fork privat ose tenton të marrë trademarks/assets. Kërkohen reserves, independent domains dhe keys, transferable infrastructure, escrow/backups, succession plan dhe neutral ownership të marks/specifications.


13. Trademark dhe certification neutrality
NBSR name, certification marks dhe conformance labels nuk kontrollohen nga sponsor i vetëm. Përdorimi i tyre varet nga published criteria, reproducible conformance, neutral enforcement dhe appeal process.


14. Capture indicators dhe corrective actions
Publikohen periodikisht metrics për funding, seats, affiliates, maintainers, infrastructure, witnesses, roots, repeated recusals, sponsor-favored exceptions, procurement concentration dhe participation të operatorëve të vegjël. Kur caps thyhen, mund të pezullohet vote, të bllokohet seat-i i ri nga e njëjta class, të re-tenderohet contract-i, të zgjerohet authority membership, të aktivizohet independent review ose të shtyhet high-risk change.


15. Fork safety
Specification dhe software duhet të mbeten forkable si safety valve. Fork-u përdor namespace/version të ri, nuk trashëgon global registry authority ose global root set automatikisht dhe duhet të publikojë governance/trust differences.


16. Conformance dhe governance audits
Duhet të auditohen skenarët: sponsor me shumë affiliates, majority funding, sponsor-controlled maintainer majority, registry/log/witness common ownership, permanent seat attempt, secret funding, conflict pa recusal, procurement favoritism, sponsor exit, trademark misuse dhe threshold diversity me të njëjtin beneficial owner.


Kushti kryesor: sponsorët duhet të jenë të dobishëm, jo sovranë. Paratë mund ta përshpejtojnë NBSR-në, por nuk duhet ta blejnë; authority duhet të vijë nga procesi, diversiteti dhe evidence-i, jo nga madhësia e çekut.
